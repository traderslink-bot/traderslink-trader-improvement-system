# Average Down Tracking for Trade Review

Average down tracking helps traders review when they added to a position after price moved against them. Trader Intelligence is being built to help active traders study average down behavior, adverse adds, and repeated adds into losing trades.

Averaging down is not automatically wrong in every strategy, but for many active traders it can become a repeated mistake when it is used emotionally or without a clear plan.

## What This Feature Reviews

Average down tracking focuses on adds that happen after a trade has moved against the trader.

It can help review:

- Adds below the original entry on long trades.
- Adds above the original entry on short trades.
- Adds after weakness appears.
- Adds without confirmation.
- Adds that increase risk after the trade idea changes.
- Whether the trader used size to avoid accepting a bad entry.
- Whether adverse adds repeat across multiple trades.

The goal is to help traders review whether adding improved the trade structure or made the risk harder to manage.

## Why It Matters For Trade Review

Averaging down can hide a decision problem.

A trader may add because the plan still supports the trade. But a trader may also add because they do not want to accept that the first entry was poor. The difference matters.

Without average down tracking, a journal may only show a better average price. It may not show that the trader increased size while the trade was weakening.

## How Trader Intelligence Uses This Information

Trader Intelligence is being built to use imported executions to help identify when adds happened relative to the original position and later trade behavior.

The system can support review of whether an add happened into strength, into weakness, after a failed level, after a pullback, or after the original trade idea became less valid.

This is not a rule that all adds are bad. It is a review process that helps traders study the context and frequency of adverse adds.

## Example Review Questions

Average down tracking can help traders ask:

- Did I add because the trade still matched my plan?
- Did I add only because I wanted a better average price?
- Did the add happen after the trade already started failing?
- Did the add increase risk beyond what I intended?
- Did I reduce risk after the add failed?
- Did this average down behavior repeat across several losing trades?
- Would the trade have been easier to manage without the add?

These questions help traders review whether adding was structured or emotional.

## Common Mistakes This Can Help Surface

Average down tracking can help surface patterns such as:

- Adding to a losing trade without a clear reason.
- Increasing size after the original idea failed.
- Using a better average price to avoid accepting a bad entry.
- Turning a small loss into a larger problem.
- Adding near support without waiting for confirmation.
- Adding multiple times into the same failed move.
- Failing to reduce risk after the add does not work.

The purpose is not to ban every add. The purpose is to make adverse adds visible during review.

## FAQ

### What is average down tracking?

Average down tracking is the process of reviewing when a trader adds to a position after the trade has moved against them. It helps show whether the trader repeatedly added into weakness.

### Is averaging down always a mistake?

No. Averaging down depends on the strategy, risk plan, position size, and context. The risk is that it can become an emotional habit when traders add to avoid taking a loss.

### Can Trader Intelligence track adverse adds?

Trader Intelligence is being built to review broker executions and position changes, which can support review of adverse adds and average down behavior when execution data is available.

### Why is averaging down risky for active traders?

Averaging down can increase position size while the trade is moving against the trader. That can make risk harder to control if the trade continues to fail.

### Is average down tracking financial advice?

No. Average down tracking is a review tool. It does not tell traders when to add, hold, buy, or sell.
