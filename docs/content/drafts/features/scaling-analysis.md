# Scaling Analysis for Trade Review

Scaling in and out of trading positions can make trade review more complicated. Trader Intelligence is being built to help active traders review adds, reductions, partial exits, re-adds, and position changes with more structure.

A trade is not always one entry and one exit. Many active traders build, trim, reduce, and re-enter positions. Scaling analysis helps review whether those decisions improved the trade or added unnecessary risk.

## What This Feature Reviews

Scaling analysis focuses on how the position changed after the first entry.

It can help review:

- Adds after entry.
- Re-adds after a reduction.
- Partial exits.
- Scale-outs.
- Size reductions.
- Position rebuilding.
- Average price changes.
- Whether size increased into strength or weakness.
- Whether reductions protected risk or cut winners too early.

The goal is to review the structure of the position, not only the final average entry and exit.

## Why It Matters For Trade Review

Scaling can help or hurt a trade depending on how it is used.

A trader may add when a trade is working and use reductions to manage risk. Another trader may add into weakness only because they want a better average. A trader may scale out with a clear plan or reduce too early because they are uncomfortable holding profit.

Without scaling analysis, these decisions can get hidden inside one final P&L number.

## How Trader Intelligence Uses This Information

Trader Intelligence is being built to use imported executions to organize adds, reductions, and exits inside a trade.

The system can help traders review whether scaling decisions aligned with the trade idea, increased risk, protected profit, or repeated a pattern. When market context is available, scaling review can also consider whether the trader added near support, added into resistance, reduced after rejection, or re-added after a reclaim.

This is review support. It is not a recommendation to scale in or out.

## Example Review Questions

Scaling analysis can help traders ask:

- Did I add because the trade improved or because the trade was going against me?
- Did my add make the position better or increase risk?
- Did I reduce size at a planned area or react emotionally?
- Did I scale out too early before the trade had room?
- Did I re-add after a valid continuation or after hesitation?
- Did scaling improve my trade management or make the trade harder to control?
- Is this scaling behavior repeated across trades?

These questions help traders review how position changes affected the process.

## Common Mistakes This Can Help Surface

Scaling review can help surface issues such as:

- Adding to weakness without a clear plan.
- Increasing size after the best entry has passed.
- Reducing winners too quickly.
- Re-adding after an emotional exit.
- Scaling out randomly instead of using a plan.
- Letting average price hide poor decision-making.
- Turning a controlled trade into a larger risk problem.

The point is not that scaling is good or bad by itself. The point is to review whether scaling decisions were controlled and repeatable.

## FAQ

### What is scaling in and out of trading?

Scaling in means adding to a position over time. Scaling out means reducing or exiting a position in parts instead of all at once.

### Why should traders review scaling decisions?

Scaling decisions can change risk, average price, and trade management. Reviewing them helps traders see whether adds and reductions helped the trade or created avoidable problems.

### Can Trader Intelligence review partial exits?

Trader Intelligence is being built around imported executions, which can support review of partial exits, reductions, adds, and re-adds when that data is available.

### Is scaling analysis financial advice?

No. Scaling analysis is a review process. It does not tell traders when to add, reduce, buy, or sell.

### Can scaling analysis help with averaging down?

Yes. Scaling analysis can help review whether a trader added into weakness, added without a plan, or repeatedly increased risk in losing trades.
