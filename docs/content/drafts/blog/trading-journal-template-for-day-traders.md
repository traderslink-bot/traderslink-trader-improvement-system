# Trading Journal Template for Day Traders

A trading journal template for day traders should help you review more than profit and loss. The best template tracks trade details, entries, exits, risk, time of day, trade management, mistakes, and lessons.

A template is a starting point. The real value comes from reviewing the same fields consistently so repeated patterns become easier to see.

## Direct Answer

A practical day trading journal template should include these fields:

- Date.
- Ticker.
- Trade direction.
- Entry price.
- Exit price.
- Position size.
- Entry time.
- Exit time.
- Trade idea.
- Risk plan.
- Stop area or invalidation area.
- Adds and reductions.
- Final P&L.
- Screenshot or chart notes.
- Mistake tags.
- Trade management notes.
- Lesson from the trade.

The goal is to make each trade easier to review later.

## Why This Matters

Day traders make decisions quickly. Without a template, reviews can become inconsistent. One trade gets detailed notes, another gets only P&L, and another gets skipped entirely.

A simple template creates structure. It helps you compare trades across sessions and find repeated behavior.

For example, you may notice that your best trades happen near the open, your weakest trades happen after a loss, or your biggest mistakes come from adding into weakness.

## Step-By-Step Template

### Basic trade fields

Start with the objective facts:

- Date.
- Ticker.
- Long or short.
- Entry price.
- Exit price.
- Share size.
- Time in trade.
- Realized P&L.

These fields create the basic trade record.

### Trade idea fields

Add fields that explain why the trade was taken:

- Setup type.
- Catalyst or reason.
- Key level.
- Support or resistance area.
- Volume context.
- Planned risk.

These fields help you review whether the trade had a clear reason.

### Execution fields

Track what you actually did:

- First entry.
- Adds.
- Reductions.
- Partial exits.
- Final exit.
- Re-entries.
- Average entry.
- Average exit.

For active traders, this section matters because one trade may include several decisions.

### Review fields

This is where the template becomes useful:

- Was the entry planned or reactive?
- Was the exit planned or emotional?
- Did you follow the risk plan?
- Did you protect profit?
- Did you average down?
- Did you chase?
- What would you repeat?
- What would you avoid next time?

These questions help turn raw data into trade review.

### Mistake tags

Use simple mistake tags so patterns are easier to count.

Examples:

- Late entry.
- Chased.
- Added to loser.
- Sold too early.
- Held too long.
- Gave back profit.
- Overtraded.
- No clear plan.
- Ignored level.
- Poor size.

Mistake tags should be specific enough to review later.

## Common Mistakes

Common template mistakes include:

- Tracking too many fields and then not using the template.
- Tracking only P&L.
- Not tracking adds and partial exits.
- Not tracking time of day.
- Writing vague notes.
- Reviewing only losing trades.
- Never summarizing weekly patterns.

A template should be simple enough to use and detailed enough to teach you something.

## FAQ

### What should be in a day trading journal template?

A day trading journal template should include trade details, execution data, trade idea, risk plan, entries, exits, adds, reductions, mistakes, notes, and lessons.

### Is a spreadsheet enough for a trading journal?

A spreadsheet can be enough to start, especially for basic tracking. Active traders may eventually need more structured review, execution imports, and mistake tracking.

### Should I track every trade?

Yes, tracking every trade helps avoid cherry-picking. Even small trades can reveal habits around timing, entries, exits, and risk.

### What is the most important field in a trading journal?

There is no single field that explains everything. The most useful fields usually include trade idea, entry reason, exit reason, risk plan, and mistake tags.

### Should I include screenshots?

Screenshots can help, especially when reviewing chart context. They are most useful when paired with notes about the entry, exit, and trade idea.
