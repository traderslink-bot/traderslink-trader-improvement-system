# How to Analyze Trade Entries and Exits

To analyze trade entries and exits, review the full decision process behind the trade. Look at where you entered, why you entered, what happened after entry, where you exited, why you exited, and whether your decisions matched the original trade idea.

A good entry and exit review does not only ask whether the trade made money. It asks whether the trade was executed well.

## Direct Answer

A practical entry and exit review should answer these questions:

- Was the entry planned or reactive?
- Was the entry early, late, or extended?
- Was the entry near support, resistance, or another important area?
- Did price confirm or reject after entry?
- Did the exit match the trade idea?
- Was the exit early, late, defensive, or structured?
- Did the trader protect profit or hold too long?
- Does this entry or exit behavior repeat across trades?

The goal is to understand the quality of the decision, not just the result.

## Why This Matters

Entries and exits shape the entire trade.

A late entry can make a trade harder to manage. A poor exit can turn a working trade into a frustrating one. A trader may have the right idea but still execute it poorly. Another trader may lose money but still make a disciplined exit when the setup fails.

That is why entry and exit analysis should be part of every serious trade review process.

## Step-By-Step Review Process

### 1. Start with the trade idea

Before judging the entry or exit, review the original reason for the trade.

Was the trade based on momentum, a breakout, a pullback, a reclaim, a support bounce, a news catalyst, or another setup? If the trade had no clear reason, that is the first review note.

### 2. Review the entry location

Look at where the entry happened on the chart.

Ask:

- Was the entry close to the planned area?
- Was the entry far from support?
- Was the entry directly under resistance?
- Was price already extended?
- Did volume support the move?

The entry location often affects the risk and emotional pressure of the trade.

### 3. Review the entry timing

Timing matters, especially for day traders.

A trade can be late even if the idea is correct. If the clean move already happened, the entry may leave less room for error. Review whether you entered before confirmation, after confirmation, or after the move was already crowded.

### 4. Review what happened after entry

Do not skip the middle of the trade.

Ask:

- Did price move in your favor quickly?
- Did it reject almost immediately?
- Did you add, reduce, or re-enter?
- Did the trade require more risk than planned?
- Did your management decisions change the trade quality?

This helps connect entry quality to trade management.

### 5. Review the exit reason

A good exit review should explain why the trade ended.

Was the exit based on the plan, a level, loss of momentum, risk control, fear, impatience, or a stop? The reason matters more than simply saying the exit was good or bad.

### 6. Review the exit timing

Ask whether the exit was too early, too late, or appropriate for the trade idea.

A trader can exit too early because they are afraid of giving back profit. A trader can exit too late because they do not want to accept that the trade failed. Both patterns are worth tracking.

## Common Mistakes

Common entry mistakes include:

- Chasing after the move is extended.
- Buying directly under resistance.
- Entering without a clear trade idea.
- Entering because of fear of missing out.
- Increasing size on a lower-quality setup.

Common exit mistakes include:

- Selling too early without a plan.
- Holding after the trade idea fails.
- Giving back open profit.
- Panic selling into weakness.
- Re-entering after an emotional exit.

The most important review question is whether these mistakes repeat.

## How A Trading Journal Helps

A trading journal helps organize entry and exit review over time.

Instead of reviewing each trade from memory, a journal can help track the entry, exit, execution time, trade idea, management decisions, and repeated patterns. If broker executions are imported, the review can become more accurate because the actual sequence of decisions is preserved.

## FAQ

### How do you analyze a trade entry?

Analyze a trade entry by reviewing the trade idea, entry location, timing, chart context, risk area, and what happened immediately after entry.

### How do you analyze a trade exit?

Analyze a trade exit by reviewing why the trade ended, whether the exit matched the plan, whether it protected risk or profit, and whether the trader exited too early or too late.

### What is a late entry in trading?

A late entry usually happens after the clean part of a move has already developed. Late entries can leave less room for error and may increase emotional pressure.

### Should I review entries and exits separately?

Yes. Entries and exits should be reviewed separately because each decision can reveal different strengths and weaknesses.

### Can a winning trade still have a bad entry or exit?

Yes. A winning trade can still include a poor entry, weak management, or an emotional exit. That is why process review matters.
