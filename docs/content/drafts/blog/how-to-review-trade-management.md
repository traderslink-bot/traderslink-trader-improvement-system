# How to Review Trade Management

To review trade management, study what happened after the entry. Look at how you added, reduced, held, exited, protected profit, and controlled risk while the trade was active.

Many traders review entries and exits but skip the middle of the trade. That middle section is often where the most important decisions happen.

## Direct Answer

A good trade management review should answer:

- Did I follow the original trade idea?
- Did I add for a valid reason or because I wanted a better average?
- Did I reduce risk when the trade changed?
- Did I protect open profit?
- Did I hold too long after the setup failed?
- Did I exit too early because I was uncomfortable?
- Did my management decisions make the trade easier or harder to control?

The goal is to review the quality of the decisions after entry.

## Why This Matters

A trade can start well and still be managed poorly.

A trader may enter near a good area, then add too late, ignore a failed level, refuse to reduce size, or give back open profit. Another trader may enter imperfectly but manage risk quickly and keep the loss controlled.

Trade management review helps traders understand what happened while the trade was alive.

## Step-By-Step Review Process

### 1. Review the original trade idea

Start by asking what the trade was supposed to be.

Was it a breakout, pullback, scalp, reversal, momentum trade, or small-cap news trade? The management should be reviewed against that original idea.

If the original idea was unclear, that is important to write down.

### 2. Review each add

Every add should have a reason.

Ask:

- Did I add because the trade improved?
- Did I add after confirmation?
- Did I add into weakness?
- Did I add because I wanted a better average?
- Did the add increase risk beyond my plan?

Adds can improve a trade when planned, but they can also turn a manageable loss into a bigger problem.

### 3. Review each reduction

Reductions are part of trade management too.

Ask:

- Did I reduce at a planned area?
- Did I reduce because risk increased?
- Did I reduce too early?
- Did I fail to reduce when the trade stopped working?
- Did I protect open profit?

A reduction can be disciplined or emotional depending on the context.

### 4. Review holding behavior

Ask whether you held for a reason.

Holding a trade is an active decision. Review whether the trade still matched the idea, whether price was still respecting important areas, and whether risk was still controlled.

### 5. Review the final exit

The final exit should be reviewed in relation to the full management process.

Did the exit happen because the idea failed? Did the trade hit a planned area? Did you exit after giving back profit? Did you exit late because earlier management decisions made the trade harder?

### 6. Look for repeated patterns

Trade management problems often repeat.

Examples include adding into weakness, scaling out too early, holding after failure, giving back profit, or re-entering after emotional exits.

## Common Mistakes

Common trade management mistakes include:

- Adding to losing trades without a clear plan.
- Refusing to reduce risk.
- Taking partial exits too randomly.
- Holding after the trade idea fails.
- Giving back open profit.
- Re-entering because of frustration.
- Managing the trade differently than planned.
- Letting emotions change the position size.

The best review is specific. Do not write "managed badly." Write what happened.

## How A Trading Journal Helps

A trading journal helps organize trade management decisions over time.

Instead of relying on memory, a journal can track adds, reductions, exits, notes, timing, and whether similar behaviors repeat across trades.

For active traders, execution data is especially useful because it shows the real sequence of position changes.

## FAQ

### What is trade management?

Trade management is how a trader handles a position after entry. It includes adding, reducing, holding, exiting, protecting profit, and controlling risk.

### Why is trade management important?

Trade management affects whether a trade stays controlled after entry. Good entries can be damaged by poor management, and imperfect entries can sometimes be managed with discipline.

### What should I review after a trade?

Review the trade idea, entry, adds, reductions, holding behavior, exit, risk control, and whether the same behavior repeats across trades.

### Is adding to a trade always bad?

No. Adding depends on the plan, context, size, and risk. The important question is whether the add was structured or emotional.

### How can I track trade management mistakes?

Use a trading journal to tag mistakes such as adverse adds, late exits, early exits, profit giveback, or holding after failure.
