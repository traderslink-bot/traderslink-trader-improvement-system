# Daily Trade Review Checklist

A daily trade review checklist helps traders review each session with structure instead of emotion. After trading, the goal is to understand what happened, how you traded, what mistakes repeated, and what should be carried into the next session.

The checklist should not only ask whether the day was green or red. It should ask whether the decisions were good.

## Direct Answer

Use this daily trade review checklist after each session:

- Did I follow my trading plan?
- What trades did I take?
- Why did I enter each trade?
- Were my entries planned or reactive?
- Did I manage risk properly?
- Did I add, reduce, or re-enter with a clear reason?
- Did I protect open profit?
- Did I exit according to the idea?
- What mistakes showed up?
- What did I do well?
- What is one thing to improve tomorrow?

A daily review should be specific, short enough to complete, and honest enough to be useful.

## Why This Matters

Trading sessions can move quickly. Without a checklist, traders often review based on emotion.

A green day can hide poor decisions. A red day can hide good discipline. A checklist helps slow the review down and forces the trader to look at process, not just results.

The best daily checklist helps identify repeated behavior over time.

## Step-By-Step Review Process

### 1. Record the session result

Start with the basic result:

- Total P&L.
- Number of trades.
- Biggest win.
- Biggest loss.
- Time traded.
- Main tickers traded.

This gives the session context, but it should not be the entire review.

### 2. Review each trade idea

For every trade, ask why it was taken.

Was there a real setup, catalyst, level, or reason? Or was the trade taken because price was moving and you reacted?

A trade with no clear reason deserves a note.

### 3. Review entries

For each entry, ask:

- Was the entry planned?
- Was it late?
- Was it near support or resistance?
- Was I chasing?
- Did the entry match the trade idea?

Entry review helps identify whether the trade started with structure.

### 4. Review exits

For each exit, ask:

- Did I exit because the idea failed?
- Did I exit too early?
- Did I exit too late?
- Did I protect profit?
- Did I hold after the trade changed?

Exit review helps identify whether the trade ended with a plan or emotion.

### 5. Review trade management

Look at adds, reductions, partial exits, and re-entries.

Ask:

- Did each add have a reason?
- Did each reduction protect risk or cut a winner too early?
- Did I re-enter with a plan?
- Did management decisions improve or damage the trade?

The middle of the trade often contains the best lessons.

### 6. Tag mistakes

Use simple mistake tags.

Examples:

- Chased.
- Late entry.
- Added to loser.
- Sold too early.
- Held too long.
- Overtraded.
- Gave back profit.
- No clear plan.
- Ignored level.
- Poor size.

Keep the tags consistent so they can be reviewed weekly.

### 7. Write one lesson

End the review with one clear lesson.

Examples:

- "I traded worse after my first loss."
- "I followed my plan on the first trade but overmanaged the second."
- "My best trade came from waiting near support."
- "I gave back open profit because I had no exit plan."

One clear lesson is better than a long emotional note.

## Common Mistakes

Common daily review mistakes include:

- Reviewing only P&L.
- Skipping winning trades.
- Writing vague notes.
- Not reviewing adds and reductions.
- Ignoring time of day.
- Not tagging repeated mistakes.
- Making the checklist too long to use consistently.

A checklist should be realistic. If it takes too long, many traders will stop using it.

## How A Trading Journal Helps

A trading journal helps store daily review data in one place.

Over time, the journal can show whether the same mistakes repeat, whether certain times of day are weaker, and whether trade management is improving.

For active traders, execution data can make daily review more accurate because it preserves the real sequence of entries, adds, reductions, and exits.

## FAQ

### What is a daily trade review checklist?

A daily trade review checklist is a set of questions traders use after each session to review trades, entries, exits, risk, mistakes, timing, and lessons.

### Should I review every trade daily?

Yes, at least briefly. Every trade can provide useful information, especially when similar mistakes repeat across multiple trades.

### How long should a daily trade review take?

It depends on the number of trades, but the checklist should be short enough to use consistently. A simple daily review is better than a perfect review that never gets done.

### Should I review green days?

Yes. Green days can still include weak decisions, poor risk management, or mistakes that should not be repeated.

### What is the most important part of daily trade review?

The most important part is identifying specific behavior. Instead of writing "bad day," write what actually happened and what should change.
