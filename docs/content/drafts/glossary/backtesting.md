# Backtesting

Backtesting means testing a trading idea, setup, or strategy using historical data. Traders use backtesting to see how rules may have performed in past market conditions before applying them in real time.

Backtesting does not guarantee future results. Historical performance can be misleading if the test uses unrealistic assumptions, ignores slippage, or overfits past data.

## Simple Definition

Backtesting is testing a strategy on past data.

Backtesting may review:

1. Setup rules.
2. Entry conditions.
3. Exit rules.
4. Stop placement.
5. Position sizing.
6. Risk-reward.
7. Win rate.
8. Drawdown.
9. Profit factor.
10. Trade frequency.

The goal is to learn whether the idea had historical support under defined rules.

## Why Backtesting Matters

Backtesting matters because it helps traders test ideas before risking money.

This is where traders can get into trouble. They may change rules after seeing the results until the backtest looks perfect, even if the strategy would not work in real time.

A useful review asks:

1. Were the rules clear before testing?
2. Was the data realistic?
3. Were slippage and fees considered?
4. Was the sample size large enough?
5. Did the strategy work across different conditions?
6. Was the test overfit to the past?

Backtesting should be treated as research, not proof.

## Example

A trader backtests a breakout setup over six months of chart data. They record each qualifying trade, entry, stop, target, and result.

If the setup only works after changing rules repeatedly, the test may be overfit and less useful.

## How To Review A Backtest

A good backtest review looks at assumptions and consistency.

Useful questions include:

1. Were entry rules objective?
2. Were exits realistic?
3. Did the test include losing periods?
4. Were fees and slippage included?
5. Did results depend on a few huge trades?
6. Should the idea be forward tested next?

The goal is to decide whether the idea deserves more testing.

## FAQ

### What is backtesting?

Backtesting is testing a trading idea or strategy on historical data.

### Does backtesting guarantee future performance?

No. Backtesting does not guarantee future performance or profitability.

### Why do traders backtest strategies?

They backtest to understand how rules may have performed historically before using them live.

### What can make a backtest misleading?

Overfitting, small sample size, bad data, ignored slippage, ignored fees, and unrealistic fills can make a backtest misleading.

### What should happen after backtesting?

A trader may forward test or paper trade the strategy to see how it behaves in current market conditions.

### How does Trader Intelligence relate to backtesting?

Trader Intelligence is being built to help traders review testing ideas, execution assumptions, risk behavior, and repeated decision patterns.
