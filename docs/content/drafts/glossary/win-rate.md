# Win Rate

Win rate is the percentage of trades that are winners. It helps traders understand how often their trades close green, but it does not show the full quality of a strategy by itself.

A high win rate does not guarantee profitability. A trader can win often but still lose money if the average loss is much larger than the average win.

## Simple Definition

Win rate measures the percentage of winning trades out of total trades.

For example:

1. Total trades: 100.
2. Winning trades: 60.
3. Win rate: 60%.

This means 60 out of 100 trades were winners.

## Why Win Rate Matters

Win rate matters because it gives a quick view of trade frequency and consistency.

This is where traders can get into trouble. They may focus only on winning often and ignore whether losses are too large.

A useful review asks:

1. What is the win rate?
2. What is the average win?
3. What is the average loss?
4. What is the profit factor?
5. Are winners large enough?
6. Are losing trades controlled?

Win rate is useful, but only with context.

## Example

A trader has a 70% win rate, but the average win is $40 and the average loss is $200. The high win rate may look good, but the trader may still struggle because the losses are too large.

Another trader may have a 40% win rate but larger winners than losers. That trader may still have a stronger performance profile.

## How To Review Win Rate

A good review looks at why the win rate is what it is.

Useful questions include:

1. Which setups have the highest win rate?
2. Which setups have the lowest win rate?
3. Does win rate change by time of day?
4. Are high win rate trades too small?
5. Are low win rate trades still profitable because winners are larger?
6. Is the sample size large enough?

The goal is not just to increase win rate. The goal is to understand how win rate interacts with risk and reward.

## FAQ

### What is win rate in trading?

Win rate is the percentage of trades that are profitable out of total trades.

### How do you calculate win rate?

Divide the number of winning trades by total trades, then multiply by 100.

### Is a high win rate always good?

Not always. A high win rate can still be unprofitable if losses are much larger than wins.

### What is a good win rate?

It depends on the strategy, average win, average loss, risk-reward, and trade management.

### Is win rate more important than profit factor?

No single metric is enough. Win rate and profit factor should be reviewed together.
