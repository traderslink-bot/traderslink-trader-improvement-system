# Fakeout

A fakeout happens when price appears to break an important level but then fails and reverses back through that level. Traders often use the word fakeout for false breakouts, false breakdowns, and failed level moves.

A fakeout can trap traders who enter too late or who do not wait for confirmation. It does not mean the trader should always avoid breakouts or breakdowns. It means the trader should review how the level behaved and how risk was managed.

## Simple Definition

A fakeout is a failed move beyond a key level.

A fakeout may happen when:

1. Price breaks above resistance but cannot hold.
2. Price breaks below support but quickly reclaims.
3. Volume fades after a level break.
4. A breakout turns into rejection.
5. A breakdown turns into reclaim.
6. Traders chase a move that reverses quickly.
7. The move lacks confirmation.

The key point is that the initial level break does not follow through.

## Why Fakeouts Matter

Fakeouts matter because they can reveal failed confirmation, trapped traders, and poor entry timing.

This is where traders can get into trouble. They may buy a breakout after the move is already extended, then hold when price falls back under the breakout level.

A useful review asks:

1. What level created the fakeout?
2. Was the move confirmed?
3. Did volume support the move?
4. Was the entry late?
5. Did the trader exit when the level failed?
6. Did the trader add after the fakeout?

Fakeout review helps traders understand whether they were trading structure or chasing movement.

## Example

A stock breaks above $2.00 and attracts buyers. A few minutes later, price falls back below $2.00 and cannot reclaim it.

That can be a fakeout above resistance. The trader can review whether the entry was too late and whether the exit followed the plan.

## How To Review A Fakeout

A good fakeout review looks at level behavior and trader behavior.

Useful questions include:

1. Was the level important?
2. Did price hold the level after breaking it?
3. Did the trader wait for confirmation?
4. Was the stop or invalidation level clear?
5. Did the trader respond quickly when the move failed?
6. Has this fakeout pattern repeated before?

The goal is to learn from the failed move without assuming every breakout or breakdown will fail.

## FAQ

### What is a fakeout in trading?

A fakeout is a move beyond a key level that fails and reverses back through that level.

### Is a fakeout the same as a false breakout?

A false breakout is one type of fakeout. A false breakdown can also be called a fakeout.

### Why do fakeouts happen?

Fakeouts can happen because of weak follow-through, low volume, trapped traders, liquidity, or failed confirmation.

### How can traders review fakeouts?

They can review level quality, confirmation, entry timing, volume, invalidation, and whether they exited when the move failed.

### Does a fakeout guarantee reversal?

No. A fakeout can lead to reversal, chop, or another attempt at the level.
