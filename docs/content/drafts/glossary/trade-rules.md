# Trade Rules

Trade rules are guidelines a trader follows before, during, and after trades. They help define what trades are allowed, how risk is managed, when to exit, and when to stop trading.

Trade rules do not guarantee profitability. They help make decisions more consistent and easier to review.

## Simple Definition

Trade rules are the instructions a trader uses to guide behavior.

Trade rules may include:

1. Setup rules.
2. Entry rules.
3. Exit rules.
4. Position sizing rules.
5. Stop rules.
6. Daily risk limits.
7. No-trade conditions.
8. Review rules.
9. Rules for halts or news.
10. Rules for stopping after mistakes.

The rules should be specific enough to follow in real time.

## Why Trade Rules Matter

Trade rules matter because trading decisions can become emotional without structure.

This is where traders can get into trouble. They may know what they should do but change the plan when a stock moves quickly.

A useful review asks:

1. What rule applied to the trade?
2. Was the rule followed?
3. Was the rule clear enough?
4. Did the trader break risk rules?
5. Did the rule prevent a bad trade?
6. Does the rule need improvement?

Trade rules turn behavior into something reviewable.

## Example

A trader has a rule to stop trading after hitting a daily max loss. If they continue trading after that point, the issue is not only the next trade result. The issue is the rule break.

Reviewing trade rules helps identify where discipline failed.

## How To Review Trade Rules

A good trade rules review compares written rules with actual behavior.

Useful questions include:

1. Were the rules written down?
2. Were they realistic?
3. Did the trader follow them?
4. Which rules were broken most often?
5. Did certain rules reduce losses?
6. Should any rules be simplified?

The goal is to create rules the trader can actually follow.

## FAQ

### What are trade rules?

Trade rules are guidelines a trader follows before, during, and after trades.

### Why do traders need rules?

Rules help reduce emotional decision-making and make trades easier to review.

### Are trade rules the same as trade criteria?

Not exactly. Criteria define what qualifies as a trade. Rules guide how the trader acts.

### What are examples of trade rules?

Examples include max daily loss, position size limits, no chasing, stop rules, and setup requirements.

### Can trade rules change?

Yes. Rules can be adjusted based on review, but they should not be changed emotionally during a trade.
