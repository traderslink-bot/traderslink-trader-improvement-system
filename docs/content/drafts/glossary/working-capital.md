# Working Capital

Working capital compares a company’s current assets with its current liabilities. Traders review working capital because it can help show short-term financial strength, liquidity, and whether a company may need more financing.

Working capital does not tell the full story by itself. It should be reviewed with cash, debt, cash burn, revenue, and filing details.

## Simple Definition

Working capital is usually calculated as current assets minus current liabilities.

Current assets may include:

1. Cash.
2. Accounts receivable.
3. Inventory.
4. Prepaid expenses.
5. Other short-term assets.

Current liabilities may include accounts payable, short-term debt, accrued expenses, and other obligations due within a year.

## Why Working Capital Matters

Working capital matters because it can show whether a company has enough short-term resources to cover short-term obligations.

This is where traders can get into trouble. They may focus on cash alone but ignore current liabilities or debt due soon.

A useful review asks:

1. Is working capital positive or negative?
2. What are current assets?
3. What are current liabilities?
4. Is cash enough to cover near-term obligations?
5. Is debt due soon?
6. Could financing be needed?

Working capital can help traders understand short-term financial risk.

## Example

A company has $10 million in current assets and $14 million in current liabilities. Its working capital is negative $4 million.

That does not automatically mean the company will fail, but it can indicate short-term financial pressure.

## How To Review Working Capital

A good working capital review looks at the balance sheet and trend over time.

Useful questions include:

1. Did working capital improve or worsen?
2. Is cash a large part of current assets?
3. Are receivables collectible?
4. Is debt coming due?
5. Are accounts payable increasing?
6. Does management discuss liquidity risk?

The goal is to understand whether short-term financial condition affects the trade thesis.

## FAQ

### What is working capital?

Working capital is current assets minus current liabilities.

### What does negative working capital mean?

Negative working capital means current liabilities are greater than current assets.

### Why do traders review working capital?

They review it to understand short-term liquidity, financial pressure, and financing risk.

### Is positive working capital always good?

Positive working capital can be helpful, but traders still need to review cash quality, debt, revenue, and cash burn.

### Where can traders find working capital information?

Working capital information comes from the balance sheet in quarterly and annual reports.
