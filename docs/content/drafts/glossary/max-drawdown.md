# Max Drawdown

Max drawdown, or maximum drawdown, is the largest decline from a prior account or performance high over a selected period. Traders use max drawdown to understand the worst peak-to-trough decline in their trading results.

Max drawdown does not predict future losses. It is a historical risk metric that helps traders review how much damage occurred during a difficult period.

## Simple Definition

Max drawdown is the largest drawdown in a performance sample.

For example:

1. Account reaches $10,000.
2. Account drops to $8,500 before recovering.
3. Max drawdown for that period is $1,500, or 15%.

If a later decline is larger, the max drawdown number changes.

## Why Max Drawdown Matters

Max drawdown matters because it shows the deepest decline a trader experienced during the period being reviewed.

This is where traders can get into trouble. A strategy may look profitable overall but have drawdowns that are too large for the trader to handle emotionally or financially.

A useful review asks:

1. What was the max drawdown?
2. What trades caused it?
3. Was it from one large loss or many losses?
4. Did the trader follow risk limits?
5. Did position sizing increase the damage?
6. Did the trader change behavior during the drawdown?

The size of the drawdown matters, but the cause matters too.

## Example

A trader makes $3,000 over two months, but at one point they were down $2,500 from their account high. The final profit looks positive, but the max drawdown shows the trader went through a large decline to get there.

That can reveal risk that P&L alone does not show.

## How To Review Max Drawdown

A good max drawdown review looks at both numbers and behavior.

Useful questions include:

1. When did max drawdown occur?
2. What setup or behavior caused it?
3. Was the loss within planned limits?
4. Were stops respected?
5. Did the trader overtrade during the decline?
6. Was recovery based on good trades or risky behavior?

The goal is to understand whether the worst decline was controlled or avoidable.

## FAQ

### What is max drawdown?

Max drawdown is the largest decline from a prior account or performance high during a selected period.

### How is max drawdown different from drawdown?

Drawdown can refer to any decline from a high. Max drawdown is the largest decline in the sample.

### Why do traders track max drawdown?

They track it to understand worst-case historical declines, risk behavior, and performance stability.

### Is lower max drawdown always better?

Lower drawdown can be easier to manage, but it still needs context such as strategy type, returns, risk, and sample size.

### Does max drawdown predict future losses?

No. It is based on past performance and does not guarantee future risk levels.
