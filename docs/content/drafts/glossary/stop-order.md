# Stop Order

A stop order is an order that becomes active when price reaches a specific trigger level. Traders often use stop orders to help manage risk, define exits, or trigger entries after a certain price level is reached.

A stop order does not guarantee a perfect fill. Fast markets, gaps, low liquidity, and volatility can cause slippage. That is why stop orders should be reviewed as part of a broader trade plan.

## Simple Definition

A stop order is an order that triggers after price reaches a selected stop price.

Common stop-related orders include:

1. Stop market orders.
2. Stop limit orders.
3. Stop loss orders.
4. Buy stop orders.
5. Sell stop orders.
6. Trailing stop orders.

For trade review, the important question is whether the stop was planned, realistic, and followed.

## Why Stop Orders Matter

Stop orders matter because they can help traders define risk before entering a trade.

This is where traders can get into trouble. A trader may say they have a stop, but then cancel it, move it emotionally, or ignore the reason the stop was placed.

A useful review asks:

1. Was the stop level chosen before entry?
2. Was the stop based on chart structure?
3. Was the stop too tight for volatility?
4. Was the stop moved emotionally?
5. Did slippage affect the exit?
6. Did the trader respect the original risk plan?

A stop order is only useful if it fits the trade plan.

## Example

A trader enters a stock at $2.00 and places a stop market order at $1.90. If price trades down to the stop price, the order triggers and attempts to exit at the market.

The planned risk is about $0.10 per share before slippage, fees, and execution differences.

If the stock gaps quickly through $1.90, the actual fill may be lower.

## How To Review Stop Orders

A good review looks at both the stop plan and the actual execution.

Useful questions include:

1. Was the stop level logical?
2. Did the trader size the position based on the stop?
3. Did the trader leave the stop alone?
4. Did the stop trigger as expected?
5. Was there slippage?
6. Did the stop prevent a larger mistake?

The goal is not to avoid every losing trade. The goal is to keep risk planned and reviewable.

## FAQ

### What is a stop order?

A stop order is an order that triggers when price reaches a specified stop price.

### Is a stop order the same as a stop loss?

A stop loss is a common use of a stop order. Stop orders can also be used for entries or other trade plans.

### Does a stop order guarantee the stop price?

No. A stop market order does not guarantee the stop price. It triggers a market order and can fill with slippage.

### What is a stop limit order?

A stop limit order triggers at the stop price but only fills at the limit price or better. It may not fill if price moves too fast.

### Why should traders review stop behavior?

Stop behavior shows whether the trader respected risk, moved stops emotionally, or sized the trade properly.
