# Trade Expectancy

Trade expectancy is a performance concept that estimates the average expected result per trade over a group of trades. It combines win rate, average win, and average loss to help traders understand whether their trade results have a positive or negative profile.

Trade expectancy does not predict the next trade. It is based on historical results and should be reviewed with sample size, market conditions, execution quality, and risk management.

## Simple Definition

Trade expectancy estimates the average result a trader might expect per trade based on past performance.

A simple expectancy idea uses:

1. Win rate.
2. Average win.
3. Loss rate.
4. Average loss.

The concept helps traders review whether wins are large enough and frequent enough compared with losses.

## Why Trade Expectancy Matters

Trade expectancy matters because win rate alone can be misleading.

This is where traders can get into trouble. A trader may win often but have losses so large that the overall expectancy is weak. Another trader may win less often but have larger winners and controlled losses.

A useful review asks:

1. What is the win rate?
2. What is the average win?
3. What is the average loss?
4. Is the sample size large enough?
5. Does expectancy change by setup?
6. Are results distorted by one large trade?

Expectancy helps connect multiple metrics into one review.

## Example

A trader wins 50% of the time. The average win is $200 and the average loss is $100.

A simple expectancy review would suggest the trader has a positive profile because average winners are larger than average losers at the same win rate.

If the average loss were $300 instead, the same win rate would tell a very different story.

## How To Review Trade Expectancy

A good review looks at both the number and the behavior that created it.

Useful questions include:

1. Which setups have the best expectancy?
2. Which setups have negative expectancy?
3. Are winners being cut too early?
4. Are losses being allowed to grow too large?
5. Does expectancy change by market condition?
6. Are there enough trades to trust the sample?

The goal is to understand where a trader’s process has a stronger or weaker profile.

## FAQ

### What is trade expectancy?

Trade expectancy estimates the average expected result per trade based on win rate, average win, and average loss.

### Does expectancy predict the next trade?

No. It is based on historical performance and does not predict the next trade.

### Why is expectancy useful?

It helps traders review whether the combination of win rate, average win, and average loss creates a positive or negative profile.

### Can a high win rate have negative expectancy?

Yes. If losses are much larger than wins, a high win rate can still produce poor expectancy.

### Should expectancy be reviewed by setup?

Yes. Different setups can have different expectancy profiles.
