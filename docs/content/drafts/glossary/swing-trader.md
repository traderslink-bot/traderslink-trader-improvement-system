# Swing Trader

A swing trader usually holds trades for more than one trading session to capture a larger move. Swing traders may hold for days or weeks depending on the setup, risk plan, catalyst, and market conditions.

Swing trading does not guarantee larger profits. Holding longer can create overnight risk, gap risk, news risk, and changing market conditions.

## Simple Definition

A swing trader holds trades longer than an intraday trader.

Swing traders may focus on:

1. Daily charts.
2. Multi-day setups.
3. Support and resistance.
4. Catalysts.
5. Trend structure.
6. Earnings or filing risk.
7. Position sizing.
8. Stop planning.
9. Trade journaling.
10. Performance review.

The goal is to capture a planned move while managing risk across time.

## Why Swing Trader Review Matters

Swing trader review matters because longer holding periods add different risks.

This is where swing traders can get into trouble. They may hold through invalidation, ignore overnight risk, or turn a planned swing into a hope-based hold.

A useful review asks:

1. What was the swing thesis?
2. What invalidated the trade?
3. Was position size appropriate for overnight risk?
4. Did the trader follow the stop?
5. Did the catalyst remain valid?
6. Did the trader hold too long?

Swing trading review should focus on thesis, risk, and trade management.

## Example

A swing trader enters a stock near support with a catalyst expected over the next few days. The trader defines a stop below support and a target near resistance.

If support breaks and the trader keeps holding, the review should flag the broken thesis.

## How Swing Traders Can Review Performance

A good swing trading review looks at multi-day decision quality.

Useful questions include:

1. Did the setup stay valid?
2. Did overnight risk fit the position size?
3. Did the trader react to new filings or news?
4. Did the trader protect profit?
5. Was the exit planned or emotional?
6. Which swing setups performed best?

The goal is to learn which multi-day trades are worth repeating.

## FAQ

### What is a swing trader?

A swing trader usually holds trades for more than one trading session to capture a planned move.

### How long do swing traders hold trades?

Swing traders may hold for days or weeks depending on the setup and plan.

### Is swing trading less risky than day trading?

Not automatically. Swing trading has different risks, including overnight gaps and news events.

### What should swing traders review?

They can review thesis, catalyst, levels, position size, overnight risk, trade management, and exits.

### Can a swing trade become a bad hold?

Yes. If the original thesis fails and the trader keeps holding, the trade may become hope-based.
