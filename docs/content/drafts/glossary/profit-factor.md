# Profit Factor

Profit factor is a trading performance metric that compares gross profit with gross loss. It helps traders see how much profit was made for every dollar lost over a group of trades.

Profit factor does not guarantee future results. It is a review metric, not a prediction. It should be used with win rate, average win, average loss, drawdown, sample size, and trade quality.

## Simple Definition

Profit factor is calculated by dividing gross profit by gross loss.

For example:

1. Gross profit from winning trades: $2,000.
2. Gross loss from losing trades: $1,000.
3. Profit factor: 2.0.

A profit factor of 2.0 means the strategy produced $2 in gross profit for every $1 in gross loss over that sample.

## Why Profit Factor Matters

Profit factor matters because it combines winners and losers into one performance metric.

This is where traders can get into trouble. They may focus only on win rate without noticing that losses are much larger than wins.

A useful review asks:

1. What is the profit factor?
2. How many trades are in the sample?
3. What is the average win?
4. What is the average loss?
5. Is one large winner distorting the number?
6. Is one large loss damaging the number?
7. Does the metric hold across different setups?

Profit factor is useful, but only when reviewed with context.

## Example

A trader has $5,000 in gross profits and $4,000 in gross losses. The profit factor is 1.25.

That means the trader made $1.25 for every $1 lost over that sample. This may be positive, but the trader still needs to review drawdowns, consistency, fees, and execution quality.

## How To Review Profit Factor

A good review looks at what created the number.

Useful questions include:

1. Is the sample size large enough?
2. Which setups have the strongest profit factor?
3. Which setups are dragging it down?
4. Are losses too large?
5. Are winners too small?
6. Does profit factor change by time of day?
7. Did fees or slippage affect the metric?

The goal is to understand the behavior behind the metric.

## FAQ

### What is profit factor in trading?

Profit factor is gross profit divided by gross loss over a group of trades.

### Is a higher profit factor better?

Generally, a higher profit factor can indicate stronger historical performance, but it still needs context such as sample size, drawdown, fees, and consistency.

### Does profit factor predict future results?

No. Profit factor is based on past trades and does not guarantee future performance.

### Can one trade distort profit factor?

Yes. One very large win or loss can distort profit factor, especially in a small sample.

### Is profit factor better than win rate?

It is different. Win rate shows how often trades win. Profit factor compares total winning dollars with total losing dollars.
