# Gross Margin

Gross margin shows how much revenue remains after subtracting the cost of revenue. Traders review gross margin because it can help explain revenue quality, business efficiency, and whether sales are likely to support profitability.

Gross margin does not guarantee profitability. A company can have strong gross margin and still lose money if operating expenses are high.

## Simple Definition

Gross margin compares gross profit with revenue.

A simple version is:

1. Revenue minus cost of revenue equals gross profit.
2. Gross profit divided by revenue equals gross margin.

For example, if a company has $10 million in revenue and $4 million in cost of revenue, gross profit is $6 million and gross margin is 60%.

## Why Gross Margin Matters

Gross margin matters because not all revenue has the same quality.

This is where traders can get into trouble. They may see revenue growth but miss that margins are weak or declining.

A useful review asks:

1. What is gross margin?
2. Is gross margin improving or weakening?
3. What is cost of revenue?
4. Are expenses too high after gross profit?
5. Is revenue growth high quality?
6. Does margin support the company thesis?

Gross margin should be reviewed with revenue and operating expenses.

## Example

A company reports higher revenue, but gross margin drops sharply. That may mean sales increased, but costs also rose.

A trader should review whether the revenue growth is profitable or whether the company is spending too much to generate sales.

## How To Review Gross Margin

A good gross margin review looks at trend and business model.

Useful questions include:

1. Is gross margin positive?
2. Is margin improving over time?
3. Did cost of revenue rise faster than revenue?
4. Is the margin normal for the industry?
5. Did management explain margin changes?
6. Can the company cover operating expenses after gross profit?

The goal is to understand whether sales quality supports the broader trade thesis.

## FAQ

### What is gross margin?

Gross margin shows how much revenue remains after subtracting cost of revenue.

### Is gross margin the same as profit?

No. Gross margin does not include operating expenses, interest, taxes, or other items.

### Why do traders review gross margin?

They review it to understand revenue quality, cost structure, and whether sales may support profitability.

### Can revenue grow while gross margin falls?

Yes. Revenue can increase while costs rise faster, causing margin to fall.

### Is high gross margin always good?

High gross margin can be positive, but traders still need to review operating expenses, cash burn, and valuation.
