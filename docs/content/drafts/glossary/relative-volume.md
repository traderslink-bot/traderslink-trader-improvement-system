# Relative Volume

Relative volume, often shortened to RVOL, compares current trading volume with the stock’s normal or average volume. Traders use relative volume to understand whether a stock is trading with unusual activity.

High relative volume does not guarantee a good trade or continued price movement. It only shows that volume is higher than normal for the comparison period.

## Simple Definition

Relative volume measures current volume compared with average volume.

For example:

1. A stock normally trades 500,000 shares by this time of day.
2. Today it has traded 2,000,000 shares.
3. Relative volume is about 4x normal activity.

The exact calculation can vary by platform and timeframe.

## Why Relative Volume Matters

Relative volume matters because it can help traders find unusual attention, liquidity, and catalyst reaction.

This is where traders can get into trouble. They may see high RVOL and assume the stock must continue higher, even if price is rejecting or the move is already extended.

A useful review asks:

1. What was the relative volume?
2. What caused the unusual volume?
3. Did price confirm the volume?
4. Did the volume create better liquidity?
5. Did the trader chase because of RVOL?
6. Did the move hold after the volume spike?

Relative volume should be reviewed with price action and context.

## Example

A stock has 10x relative volume after news. That means it is trading far more volume than usual. If price is also breaking above key levels and holding, the RVOL may support the trade thesis.

If price spikes and fades, the high RVOL may show attention but not a clean trade.

## How To Review Relative Volume

A good relative volume review looks at context and execution.

Useful questions include:

1. Was RVOL high compared with normal trading?
2. Was there a catalyst?
3. Did volume continue or fade?
4. Did price hold key levels?
5. Did liquidity improve or become unstable?
6. Did the trader follow the plan?

The goal is to understand whether RVOL helped the trade setup or only created excitement.

## FAQ

### What is relative volume?

Relative volume compares current trading volume with normal or average volume.

### What does RVOL mean?

RVOL means relative volume.

### Is high relative volume bullish?

Not automatically. High relative volume can appear during strong moves, selloffs, reversals, fakeouts, or news reactions.

### Why do traders watch relative volume?

They watch it to find unusual activity, catalyst reaction, liquidity, and attention.

### Can relative volume be misleading?

Yes. High RVOL does not guarantee direction or follow-through.
