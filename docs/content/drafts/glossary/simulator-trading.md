# Simulator Trading

Simulator trading means practicing trades in a simulated market environment. Traders use simulators to practice setups, order entry, risk management, execution routines, and trade review without using real money.

Simulator trading does not perfectly match live trading. Real money emotions, live liquidity, slippage, order routing, and market pressure can still be different.

## Simple Definition

Simulator trading is trading practice in a simulated environment.

A trading simulator may help practice:

1. Entries.
2. Exits.
3. Order types.
4. Risk management.
5. Position sizing.
6. Chart reading.
7. Trade journaling.
8. Setup recognition.
9. Alert response.
10. Trade review.

The goal is to practice process before adding real capital risk.

## Why Simulator Trading Matters

Simulator trading matters because it lets traders practice without immediate financial loss.

This is where traders can get into trouble. They may treat the simulator like a game and take unrealistic trades that would not be acceptable live.

A useful review asks:

1. Were trades realistic?
2. Were rules followed?
3. Were fills believable?
4. Was risk defined?
5. Did the trader overtrade because money was not real?
6. Were simulator trades journaled?

Simulator trading should be reviewed seriously if it is meant to build skill.

## Example

A trader uses a simulator to practice entries around support and resistance. They log each simulated trade with entry, stop, target, setup, and mistake notes.

That process can help identify habits before trading live.

## How To Review Simulator Trading

A good simulator review looks at realism and discipline.

Useful questions include:

1. Did the trader follow the same rules as live trading?
2. Were entries realistic?
3. Did the trader account for spread and slippage?
4. Did the trader chase or overtrade?
5. Did the simulator help build confidence or bad habits?
6. What should be improved before live trading?

The goal is to make simulated practice useful and honest.

## FAQ

### What is simulator trading?

Simulator trading is practicing trades in a simulated market environment without using real money.

### Is simulator trading the same as paper trading?

They are closely related. Paper trading is simulated trading, and a simulator is one tool used to practice it.

### Is simulator trading useful?

It can be useful for practicing order entry, setups, risk management, and review habits.

### Does simulator trading match live trading?

Not perfectly. Live trading can involve different emotions, fills, liquidity, slippage, and pressure.

### Can simulator trading create bad habits?

Yes. Unrealistic size, poor rules, and careless trades can create bad habits.
