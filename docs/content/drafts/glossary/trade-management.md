# Trade Management

Trade management means how a trader handles a trade after entering it. It includes risk control, stop behavior, partial exits, adds, reductions, profit protection, and whether the trader follows the plan as the trade develops.

A trade is not finished when the entry happens. Many trading mistakes happen after entry, during the management phase.

## Simple Definition

Trade management is the process of managing a trade from entry to exit.

It can include:

1. Following the stop plan.
2. Adjusting risk when conditions change.
3. Taking partial exits.
4. Adding to a position.
5. Reducing exposure.
6. Protecting profit.
7. Exiting when the thesis fails.
8. Avoiding emotional decisions.

The goal is to manage the trade according to a plan, not according to panic or hope.

## Why Trade Management Matters

Trade management matters because a good entry can still become a bad trade if it is managed poorly.

This is where traders can get into trouble. They may enter well, then hold too long, add after the setup fails, or give back profit because there was no exit plan.

A useful review asks:

1. Did the trader follow the original plan?
2. Did the stop stay respected?
3. Were partial exits planned?
4. Did adds improve or worsen risk?
5. Did the trader protect open profit?
6. Did the trader exit when the thesis failed?

The management phase often explains more than the entry alone.

## Example

A trader buys a stock near support with a clear stop. The trade moves in their favor, but they never reduce risk or protect profit. Later, the stock reverses and the trader exits near breakeven or red.

The entry may have been fine. The management issue may have been profit protection or exit discipline.

## How To Review Trade Management

A good review looks at the full sequence of decisions.

Useful review questions include:

1. What was the plan before entry?
2. What changed during the trade?
3. Did the trader respond to new information?
4. Were adds planned or emotional?
5. Were exits based on structure or fear?
6. What should be repeated or changed next time?

The goal is to learn how the trade was handled, not just whether it made money.

## FAQ

### What is trade management?

Trade management is how a trader manages a position after entry, including risk, adds, reductions, exits, and rule-following.

### Why is trade management important?

It matters because many trading outcomes are shaped by what happens after entry.

### Is trade management the same as risk management?

They are related, but not identical. Risk management focuses on controlling loss and exposure. Trade management includes the full handling of the trade.

### What are common trade management mistakes?

Common mistakes include ignoring stops, adding emotionally, cutting winners too early, holding losers too long, and failing to protect profit.

### How can traders review trade management?

They can review the trade plan, each decision after entry, and whether the exit matched the thesis.
