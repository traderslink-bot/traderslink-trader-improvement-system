# Scalper

A scalper is a trader who takes quick trades, often aiming to capture small price movements. Scalpers usually focus on speed, liquidity, spread, order execution, and strict risk control.

Scalping does not guarantee easier profits. Small moves can be affected heavily by fees, spread, slippage, poor fills, and fast reversals.

## Simple Definition

A scalper is a trader who enters and exits quickly.

Scalpers may focus on:

1. Tight spreads.
2. High liquidity.
3. Fast fills.
4. Small price moves.
5. Short holding periods.
6. Level 2 or tape reading.
7. Strict stops.
8. Quick profit taking.
9. Execution review.
10. Fee awareness.

The strategy depends heavily on execution quality.

## Why Scalper Review Matters

Scalper review matters because small execution mistakes can erase small expected gains.

This is where scalpers can get into trouble. They may enter too late, pay a wide spread, or hold a quick trade too long after momentum fades.

A useful review asks:

1. Was the spread tight enough?
2. Was liquidity strong enough?
3. Was the fill price acceptable?
4. Was the exit fast enough?
5. Did fees or slippage matter?
6. Did the trader overtrade?

Scalping review should focus on execution and discipline.

## Example

A scalper enters a liquid stock near a key level and exits within minutes after a small move. If the spread was wide or the exit was delayed, the trade may not have matched the scalping plan.

That makes execution review important.

## How Scalpers Can Review Performance

A good scalping review looks at many small decisions.

Useful questions include:

1. Which setups produced clean fills?
2. Did the trader chase entries?
3. Did spread affect results?
4. Did the trader respect quick invalidation?
5. Did trade frequency become overtrading?
6. Did profits justify fees and slippage?

The goal is to understand whether the scalping process is repeatable.

## FAQ

### What is a scalper in trading?

A scalper is a trader who takes quick trades, often aiming to capture small price movements.

### Is scalping the same as day trading?

Scalping is a type of day trading, but not all day traders are scalpers.

### What do scalpers need to review?

Scalpers often review spread, liquidity, fill quality, slippage, fees, timing, and discipline.

### Why is execution important for scalpers?

Because small moves can be heavily affected by poor fills, wide spreads, and slippage.

### Can scalping lead to overtrading?

Yes. Frequent short-term trading can become overtrading if trades stop matching the plan.

### How does Trader Intelligence relate to scalpers?

Trader Intelligence is being built to help scalpers review execution, spread, liquidity, risk, and repeated decision patterns.
