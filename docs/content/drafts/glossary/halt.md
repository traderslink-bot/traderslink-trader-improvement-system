# Halt

A halt is a temporary pause in trading for a stock or market. Halts can happen because of volatility, pending news, regulatory concerns, order imbalance, or broader market rules.

A halt does not guarantee a stock will reopen higher or lower. The reaction after trading resumes depends on the reason for the halt, news, order imbalance, liquidity, and trader positioning.

## Simple Definition

A halt means trading has paused temporarily.

A halt may happen because of:

1. Extreme volatility.
2. Pending news.
3. Order imbalance.
4. Regulatory review.
5. Market-wide circuit breaker rules.
6. Exchange-specific rules.
7. Corporate announcements.
8. Unusual trading activity.

The halt reason matters during trade review.

## Why Halts Matter

Halts matter because they can trap open positions and create uncertainty until trading resumes.

This is where traders can get into trouble. They may enter a fast-moving stock without considering halt risk, then lose control over the exit while trading is paused.

A useful review asks:

1. What type of halt occurred?
2. Was the stock already extended?
3. Was there news pending?
4. How did price reopen?
5. Was liquidity thin after resumption?
6. Did the trader have a risk plan?

Halt review is part of execution and risk review.

## Example

A stock runs quickly after news and triggers a volatility halt. Trading pauses for a short period, then resumes. After resumption, the stock may continue higher, reject, gap down, or become very volatile.

The halt itself does not predict the next move.

## How To Review A Halt

A good halt review looks at reason, timing, and resumption behavior.

Useful questions include:

1. What caused the halt?
2. Was it expected from volatility?
3. Did the trader enter too late before the halt?
4. What happened at resumption?
5. Did spread and liquidity change?
6. Did the trader manage risk after trading resumed?

The goal is to understand whether halt risk was handled properly.

## FAQ

### What is a halt in trading?

A halt is a temporary pause in trading for a stock or market.

### Why do stocks halt?

Stocks may halt because of volatility, pending news, regulatory review, order imbalance, or market-wide rules.

### Can traders buy or sell during a halt?

Trading is paused during a halt, so regular buying and selling cannot happen until trading resumes.

### Does a halt mean a stock will go up?

No. A halt does not guarantee direction after trading resumes.

### Why are halts risky?

Halts can prevent immediate exits and create uncertainty around the reopening price.
