# Trading Journal

A trading journal is a record of trades, decisions, notes, and performance data. Traders use journals to review what happened, why they entered, how they managed the trade, and what patterns repeat over time.

A trading journal does not guarantee better trading. It is only useful if the trader records honest information and reviews it consistently.

## Simple Definition

A trading journal is a place to track and review trades.

A trading journal may include:

1. Ticker.
2. Entry and exit price.
3. Position size.
4. Setup.
5. Catalyst.
6. Screenshots.
7. Trade notes.
8. Mistakes.
9. Risk plan.
10. Performance metrics.

The goal is to make trading decisions reviewable.

## Why Trading Journals Matter

Trading journals matter because they help traders spot repeated behavior.

This is where traders can get into trouble. They may remember only the big wins or losses and miss the patterns that happen across many trades.

A useful review asks:

1. What setup was traded?
2. Was the entry planned?
3. Was risk respected?
4. Was the exit disciplined?
5. What mistake repeated?
6. What should be changed next time?

A journal turns individual trades into useful feedback.

## Example

A trader logs every trade for a month and notices that most losses come from chasing scanner alerts after the move is extended. That pattern may not be obvious without a journal.

Once the pattern is visible, the trader can build rules to reduce the mistake.

## How To Review A Trading Journal

A good journal review looks for repeated patterns instead of one-off opinions.

Useful questions include:

1. Which setups performed best?
2. Which mistakes repeated?
3. Did losses come from poor ideas or poor execution?
4. Did the trader follow stops?
5. Did partial exits help or hurt?
6. What rule should be improved?

The goal is to convert trade history into clearer self-review.

## FAQ

### What is a trading journal?

A trading journal is a record of trades, decisions, notes, screenshots, and performance data.

### Why should traders use a journal?

A journal helps traders review decisions, execution, performance, and repeated behavior patterns.

### Is a trading journal only for beginners?

No. Traders at many experience levels can use journals to improve review and accountability.

### What should be included in a trading journal?

A journal can include entry, exit, ticker, setup, risk, notes, screenshots, results, and lessons.

### Does journaling guarantee improvement?

No. Journaling helps only if the trader reviews the data honestly and applies lessons.
