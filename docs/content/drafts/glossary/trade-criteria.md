# Trade Criteria

Trade criteria are the conditions a trade must meet before a trader considers entering. They help define what qualifies as a valid setup and what should be avoided.

Trade criteria do not guarantee a trade will work. They help make trade selection more structured, consistent, and reviewable.

## Simple Definition

Trade criteria are the requirements for taking a trade.

Trade criteria may include:

1. Setup type.
2. Catalyst.
3. Volume.
4. Relative volume.
5. Key levels.
6. Entry area.
7. Stop or invalidation.
8. Risk-reward.
9. Liquidity.
10. Market context.

The criteria should be specific enough to judge after the trade.

## Why Trade Criteria Matter

Trade criteria matter because they help traders avoid random entries.

This is where traders can get into trouble. They may enter because a stock is moving, but the trade does not meet the setup requirements.

A useful review asks:

1. What criteria were required?
2. Did the trade meet them?
3. Which criteria were missing?
4. Did the trader force the trade?
5. Was risk clearly defined?
6. Did the criteria improve selectivity?

Trade criteria make decisions easier to review.

## Example

A trader’s criteria for a breakout may require a catalyst, strong volume, a clean resistance level, and a stop near the breakout area. If a stock is moving without volume or a clear level, it may not meet the criteria.

That can help the trader wait instead of chase.

## How To Review Trade Criteria

A good trade criteria review looks at whether the rules matched actual trade behavior.

Useful questions include:

1. Were the criteria written before the trade?
2. Did the trader follow them?
3. Did losing trades skip key criteria?
4. Did winning trades share common criteria?
5. Are the criteria too vague?
6. Should any criteria be added or removed?

The goal is to improve trade selection over time.

## FAQ

### What are trade criteria?

Trade criteria are the conditions a trade must meet before a trader considers entering.

### Why do trade criteria matter?

They help traders avoid random or emotional trades and make setups easier to review.

### Are trade criteria the same as trade rules?

They are related. Criteria define what qualifies as a trade. Rules define how the trader acts before, during, and after the trade.

### What should trade criteria include?

Criteria may include setup type, catalyst, volume, key levels, entry area, stop, risk-reward, liquidity, and market context.

### Can trade criteria be too strict?

Yes. Criteria can be too strict or too loose, which is why traders should review results over time.
