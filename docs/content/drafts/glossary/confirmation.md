# Confirmation

Confirmation means evidence that supports a trade setup, trade thesis, or planned entry. Traders may look for confirmation before entering, adding, holding, or exiting a trade.

Confirmation does not guarantee that a trade will work. It only means the trader has more evidence that the setup may be behaving as expected.

## Simple Definition

Confirmation is supporting evidence for a trade idea.

Confirmation may include:

1. Price holding a key level.
2. Volume increasing.
3. A breakout holding above resistance.
4. A pullback finding support.
5. A reclaim of an important level.
6. A news reaction continuing.
7. A candle closing in favor of the setup.
8. Market context supporting the trade.

The type of confirmation depends on the strategy and setup.

## Why Confirmation Matters

Confirmation matters because it can help traders avoid entering too early or guessing before the setup is ready.

This is where traders can get into trouble. They may say they waited for confirmation, but the trade review shows they entered before the actual setup confirmed.

A useful review asks:

1. What confirmation was required?
2. Did confirmation happen before entry?
3. Did the trader enter too early?
4. Did the trader wait too long and chase?
5. Did confirmation fail after entry?
6. Did the trader follow the plan after confirmation changed?

Confirmation should be defined clearly enough to review.

## Example

A trader wants to buy a breakout only if price clears resistance and holds above it with volume. The break above resistance is one part of the setup. Holding above the level with volume may be the confirmation.

If the trader enters before the level breaks, the trade may not have had confirmation yet.

## How To Review Confirmation

A good confirmation review looks at the planned evidence and actual timing.

Useful questions include:

1. What did the trader need to see?
2. Did the setup confirm?
3. Was the entry before or after confirmation?
4. Was the confirmation strong or weak?
5. Did confirmation disappear after entry?
6. Did the trader ignore failed confirmation?

The goal is to understand whether the trade was based on evidence or anticipation.

## FAQ

### What is confirmation in trading?

Confirmation is evidence that supports a setup or trade thesis before or during a trade.

### Does confirmation guarantee a trade will work?

No. Confirmation does not guarantee continuation, profitability, or reduced risk.

### Why do traders wait for confirmation?

They may wait for confirmation to avoid entering before the setup has enough evidence.

### Can waiting for confirmation cause late entries?

Yes. Waiting too long can sometimes lead to chasing if the trader enters after the move is already extended.

### What counts as confirmation?

Confirmation depends on the strategy. It may include price action, volume, level holds, candle closes, or catalyst reaction.
