# Partial Exit

A partial exit happens when a trader closes part of a position while keeping the rest open. Traders often use partial exits to lock in some profit, reduce risk, or manage a trade in stages.

A partial exit is not automatically good or bad. It depends on the trade plan, setup, risk, and whether the partial helped or hurt the overall management of the trade.

## Simple Definition

A partial exit means exiting only part of a position.

For example:

1. A trader holds 1,000 shares.
2. The trader sells 500 shares.
3. The trader still holds 500 shares.
4. That first sale is a partial exit.

The remaining position can still gain, lose, or be managed further.

## Why Partial Exits Matter

Partial exits matter because they affect profit protection, risk, and average exit price.

This is where traders can get into trouble. They may take partial profits too early, sell too much too soon, or fail to manage the remaining position.

A useful review asks:

1. Was the partial exit planned?
2. Did it reduce risk?
3. Did it protect profit?
4. Did the trader sell too much too early?
5. Did the trader manage the remaining shares well?
6. Did the partial exit improve the average exit?

A partial exit should be reviewed as part of the full trade sequence.

## Example

A trader enters at $2.00 and sells half at $2.30. The stock later reaches $2.60, but the trader has already reduced the position.

That partial exit may have protected profit, but the trader should review whether it matched the plan or came from fear.

## How To Review Partial Exits

A good partial exit review looks at both risk and opportunity.

Useful questions include:

1. Why was the partial taken?
2. Was it based on a level, target, or emotion?
3. How much of the position was reduced?
4. Did it improve risk management?
5. Did it reduce the upside too much?
6. Was the rest of the position managed well?

The goal is to understand whether the partial exit supported the trade plan.

## FAQ

### What is a partial exit?

A partial exit is when a trader closes part of a position while keeping the rest open.

### Why do traders take partial exits?

They may take partial exits to lock in some profit, reduce risk, or manage the trade in stages.

### Is taking partial profits always good?

Not always. It can protect profit, but it can also reduce upside if done too early or without a plan.

### How does a partial exit affect average exit?

Each partial exit contributes to the weighted average exit price of the full trade.

### Should partial exits be planned before entry?

They can be more useful when planned ahead, but traders may also adjust based on changing conditions.
