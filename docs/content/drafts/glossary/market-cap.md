# Market Cap

Market cap, short for market capitalization, is the value of a company based on its current share price and shares outstanding. Traders use market cap to compare company size, valuation, and share structure context.

Market cap does not tell the full story by itself. It should be reviewed with float, fully diluted shares, cash, dilution risk, liquidity, and company fundamentals.

## Simple Definition

Market cap is usually calculated as:

1. Share price.
2. Multiplied by shares outstanding.
3. Equals market cap.

For example, if a stock trades at $2.00 and has 50 million shares outstanding, the market cap is about $100 million.

## Why Market Cap Matters

Market cap matters because it helps traders understand company size and valuation context.

This is where traders can get into trouble. They may focus only on share price and assume a low-priced stock is cheap, without checking how many shares exist.

A useful review asks:

1. What is the current market cap?
2. What are shares outstanding?
3. What is the float?
4. What is the fully diluted market cap?
5. Has dilution changed the share count?
6. Is the market cap reasonable for the catalyst?

Market cap gives context that share price alone does not provide.

## Example

A $1.00 stock with 200 million shares outstanding has a market cap of about $200 million. A $5.00 stock with 10 million shares outstanding has a market cap of about $50 million.

The lower-priced stock is not automatically cheaper.

## How To Review Market Cap

A good market cap review looks at share count and context.

Useful questions include:

1. Is the share count current?
2. Are there warrants or convertibles?
3. Is fully diluted market cap much higher?
4. How does market cap compare with cash and revenue?
5. Did the stock already price in the catalyst?
6. Is liquidity strong enough for the trade?

The goal is to avoid judging valuation only by stock price.

## FAQ

### What is market cap?

Market cap is the company’s market value based on share price multiplied by shares outstanding.

### Is market cap the same as stock price?

No. Stock price is one share’s price. Market cap includes both share price and share count.

### Why do traders review market cap?

They review it to understand company size, valuation, dilution context, and comparison with other stocks.

### Can market cap change quickly?

Yes. Market cap changes when stock price changes or when shares outstanding changes.

### What is fully diluted market cap?

Fully diluted market cap estimates valuation using potential shares from warrants, convertibles, options, or other dilutive securities.
