# Stock Split

A stock split increases the number of shares outstanding while reducing the share price proportionally. It is often called a forward split to distinguish it from a reverse split.

A stock split does not create business value by itself. It changes the share count and price math, but traders still need to review market reaction, liquidity, valuation, and the actual trade setup.

## Simple Definition

A stock split divides existing shares into more shares.

For example:

1. A 2-for-1 stock split turns 1 share into 2 shares.
2. A $100 stock may become a $50 stock after adjustment.
3. The total position value is intended to stay similar before market reaction.

The number of shares increases, while the per-share price adjusts lower.

## Why Stock Splits Matter

Stock splits matter because they can change how a stock trades, how charts appear, and how traders interpret historical prices.

This is where traders can get into trouble. They may compare pre-split and post-split prices without using split-adjusted data.

A useful review asks:

1. What was the split ratio?
2. When did the split take effect?
3. Are charts split-adjusted?
4. Did liquidity change after the split?
5. Did the market react positively or negatively?
6. Did the trade setup remain valid after adjustment?

Stock split review should focus on adjusted context, not just the headline.

## Example

A stock trades at $100 and completes a 2-for-1 split. After the split, the adjusted price may be around $50, and each shareholder owns twice as many shares.

The company is not automatically worth more because of the split.

## How To Review A Stock Split

A good stock split review looks at split ratio, price adjustment, and post-split behavior.

Useful questions include:

1. Was the split already expected?
2. Did volume increase after the split?
3. Did the stock hold important adjusted levels?
4. Did liquidity improve or worsen?
5. Did the trader use split-adjusted charts?
6. Did the trader chase the split headline?

The goal is to understand whether the split changed trade behavior or only changed share math.

## FAQ

### What is a stock split?

A stock split increases the number of shares and lowers the per-share price proportionally.

### Does a stock split make a company more valuable?

No. A stock split changes share count and price math, but it does not create business value by itself.

### What is a forward stock split?

A forward stock split is the common type of stock split where shares increase and the adjusted price decreases.

### How is a stock split different from a reverse split?

A stock split increases share count and lowers price. A reverse split reduces share count and raises price.

### Why should traders use split-adjusted charts?

Split-adjusted charts help avoid misleading comparisons between pre-split and post-split prices.
