# Float

Float is the number of shares that are generally available for public trading. Traders often review float because it can affect liquidity, volatility, and how quickly a stock may move when volume increases.

Float does not guarantee a stock will move. A low float can increase volatility, but the actual trade still depends on volume, catalyst, liquidity, spread, and execution.

## Simple Definition

Float is the portion of shares that can trade publicly.

Float is usually different from shares outstanding because shares outstanding may include:

1. Insider-held shares.
2. Restricted shares.
3. Closely held shares.
4. Locked-up shares.
5. Shares not actively available for public trading.

Float is meant to estimate tradable supply.

## Why Float Matters

Float matters because lower tradable supply can sometimes create faster price movement when demand increases.

This is where traders can get into trouble. They may assume low float alone makes a stock a good trade, even if there is no catalyst or the spread is too wide.

A useful review asks:

1. What was the float?
2. Was the float source current?
3. How did volume compare with float?
4. Was liquidity good enough?
5. Was there dilution or resale risk?
6. Did the trader size properly for volatility?

Float is one part of share structure, not the entire trade thesis.

## Example

A stock has 5 million shares in the public float. If volume suddenly rises to 20 million shares after news, traders may view the stock as highly active relative to its tradable supply.

That can create volatility, but it does not guarantee direction or continuation.

## How To Review Float

A good float review looks at context and data quality.

Useful questions include:

1. Was the float data recent?
2. Did the stock have fresh filings or offerings?
3. Was volume unusually high relative to float?
4. Was the spread manageable?
5. Did low float increase risk?
6. Did the trader chase because of float alone?

The goal is to understand whether float helped explain the trade environment.

## FAQ

### What is float in stocks?

Float is the number of shares generally available for public trading.

### Is float the same as shares outstanding?

No. Shares outstanding is the total issued share count. Float usually refers to shares available for public trading.

### Why do traders care about float?

They care because float can affect liquidity, volatility, and how a stock reacts to volume.

### Is low float always good for trading?

No. Low float can increase volatility and risk. It does not guarantee a good trade.

### Can float change?

Yes. Float can change after offerings, resale registrations, insider sales, lockup changes, or share issuance.
