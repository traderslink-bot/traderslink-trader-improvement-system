# ATM Offering

An ATM offering, short for at-the-market offering, lets a company sell shares into the market over time, usually through a sales agent. Instead of pricing one large block at once, the company can sell shares gradually under the program.

An ATM offering can create dilution, but the timing and amount sold may depend on price, volume, company needs, and the terms of the sales agreement.

## Simple Definition

An ATM offering is a program that allows a company to sell shares into the open market.

An ATM program may involve:

1. A shelf registration.
2. An equity distribution agreement.
3. A sales agent.
4. Periodic share sales.
5. Prospectus supplement filings.
6. Updates in quarterly or annual reports.
7. Dilution from shares sold.
8. Market-price sales rather than one fixed offering price.

The company may not sell the full amount immediately.

## Why ATM Offerings Matter

ATM offerings matter because they can create ongoing supply while the stock is trading.

This is where traders can get into trouble. They may see a stock running on volume but miss that the company has an active ATM program that could be used into strength.

A useful review asks:

1. Is there an active ATM program?
2. How much can the company sell?
3. How much has already been sold?
4. Is the shelf effective?
5. Does the company need cash?
6. Did the stock trade heavy into volume?

ATM risk should be reviewed with filings and trading behavior.

## Example

A company files a prospectus supplement for a $50 million ATM offering. It may sell shares over time as market conditions allow.

If the stock later spikes on news, traders may review whether the ATM could be used during the increased volume.

## How To Review An ATM Offering

A good ATM review looks at filing terms and share sale updates.

Useful questions include:

1. What is the maximum ATM amount?
2. Which sales agent is listed?
3. How much capacity remains?
4. Did the company disclose recent ATM sales?
5. Is volume high enough to absorb selling?
6. Did price action show unusual selling pressure?

The goal is to understand whether ATM supply could affect the trade thesis.

## FAQ

### What is an ATM offering?

An ATM offering lets a company sell shares into the market over time through a sales agent.

### Does an ATM offering happen all at once?

Not usually. Shares can be sold gradually depending on market conditions and company decisions.

### Is an ATM offering dilutive?

Yes, shares sold through an ATM can increase shares outstanding and create dilution.

### Why do traders care about ATM offerings?

They care because an active ATM can create ongoing share supply during price strength or high volume.

### Where can traders find ATM details?

ATM details are often found in prospectus supplements, shelf filings, equity distribution agreements, and periodic reports.
