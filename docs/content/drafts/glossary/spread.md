# Spread

Spread is the difference between the bid price and the ask price. Traders review spread because it affects entries, exits, fills, slippage, and the true cost of trading a stock.

A tight spread can make execution easier. A wide spread can make a trade harder to enter and exit cleanly, especially in low-liquidity or fast-moving stocks.

## Simple Definition

Spread is the gap between the highest price buyers are bidding and the lowest price sellers are asking.

For example:

1. Bid price: $2.00.
2. Ask price: $2.05.
3. Spread: $0.05.

The wider the spread, the more careful traders usually need to be with entries, exits, and position sizing.

## Why Spread Matters

Spread matters because it can affect the real cost of the trade.

This is where traders can get into trouble. They may think they are entering at a clean level, but the spread is so wide that the fill changes the risk-reward immediately.

A useful review asks:

1. What was the spread at entry?
2. What was the spread at exit?
3. Was the stock liquid enough?
4. Did the spread cause slippage?
5. Was position size too large for the spread?
6. Did the trader use the right order type?

Spread is part of execution quality.

## Example

A trader buys a stock with a bid of $2.00 and an ask of $2.10. If the trader buys at the ask and immediately needs to sell at the bid, the spread creates an instant disadvantage.

That does not mean the trade cannot work, but the trader needs to account for the spread in the plan.

## How To Review Spread

A good spread review looks at how the spread affected trade execution.

Useful questions include:

1. Was the spread normal for the stock?
2. Did the spread widen during volatility?
3. Did the trader chase the ask?
4. Did the trader exit into a weak bid?
5. Did spread affect stop placement?
6. Did the trade require smaller size?

The goal is to understand whether spread created hidden execution risk.

## FAQ

### What is spread in trading?

Spread is the difference between the bid price and the ask price.

### What is bid-ask spread?

Bid-ask spread is another name for the gap between the highest bid and lowest ask.

### Why does spread matter?

Spread affects entries, exits, slippage, fills, and the real cost of the trade.

### Is a wide spread risky?

It can be. A wide spread can make it harder to enter or exit at expected prices.

### What causes spreads to widen?

Low liquidity, volatility, news, thin order books, and fast price movement can widen spreads.
