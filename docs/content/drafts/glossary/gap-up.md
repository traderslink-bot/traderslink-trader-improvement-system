# Gap Up

A gap up happens when a stock opens higher than its previous closing price. Traders often watch gap-up stocks because they can show fresh attention, news reaction, momentum, or strong overnight demand.

A gap up does not guarantee continuation. A stock can gap up and keep running, fade after the open, reject a level, or trade sideways.

## Simple Definition

A gap up means the stock opens above the prior closing price.

A gap up can happen because of:

1. News.
2. Earnings.
3. SEC filings.
4. Analyst updates.
5. Market strength.
6. Sector momentum.
7. Premarket buying.
8. Low-float volatility.

The reason for the gap matters during trade review.

## Why Gap Ups Matter

Gap ups matter because they can create opportunity and risk at the same time.

This is where traders can get into trouble. They may chase a stock after it already moved far premarket without reviewing the catalyst, volume, or key levels.

A useful review asks:

1. Why did the stock gap up?
2. Was the catalyst strong?
3. How much did it move premarket?
4. Did it hold premarket levels?
5. Did volume support the move?
6. Did the trader chase after the open?

A gap up should be reviewed with context, not treated as a signal by itself.

## Example

A stock closes at $2.00 and opens the next day at $2.50 after news. That is a gap up.

If the stock holds above premarket support and volume expands, the move may have follow-through. If it loses key levels and fades, the gap up may fail.

## How To Review A Gap Up

A good gap-up review looks at the catalyst, levels, and execution.

Useful questions include:

1. What caused the gap?
2. Was the move already extended?
3. What was the premarket high?
4. What was the premarket low?
5. Did the stock hold the open?
6. Did the trader have a clear plan?

The goal is to understand whether the trader traded structure or just reacted to a big move.

## FAQ

### What is a gap up?

A gap up happens when a stock opens above its previous closing price.

### Does a gap up mean the stock will keep going?

No. A gap up can continue, fade, reject, or trade sideways.

### Why do stocks gap up?

Stocks can gap up because of news, earnings, filings, sector strength, analyst updates, or premarket buying.

### What should traders review on a gap up?

Traders often review catalyst quality, premarket high, premarket low, volume, liquidity, and entry timing.

### Is chasing a gap up risky?

It can be risky if the stock is already extended or the trader has no clear risk plan.
