# Scale In

Scale in means building a position in stages instead of entering the full size all at once. Traders may scale in to manage timing, confirm a setup, or reduce the risk of taking full size at the wrong moment.

Scaling in is not automatically good or bad. It depends on whether the adds are planned, whether risk stays controlled, and whether the setup remains valid.

## Simple Definition

To scale in means to add to a trade over multiple entries.

For example:

1. A trader buys 250 shares first.
2. The setup confirms.
3. The trader adds another 250 shares.
4. The final position is 500 shares.

The average entry changes as the trader adds.

## Why Scaling In Matters

Scaling in matters because each add changes risk, average entry, and trade management.

This is where traders can get into trouble. They may call it scaling in, but they are really averaging into a weak trade emotionally.

A useful review asks:

1. Was the scale-in planned?
2. Did the add happen after confirmation?
3. Did risk stay controlled?
4. Did the average entry improve or worsen the trade?
5. Did the trader add after the setup failed?
6. Was position size still appropriate?

Scaling in should support the plan, not hide emotional decision-making.

## Example

A trader plans to enter half size near support and add the rest only if the stock reclaims a key level. If the reclaim happens and the trader adds, the scale-in follows a plan.

If the stock breaks support and the trader adds only because they want a lower average, that may be a different behavior.

## How To Review Scale-Ins

A good scale-in review looks at timing, setup quality, and risk.

Useful questions include:

1. What was the first entry?
2. Why was size added?
3. Did the add follow a rule?
4. How did the add change average entry?
5. Did the add increase risk beyond the plan?
6. Did the trader exit properly if the setup failed?

The goal is to separate planned scaling from emotional adding.

## FAQ

### What does scale in mean?

Scale in means adding to a position in stages instead of entering full size all at once.

### Is scaling in the same as averaging down?

Not always. Scaling in can be planned and structured. Averaging down usually means adding after price moves against the position.

### Why do traders scale in?

They may scale in to manage timing, wait for confirmation, or build a position gradually.

### What is the risk of scaling in?

The risk is adding too much, adding emotionally, or increasing exposure after the setup weakens.

### Should scale-ins be planned before entry?

They are usually easier to review when the add criteria are planned before the trade.
