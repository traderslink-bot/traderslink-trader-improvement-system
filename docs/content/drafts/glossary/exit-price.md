# Exit Price

Exit price is the price where a trader closes or reduces a position. It matters because the exit determines realized profit or loss and shows how the trader managed the trade after entry.

A trade can have a good entry and still be managed poorly if the exit is emotional, late, early, or disconnected from the plan.

## Simple Definition

Exit price is the price where a position is closed or reduced.

For example:

1. A trader buys a stock at $2.00.
2. The trader sells at $2.25.
3. The exit price is $2.25.

If the trader exits in multiple pieces, the average exit may be different from any single fill.

## Why Exit Price Matters

Exit price matters because it shows how a trader handled profit, loss, risk, and changing conditions.

This is where traders can get into trouble. They may hold after the trade thesis fails, exit winners too early, or sell only because of fear.

A useful review asks:

1. Was the exit planned?
2. Did the exit match the original thesis?
3. Did the trader exit too early?
4. Did the trader hold too long?
5. Did the trader protect profit?
6. Did the exit follow the stop or target plan?

Exit quality is a major part of trade management.

## Example

A trader enters at $2.00 and plans to exit if price loses $1.90. Price breaks $1.90, but the trader waits and exits at $1.75.

The exit price shows that the planned risk was not followed. That is an execution and trade management issue to review.

## How To Review Exit Price

A good exit review looks at plan, timing, and behavior.

Useful questions include:

1. Where was the planned exit?
2. Where was the actual exit?
3. Did the exit protect profit or increase loss?
4. Was the exit based on structure or emotion?
5. Did the trader exit in pieces or all at once?
6. Has this exit behavior repeated before?

The goal is to understand whether the exit supported the trade plan.

## FAQ

### What is exit price?

Exit price is the price where a trader closes or reduces a position.

### Why is exit price important?

It determines realized profit or loss and helps review whether the trade was managed well.

### Is exit price the same as average exit?

Not always. If a trader exits in multiple fills, the average exit may differ from each individual exit price.

### Can a bad exit ruin a good entry?

Yes. A strong entry can still turn into a poor trade if the exit ignores the plan.

### Should traders review exit price after every trade?

Yes. Reviewing exit price helps traders understand profit protection, loss control, and trade management.
