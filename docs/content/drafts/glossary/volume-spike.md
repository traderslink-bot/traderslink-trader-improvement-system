# Volume Spike

A volume spike is a sudden increase in trading volume compared with recent activity. Traders often watch volume spikes because they can show fresh attention, news reaction, liquidity changes, or momentum.

A volume spike does not guarantee continuation. High volume can appear during breakouts, reversals, selloffs, failed moves, or emotional trading.

## Simple Definition

A volume spike happens when trading volume rises sharply above normal levels.

A volume spike may come from:

1. News.
2. Earnings.
3. SEC filings.
4. Social attention.
5. Breakouts.
6. Breakdowns.
7. Short-term momentum.
8. Institutional or high-volume activity.

The reason for the volume spike matters during trade review.

## Why Volume Spikes Matter

Volume spikes matter because they can show that more traders are paying attention.

This is where traders can get into trouble. They may see high volume and assume the move must continue, even when price is rejecting or the catalyst is weak.

A useful review asks:

1. What caused the volume spike?
2. Did volume support a breakout or breakdown?
3. Did price hold the move?
4. Did volume fade after the first reaction?
5. Was the trader chasing after the spike?
6. Did the setup remain valid?

Volume should be reviewed with price action, not alone.

## Example

A stock normally trades 100,000 shares in the morning but suddenly trades 2 million shares after a press release. That is a volume spike.

If price breaks a key level and holds, the volume may support the move. If price spikes and fades, the volume may show attention but not continuation.

## How To Review Volume Spikes

A good volume spike review looks at context, catalyst, and execution.

Useful questions include:

1. Was the volume unusual for the stock?
2. Was there a catalyst?
3. Did price confirm the volume?
4. Did the trader enter near structure or chase?
5. Did the move hold after the spike?
6. Did the volume create liquidity or volatility risk?

The goal is to understand whether volume supported the trade thesis.

## FAQ

### What is a volume spike?

A volume spike is a sudden increase in trading volume compared with recent or normal activity.

### Does a volume spike mean a stock will keep moving?

No. A volume spike does not guarantee continuation. Price can continue, reject, reverse, or fade.

### Why do traders watch volume spikes?

They watch them because unusual volume can show attention, liquidity, catalyst reaction, or momentum.

### What should traders review with volume spikes?

Traders should review the catalyst, price action, key levels, relative volume, liquidity, and execution timing.

### Can volume spikes happen on failed moves?

Yes. Volume can spike during fakeouts, failed breakouts, panic selling, or reversals.
