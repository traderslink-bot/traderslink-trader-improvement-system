# Entry Price

Entry price is the price where a trader enters a position. It is one of the most important parts of trade review because it affects risk, position sizing, stop location, profit target, and overall trade management.

A good trade idea can become difficult to manage if the entry price is poor. That is why traders often review whether the entry was planned, late, early, or emotional.

## Simple Definition

Entry price is the price paid or received when a trade position is opened.

For example:

1. A trader buys a stock at $2.00.
2. The entry price is $2.00.
3. If the trader adds later, the average entry may change.

The entry price becomes the starting point for risk and trade management review.

## Why Entry Price Matters

Entry price matters because it affects the entire trade plan.

This is where traders can get into trouble. They may chase after a move and use a stop that would have made sense only for an earlier entry.

A useful review asks:

1. Was the entry planned?
2. Was the entry near a key level?
3. Was the entry too late?
4. Was risk defined from the entry?
5. Did the trader chase because of FOMO?
6. Did the entry match the setup?

Entry quality can shape the rest of the trade.

## Example

A breakout trader plans to enter near $2.00 as a stock clears resistance. Instead, they hesitate and enter at $2.25 after the move is extended.

The trade may still work, but the risk-reward is different. The later entry may require a different stop plan and smaller size.

## How To Review Entry Price

A good entry review looks at timing, structure, and risk.

Useful questions include:

1. Where was the planned entry?
2. Where was the actual entry?
3. Did the entry improve or weaken risk-reward?
4. Was the position size correct for the entry?
5. Did the trader enter because of setup or emotion?
6. Has this entry mistake repeated before?

The goal is to understand whether the entry supported the trade plan.

## FAQ

### What is entry price?

Entry price is the price where a trader opens a position.

### Why is entry price important?

It affects risk, stop placement, position sizing, targets, and trade management.

### Is entry price the same as average entry?

Not always. If a trader enters in multiple fills or adds to a position, the average entry may differ from the first entry price.

### Can a late entry hurt risk-reward?

Yes. Entering late can reduce potential reward and increase risk if the stop or target is not adjusted.

### Should traders review entry price after every trade?

Yes. Reviewing entry price helps traders understand whether entries were planned, late, early, or emotional.
