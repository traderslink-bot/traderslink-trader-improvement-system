# Trade Log

A trade log is a structured record of trade details. It usually includes the ticker, date, entry, exit, size, profit or loss, setup, and notes.

A trade log is often part of a trading journal. The log captures what happened, while the broader journal may also include screenshots, emotions, lessons, and trade review notes.

## Simple Definition

A trade log records the details of each trade.

A trade log may include:

1. Date and time.
2. Ticker.
3. Direction.
4. Entry price.
5. Exit price.
6. Position size.
7. Profit or loss.
8. Setup.
9. Fees.
10. Notes.

The goal is to keep trade history accurate and reviewable.

## Why Trade Logs Matter

Trade logs matter because they create the data needed for review.

This is where traders can get into trouble. They may rely on memory and miss patterns that would be obvious in a clean trade log.

A useful review asks:

1. Were all trades recorded?
2. Are entries and exits accurate?
3. Are fees included?
4. Are setups tagged consistently?
5. Are mistakes noted?
6. Can performance be reviewed by setup?

A trade log turns trading history into usable data.

## Example

A trader logs 50 trades and tags each setup. Later, the trader sees that breakout trades are profitable, but dip buys after failed news reactions are consistently losing.

That insight depends on having the trades recorded clearly.

## How To Review A Trade Log

A good trade log review looks for clean data and useful categories.

Useful questions include:

1. Are all trades included?
2. Are timestamps accurate?
3. Are partial exits recorded?
4. Are entries and exits matched correctly?
5. Are setups tagged?
6. Can the trader find repeated issues?

The goal is to make the trade record trustworthy.

## FAQ

### What is a trade log?

A trade log is a structured record of trade details such as ticker, entry, exit, size, result, setup, and notes.

### Is a trade log the same as a trading journal?

Not exactly. A trade log records trade data, while a trading journal may include deeper notes, screenshots, emotions, and lessons.

### Why should traders keep a trade log?

A trade log helps traders review performance, execution, setups, and repeated mistakes.

### What should a trade log include?

It can include date, ticker, entry, exit, size, fees, P&L, setup, and notes.

### Can a trade log help find mistakes?

Yes. A clean log can reveal repeated problems that are hard to notice from memory.
