# Offering

An offering is when a company sells securities to raise capital. In public markets, an offering may involve common stock, preferred stock, warrants, units, or other securities.

Offerings matter to traders because they can increase share supply, create dilution, affect market sentiment, and change the risk profile of a stock.

## Simple Definition

An offering is a capital raise where a company sells securities.

Common offering types include:

1. Public offering.
2. Registered direct offering.
3. Private placement.
4. At-the-market offering.
5. Rights offering.
6. Unit offering.
7. Warrant-linked offering.
8. Shelf takedown.

The exact impact depends on the terms and filings.

## Why Offerings Matter

Offerings matter because they can change share structure and trader expectations.

This is where traders can get into trouble. They may see a stock moving on news but miss that the company has just priced an offering or may soon issue more shares.

A useful review asks:

1. What type of offering was announced?
2. What was the offering price?
3. How many shares or units were sold?
4. Were warrants included?
5. Was the offering dilutive?
6. How did price react after the filing?

Offerings should be reviewed with dilution and filing context.

## Example

A company announces a registered direct offering at $2.00 per share while the stock was trading at $2.80. Traders may review whether the offering price becomes a reference level and whether new shares or warrants increase dilution risk.

The headline alone is not enough. The terms matter.

## How To Review An Offering

A good offering review looks at security type, pricing, size, and follow-up filings.

Useful questions include:

1. Was the offering priced above or below market?
2. Were warrants attached?
3. Are resale filings required?
4. How much cash will the company raise?
5. How much will shares outstanding increase?
6. Did the stock hold key levels after the news?

The goal is to understand how the offering changes risk and supply.

## FAQ

### What is an offering in stocks?

An offering is when a company sells securities to raise capital.

### Are offerings always bad for stocks?

No. Offerings can raise needed capital, but they can also dilute shareholders or pressure price depending on the terms.

### What makes an offering dilutive?

An offering can be dilutive when it increases the number of shares or potential shares.

### Why do warrants matter in offerings?

Warrants can create future share issuance and additional dilution risk.

### What filings should traders review after an offering?

Traders may review prospectus supplements, 8-Ks, S-1s, 424 filings, and resale registrations depending on the offering type.
