# Trading Setup

A trading setup is a repeatable set of conditions that a trader looks for before taking a trade. A setup can include chart structure, catalyst, volume, support and resistance, risk location, and entry criteria.

A setup is not a guarantee that a trade will work. It is a way to organize decision-making so trades are not random.

## Simple Definition

A trading setup is a planned trade situation with specific conditions.

A setup may include:

1. Chart pattern.
2. Support or resistance level.
3. Volume behavior.
4. Catalyst or news.
5. Entry trigger.
6. Stop area.
7. Target area.
8. Risk-reward plan.

The more specific the setup, the easier it is to review later.

## Why Trading Setups Matter

Setups matter because they help traders separate planned trades from impulse trades.

This is where traders can get into trouble. They may think they are trading a setup, but the actual entry may be based on FOMO, a scanner alert, or a fast candle.

A useful review asks:

1. What setup was this supposed to be?
2. Were the setup conditions actually present?
3. Was the entry aligned with the setup?
4. Was risk clear before entry?
5. Did the setup fail?
6. Did the trader follow the plan after failure?

A setup only helps if it is specific enough to review.

## Example

A trader may define a breakout setup as a stock holding under resistance, building volume, breaking the level, and holding above it after the move.

If the trader buys after the stock is already extended and volume is fading, the setup label may be breakout, but the execution may not match the setup.

That difference matters during review.

## How To Review A Trading Setup

A good setup review looks at both idea quality and execution quality.

Useful review questions include:

1. Was the setup clearly defined?
2. Did the chart match the setup?
3. Was the entry near the planned area?
4. Was the stop logical?
5. Did the setup have enough confirmation?
6. Did the trader follow the exit plan?

The goal is to learn which setups are worth repeating and which ones need clearer rules.

## FAQ

### What is a trading setup?

A trading setup is a repeatable set of conditions that a trader looks for before taking a trade.

### Is a trading setup a signal?

Not exactly. A setup is context and planning. It does not guarantee a trade should be taken or that the trade will work.

### Why should traders define setups?

Defined setups make trades easier to review, compare, and improve over time.

### What makes a good setup?

A good setup is specific, repeatable, reviewable, and tied to a clear risk plan.

### Can a setup fail?

Yes. Any setup can fail. That is why risk management and post-trade review matter.
