# Risk Reward Ratio

Risk reward ratio compares the amount a trader is willing to risk with the potential reward of a trade. It is usually used before entering a trade to decide whether the possible upside is worth the possible downside.

A good-looking risk reward ratio does not guarantee a good trade. The setup, probability, execution, liquidity, and trade management still matter.

## Simple Definition

Risk reward ratio compares potential loss to potential gain.

For example:

1. A trader risks $100.
2. The target is $300.
3. The risk reward ratio is 1:3.

That means the potential reward is three times the planned risk.

## Why Risk Reward Ratio Matters

Risk reward matters because it helps traders plan before reacting.

This is where traders can get into trouble. They may enter a trade without knowing where they are wrong or where they plan to take profit.

A useful review asks:

1. Where is the stop?
2. Where is the target?
3. Is the reward worth the risk?
4. Is the stop realistic?
5. Is the target realistic?
6. Did the trader follow the plan?

Risk reward is only useful if the stop and target are based on real structure.

## Example

A trader enters at $2.00, plans to stop at $1.90, and targets $2.30.

The risk is $0.10 per share. The potential reward is $0.30 per share. That is a 1:3 risk reward ratio.

If the trader later moves the stop lower emotionally, the planned ratio no longer reflects the real trade.

## How To Review Risk Reward

A good post-trade review looks at both planned risk reward and actual management.

Useful questions include:

1. Was risk defined before entry?
2. Was the target logical?
3. Did the trader change the stop?
4. Did the trader exit early without a reason?
5. Did the trader hold past invalidation?
6. Was the planned reward realistic?

The goal is not to force every trade into a perfect ratio. The goal is to understand whether the trade was planned clearly.

## FAQ

### What is risk reward ratio?

Risk reward ratio compares the amount a trader is risking with the potential reward of the trade.

### Is a 1:3 risk reward ratio good?

It can be useful, but it does not guarantee a good trade. The setup, probability, and execution still matter.

### Why does risk reward matter?

It helps traders decide whether a trade plan offers enough potential reward for the planned risk.

### Can risk reward change during a trade?

Yes. If the trader moves the stop, changes the target, adds, reduces, or exits early, the actual risk reward can change.

### Is risk reward the same as win rate?

No. Risk reward compares possible loss and gain. Win rate measures how often trades are winners.
