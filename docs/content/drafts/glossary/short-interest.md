# Short Interest

Short interest is the number of shares that have been sold short and not yet bought back or covered. Traders review short interest because it can show how much bearish positioning exists in a stock.

High short interest does not guarantee a short squeeze. A squeeze depends on price action, volume, float, borrow conditions, catalysts, and whether short sellers are pressured to cover.

## Simple Definition

Short interest measures open short positions.

Short interest may be reviewed as:

1. Total shares sold short.
2. Short interest as a percentage of float.
3. Short interest as a percentage of shares outstanding.
4. Days to cover.
5. Changes from the prior report.
6. Comparison with volume.
7. Borrow fee context.
8. Catalyst context.

The meaning depends on the stock’s liquidity and structure.

## Why Short Interest Matters

Short interest matters because it can show how crowded the bearish side may be.

This is where traders can get into trouble. They may see high short interest and assume a squeeze is guaranteed, even when volume is weak or the stock has no catalyst.

A useful review asks:

1. How high is short interest?
2. How does it compare with float?
3. Is short interest increasing or decreasing?
4. What is days to cover?
5. Are borrow fees rising?
6. Did price action pressure shorts?

Short interest is context, not a trade signal by itself.

## Example

A stock has 10 million shares in the float and 3 million shares reported short. That means reported short interest is about 30% of float.

That may be worth watching, but the trader still needs to review volume, catalyst, and price action.

## How To Review Short Interest

A good short interest review looks at positioning and pressure.

Useful questions include:

1. Is the short interest data current?
2. What percentage of float is short?
3. Is volume high enough for covering?
4. Is there a catalyst forcing attention?
5. Is the stock breaking key levels?
6. Are traders assuming a squeeze without evidence?

The goal is to understand whether short interest supports the trade thesis.

## FAQ

### What is short interest?

Short interest is the number of shares sold short and not yet covered.

### Does high short interest guarantee a short squeeze?

No. High short interest does not guarantee a squeeze.

### Why do traders review short interest as a percentage of float?

It helps compare short positioning with the shares generally available for public trading.

### Is short interest data always current?

No. Reported short interest can be delayed, so traders should understand the reporting schedule and limitations.

### What can make short interest more important?

High volume, strong catalysts, rising borrow fees, low float, and price strength can make short interest more relevant.
