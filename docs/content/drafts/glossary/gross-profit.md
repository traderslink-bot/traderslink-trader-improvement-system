# Gross Profit

Gross profit is the total profit from winning trades before subtracting losses. Traders use gross profit to understand how much money winning trades produced over a selected period.

Gross profit does not show overall performance by itself. It needs to be reviewed with gross loss, net profit, profit factor, fees, and risk behavior.

## Simple Definition

Gross profit is the sum of all winning trades.

For example:

1. Winning trade 1: $100.
2. Winning trade 2: $250.
3. Winning trade 3: $150.
4. Gross profit: $500.

This number does not subtract losing trades.

## Why Gross Profit Matters

Gross profit matters because it shows how much the winning side of the trade history produced.

This is where traders can get into trouble. They may focus on gross profit and ignore that gross loss is even larger.

A useful review asks:

1. What was gross profit?
2. What was gross loss?
3. What was net profit?
4. What was profit factor?
5. Did a few large winners create most of the gross profit?
6. Were winners managed well?

Gross profit is useful, but it is only one part of performance review.

## Example

A trader has $4,000 in gross profit and $3,700 in gross loss. The gross profit looks strong, but the net result is only $300 before fees and other costs.

That means the trader should review both sides of the performance, not just the winners.

## How To Review Gross Profit

A good review looks at where the profit came from.

Useful questions include:

1. Which setups produced the most gross profit?
2. Which time windows produced the most winners?
3. Were profits concentrated in only a few trades?
4. Were winners cut early?
5. Were large winners repeatable or unusual?
6. Did fees reduce the final result?

The goal is to understand whether profits came from repeatable process or one-time outliers.

## FAQ

### What is gross profit in trading?

Gross profit is the total profit from all winning trades before subtracting losing trades.

### Is gross profit the same as net profit?

No. Net profit subtracts losses and costs. Gross profit only counts winning trades.

### Why should traders review gross profit?

It helps traders understand how much their winners produced and where profitable trades came from.

### Can gross profit be high while net profit is low?

Yes. Large gross loss, fees, or slippage can reduce or erase gross profit.

### How is gross profit used in profit factor?

Profit factor is calculated by dividing gross profit by gross loss.
