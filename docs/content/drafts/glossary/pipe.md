# PIPE

PIPE stands for private investment in public equity. It is a financing transaction where investors buy securities from a public company in a private placement.

PIPE transactions matter to traders because they can create dilution, resale registration risk, warrant overhang, and future share supply.

## Simple Definition

A PIPE is a private investment in a public company.

A PIPE may include:

1. Common stock.
2. Preferred stock.
3. Warrants.
4. Convertible securities.
5. Units.
6. Registration rights.
7. Lockup terms.
8. Resale registration obligations.

The terms determine the possible impact.

## Why PIPEs Matter

PIPEs matter because they can raise capital for the company while also creating future share supply.

This is where traders can get into trouble. They may see a PIPE as bullish because the company raised money, but miss warrant coverage, discount pricing, or resale registration risk.

A useful review asks:

1. Who invested in the PIPE?
2. What securities were sold?
3. What was the purchase price?
4. Were warrants included?
5. Are resale registration rights included?
6. When can shares be resold?

PIPE review should include both capital raised and dilution risk.

## Example

A company announces a $20 million PIPE with common stock and warrants. The company receives cash, but the securities may increase share supply now or in the future.

If a resale registration is later filed, traders may review whether selling holders can resell shares.

## How To Review A PIPE

A good PIPE review looks at terms, timing, and follow-up filings.

Useful questions include:

1. What was the size of the financing?
2. Was it priced at a discount?
3. Did investors receive warrants?
4. Is there a lockup?
5. Is a resale registration required?
6. How does the PIPE affect fully diluted share count?

The goal is to understand how the financing changes risk and supply.

## FAQ

### What does PIPE mean?

PIPE means private investment in public equity.

### Is a PIPE an offering?

A PIPE is a type of private financing by a public company and may be described as a private placement.

### Are PIPEs dilutive?

They can be dilutive if they add shares or securities that can convert into shares.

### Why do traders care about resale registration after a PIPE?

A resale registration can allow PIPE investors to resell registered shares, which may create supply risk.

### Do PIPEs always hurt stocks?

No. They can provide capital, but the terms, pricing, warrants, and resale rights matter.
