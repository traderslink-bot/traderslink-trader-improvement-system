# Failed Setup

A failed setup happens when a planned trade setup stops behaving as expected. The setup may break a key level, lose volume, fail to confirm, or invalidate the original trade thesis.

A failed setup does not mean the trader did something wrong by taking the trade. The important question is whether the trader recognized the failure and managed risk according to the plan.

## Simple Definition

A failed setup is a trade setup that no longer meets the conditions that made it valid.

A setup may fail when:

1. A breakout cannot hold.
2. Support breaks.
3. Volume fades.
4. A reclaim fails.
5. News reaction fades.
6. A candle closes against the setup.
7. The invalidation level is hit.
8. The thesis no longer applies.

The setup failure should be clear enough to review after the trade.

## Why Failed Setups Matter

Failed setups matter because every strategy will have trades that do not work. The trader’s response to failure is often more important than the failure itself.

This is where traders can get into trouble. They may hold after the setup fails, add to a weak trade, or change the thesis to avoid taking the loss.

A useful review asks:

1. When did the setup fail?
2. Was the invalidation level clear?
3. Did the trader exit when the setup failed?
4. Did the trader add after failure?
5. Was the loss planned or oversized?
6. Did this type of failed setup repeat?

Failed setups are normal. Poor response to failure is what needs review.

## Example

A trader buys a breakout above $2.00. The stock briefly trades above the level, then fails back below $2.00 and volume fades.

The breakout setup may have failed. The review should focus on whether the trader exited, reduced risk, or held after invalidation.

## How To Review A Failed Setup

A good failed setup review looks at setup behavior and trader behavior.

Useful questions include:

1. What was supposed to happen?
2. What actually happened?
3. What sign showed failure?
4. Did the trader follow the failure plan?
5. Did the trader take the planned loss?
6. What should be watched next time?

The goal is to learn from the failure without turning every failed trade into an emotional mistake.

## FAQ

### What is a failed setup?

A failed setup is a trade setup that stops meeting the conditions that made it valid.

### Does a failed setup mean the trade was bad?

Not always. A setup can fail even if the original trade idea was reasonable.

### What should traders do when a setup fails?

They should follow their plan, manage risk, and avoid changing the thesis emotionally.

### Why should failed setups be reviewed?

Reviewing failed setups helps traders understand whether losses were planned, avoidable, or caused by poor trade management.

### Can a failed setup become valid again?

Sometimes a setup can reset or reclaim, but that should usually be treated as a new decision with a fresh plan.
