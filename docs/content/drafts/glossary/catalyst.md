# Catalyst

A catalyst is an event, announcement, filing, or piece of information that can affect a stock’s price or trading activity. Traders often watch catalysts because they can create attention, volume, volatility, or a change in market expectations.

A catalyst does not guarantee a price move. Some catalysts create strong follow-through, while others fade quickly or have little impact.

## Simple Definition

A catalyst is something that may change how traders view a stock.

Catalysts may include:

1. Press releases.
2. Earnings reports.
3. SEC filings.
4. FDA updates.
5. Contract news.
6. Analyst actions.
7. Mergers or acquisitions.
8. Financing announcements.
9. Product launches.
10. Sector news.

The quality and context of the catalyst matter.

## Why Catalysts Matter

Catalysts matter because they can explain why volume and price action changed.

This is where traders can get into trouble. They may react to a headline without reading details, checking filings, or reviewing whether volume and price action confirm interest.

A useful review asks:

1. What was the catalyst?
2. Was it new information?
3. Were details specific or vague?
4. Did volume increase?
5. Did price hold the move?
6. Did the trader chase the reaction?

A catalyst should be reviewed with price action, volume, and risk.

## Example

A company announces a new contract. Traders may view the announcement as a catalyst. If the release includes customer details, terms, and meaningful business impact, the catalyst may be stronger than a vague headline with no specifics.

The trade review should compare the catalyst quality with the market reaction.

## How To Review Catalysts

A good catalyst review looks at headline quality, details, and price response.

Useful questions include:

1. Was the catalyst meaningful?
2. Did the catalyst include financial terms?
3. Did filings add important context?
4. Did volume confirm attention?
5. Did the stock hold key levels?
6. Did the trader enter with a plan?

The goal is to understand whether the catalyst supported the trade thesis.

## FAQ

### What is a catalyst in trading?

A catalyst is an event or information that may affect a stock’s price, volume, or trader attention.

### Does a catalyst guarantee a stock will move?

No. A catalyst does not guarantee price movement or continuation.

### What are examples of stock catalysts?

Examples include earnings, FDA updates, contracts, SEC filings, press releases, mergers, and analyst actions.

### Why do traders review catalyst quality?

Catalyst quality helps traders understand whether the headline had real details or was mostly hype.

### Can a catalyst cause a stock to fade?

Yes. A weak catalyst or overextended reaction can lead to a fade.
