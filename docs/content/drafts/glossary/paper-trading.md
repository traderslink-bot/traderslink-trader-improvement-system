# Paper Trading

Paper trading means practicing trades without using real money. Traders use paper trading to test setups, practice execution, build routines, and learn how their process behaves before risking capital.

Paper trading does not perfectly match live trading. Real money can change emotions, discipline, fills, slippage, and decision quality.

## Simple Definition

Paper trading is simulated trading without real capital at risk.

Paper trading may be used to practice:

1. Trade setups.
2. Entry timing.
3. Exit planning.
4. Risk management.
5. Position sizing.
6. Journaling.
7. Trade review.
8. Platform workflow.
9. Alert response.
10. Discipline rules.

The goal is to learn process before adding real financial risk.

## Why Paper Trading Matters

Paper trading matters because it lets traders practice without immediate capital loss.

This is where traders can get into trouble. They may treat paper trading casually and take trades they would never take with real money.

A useful review asks:

1. Was the paper trade treated seriously?
2. Was risk defined?
3. Did the setup meet criteria?
4. Were entries and exits realistic?
5. Were fills realistic?
6. Did the trader journal the trade?

Paper trading should be reviewed like live trading if it is going to teach useful lessons.

## Example

A beginner trader paper trades breakout setups for a month. They log each entry, exit, setup, and mistake. The trader finds that they often chase after the breakout instead of entering near the planned level.

That insight can help before using real money.

## How To Review Paper Trading

A good paper trading review focuses on process and realism.

Useful questions include:

1. Were rules followed?
2. Were entries realistic?
3. Were spreads and liquidity considered?
4. Did the trader overtrade because there was no real risk?
5. Did the paper results match the plan?
6. What should be improved before live trading?

The goal is to build better habits before increasing risk.

## FAQ

### What is paper trading?

Paper trading is simulated trading without real money at risk.

### Is paper trading useful?

It can be useful for practicing process, setups, platform workflow, journaling, and risk rules.

### Is paper trading the same as live trading?

No. Live trading can involve different emotions, fills, slippage, and discipline challenges.

### Should paper trades be journaled?

Yes. Journaling paper trades can help make practice more serious and reviewable.

### Can paper trading create bad habits?

Yes. If traders take unrealistic trades or ignore risk, paper trading can teach poor habits.
