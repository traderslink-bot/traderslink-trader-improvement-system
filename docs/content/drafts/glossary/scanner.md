# Scanner

A scanner is a tool that searches for stocks matching selected conditions. Traders use scanners to find volume spikes, price movement, gap-ups, new highs, unusual activity, catalysts, or other market conditions.

A scanner does not decide whether a trade is good. It only finds names that match the scan settings. The trader still needs to review the chart, catalyst, liquidity, levels, and risk.

## Simple Definition

A scanner filters the market for stocks that meet specific criteria.

Scanner criteria may include:

1. Price change.
2. Volume.
3. Relative volume.
4. Float.
5. Market cap.
6. Gap up or gap down.
7. New high or low.
8. News activity.
9. Halt or volatility behavior.
10. Technical levels.

The scanner output is only as useful as the scan logic.

## Why Scanners Matter

Scanners matter because they help traders find activity quickly.

This is where traders can get into trouble. They may chase every scanner hit without reviewing whether the move has a real setup.

A useful review asks:

1. What scan condition triggered?
2. Was there a catalyst?
3. Was volume meaningful?
4. Was the stock liquid enough?
5. Was the move already extended?
6. Did the trader wait for a setup?

Scanner hits should be treated as leads, not automatic trades.

## Example

A scanner finds a stock up 40% with 10x relative volume. The trader adds it to a watchlist, checks the news, marks key levels, and waits for confirmation.

The scanner found activity. The trade plan still needs to be built separately.

## How To Review Scanner Results

A good scanner review looks at scan quality and trader response.

Useful questions include:

1. Did the scanner find useful names?
2. Were there too many false positives?
3. Did the trader chase scanner hits?
4. Did alerts help filter noise?
5. Did the scanner find repeatable setups?
6. Did the trader review missed opportunities?

The goal is to improve the scan criteria and the decision process.

## FAQ

### What is a stock scanner?

A stock scanner is a tool that searches for stocks matching selected conditions.

### Is a scanner a trading signal?

No. A scanner finds activity, but traders still need to review setup, risk, and context.

### What can scanners look for?

Scanners can look for volume, price movement, relative volume, gap-ups, new highs, float, news, and other criteria.

### Can scanners cause overtrading?

Yes. Traders can overtrade if they react to every scanner hit without a plan.

### How can scanners support a watchlist?

A scanner can find candidates that are then added to a watchlist for deeper review.

### How does Trader Intelligence relate to scanners?

Trader Intelligence is being built to help traders review scanner context, alerts, execution, and trade management patterns.
