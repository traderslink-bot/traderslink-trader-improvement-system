# Market Order

A market order is an order to buy or sell immediately at the best available price. Traders use market orders when speed matters more than controlling the exact fill price.

A market order can fill quickly, but it can also experience slippage, especially in fast-moving or low-liquidity stocks. That makes market order review important for execution analysis.

## Simple Definition

A market order tells the broker to execute as soon as possible at the available market price.

Market orders may be used for:

1. Fast entries.
2. Fast exits.
3. Stop market exits.
4. Liquid stocks.
5. Urgent risk reduction.
6. Situations where fill speed matters more than price precision.

The tradeoff is that the final fill price is not guaranteed.

## Why Market Orders Matter

Market orders matter because they can get a trader in or out quickly, but the actual fill may be different from the expected price.

This is where traders can get into trouble. They may use a market order in a thin stock with a wide spread and get filled much worse than expected.

A useful review asks:

1. Why was a market order used?
2. Was the stock liquid enough?
3. What was the spread?
4. Was there slippage?
5. Did the order size fit the liquidity?
6. Did the fill change the risk plan?

Market orders should be reviewed with liquidity and spread context.

## Example

A trader wants to exit quickly as a stock breaks support. They send a market sell order. The order fills fast, but the fill is lower than expected because price was dropping and the bid was thin.

The exit may have reduced risk, but the trader should still review slippage and order choice.

## How To Review Market Orders

A good market order review looks at speed, fill quality, and trade context.

Useful questions include:

1. Was speed more important than price control?
2. Was the market moving quickly?
3. Was the spread wide?
4. Did the order create slippage?
5. Was a limit order more appropriate?
6. Did the market order help or hurt the trade?

The goal is to understand whether the order type matched the trade situation.

## FAQ

### What is a market order?

A market order is an order to buy or sell immediately at the best available price.

### Does a market order guarantee a fill?

A market order usually prioritizes execution, but the final fill price is not guaranteed.

### Can market orders cause slippage?

Yes. Market orders can experience slippage, especially in volatile or illiquid stocks.

### When do traders use market orders?

They may use them when speed matters more than exact price control.

### Is a market order safer than a limit order?

Not always. Market orders can fill quickly, but limit orders provide price control. Each has tradeoffs.
