# Rejection

Rejection happens when price tests a level but fails to hold above it, continue through it, or gain acceptance beyond it. Traders often watch rejection near resistance, support, VWAP, moving averages, highs, or other important levels.

A rejection does not guarantee a full reversal. It is a sign that the level mattered during that moment, but traders still need to review confirmation, volume, and risk.

## Simple Definition

Rejection means price failed at or near a level.

A rejection may happen when:

1. Price tests resistance and falls back.
2. Price breaks above a level but cannot hold.
3. A reclaim attempt fails.
4. A breakout loses momentum.
5. A candle wicks into a level and closes away from it.
6. Volume fades after a test.
7. Buyers or sellers fail to follow through.

The level should be meaningful enough to review after the trade.

## Why Rejection Matters

Rejection matters because it can show where a setup is weakening or where traders are losing control of the move.

This is where traders can get into trouble. They may keep holding a breakout trade after price clearly rejects the breakout level or resistance area.

A useful review asks:

1. What level rejected?
2. Was the level important?
3. Did the trader have a plan for rejection?
4. Did volume fade at the level?
5. Did the rejection invalidate the trade thesis?
6. Did the trader respond or hold emotionally?

Rejection review helps traders understand whether they respected changing price action.

## Example

A stock breaks above $2.00 but quickly falls back below the level and closes weak. That can be a rejection of the breakout level.

The trader can review whether the failed hold was a warning sign and whether the exit followed the plan.

## How To Review Rejection

A good rejection review looks at level quality, timing, and response.

Useful questions include:

1. Where did price reject?
2. Was rejection expected or surprising?
3. Did the trader wait for confirmation?
4. Did the rejection trigger an exit or reduction?
5. Did the trader add after rejection?
6. Has this same behavior caused losses before?

The goal is to learn whether rejection was recognized and managed correctly.

## FAQ

### What is rejection in trading?

Rejection happens when price tests a level but fails to hold or continue beyond it.

### Does rejection always mean reversal?

No. Rejection can lead to reversal, chop, or another attempt at the level.

### Where does rejection happen?

It can happen at resistance, support, VWAP, moving averages, highs, lows, or other key levels.

### Why do traders review rejection?

They review rejection to understand whether a setup weakened and whether trade management responded properly.

### Is rejection the same as a failed breakout?

A failed breakout can involve rejection, but rejection can happen in other contexts too.

### How does Trader Intelligence relate to rejection?

Trader Intelligence is being built to help traders review rejection behavior, execution, invalidation, and trade management patterns.
