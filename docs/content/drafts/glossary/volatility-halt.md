# Volatility Halt

A volatility halt is a temporary pause in trading triggered by fast price movement. It gives the market time to reset before trading resumes.

A volatility halt does not guarantee direction after the pause. The stock may reopen higher, lower, sideways, or with a wide spread depending on order imbalance, liquidity, and trader demand.

## Simple Definition

A volatility halt is a trading pause caused by extreme or rapid price movement.

Volatility halts may happen when:

1. Price moves too far too quickly.
2. A stock is reacting to news.
3. Buyers or sellers overwhelm available liquidity.
4. Spreads widen sharply.
5. Order imbalance increases.
6. Exchange rules trigger a pause.

The rules and halt code can vary by exchange and market structure.

## Why Volatility Halts Matter

Volatility halts matter because they can interrupt entries and exits during fast-moving trades.

This is where traders can get into trouble. They may chase a stock just before a halt and then have no control over the exit until trading resumes.

A useful review asks:

1. Was the stock already extended?
2. Was the halt expected from volatility?
3. What was the spread before the halt?
4. How did price reopen?
5. Was liquidity thin after resumption?
6. Did the trader have a plan for halt risk?

Volatility halt review should focus on risk, timing, and resumption behavior.

## Example

A low-float stock runs quickly after a catalyst and triggers a volatility halt. Trading pauses, then resumes with a large spread and fast movement.

The trader should review whether the entry was planned or whether they chased into halt risk.

## How To Review A Volatility Halt

A good review looks at the move before the halt and the behavior after resumption.

Useful questions include:

1. What level was price near before the halt?
2. Was volume expanding?
3. Did the halt happen near resistance or high of day?
4. Did the stock reopen with continuation or rejection?
5. Did execution quality worsen after resumption?
6. Did the trader manage risk after the reopen?

The goal is to understand whether the halt changed the trade thesis.

## FAQ

### What is a volatility halt?

A volatility halt is a temporary trading pause triggered by fast or extreme price movement.

### Does a volatility halt mean a stock will keep moving up?

No. A volatility halt does not guarantee continuation in either direction.

### Can traders exit during a volatility halt?

Trading is paused during the halt, so regular exits must wait until trading resumes.

### Why are volatility halts risky?

They can interrupt exits, widen spreads, and create uncertain reopening prices.

### What should traders review after a volatility halt?

They can review entry timing, spread, liquidity, resumption behavior, and whether the trade still matched the plan.
