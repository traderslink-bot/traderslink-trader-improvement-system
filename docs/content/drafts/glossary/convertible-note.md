# Convertible Note

A convertible note is debt that can convert into shares under certain terms. Companies may use convertible notes to raise capital, while investors may receive the right to convert debt into equity later.

Convertible notes matter to traders because they can create future dilution and share supply, especially when conversion terms are favorable to the holder.

## Simple Definition

A convertible note is a loan or debt security that may convert into stock.

Convertible note terms may include:

1. Principal amount.
2. Interest rate.
3. Maturity date.
4. Conversion price.
5. Conversion discount.
6. Floor price.
7. Reset provisions.
8. Default terms.
9. Registration rights.
10. Share issuance limits.

The risk depends heavily on the specific terms.

## Why Convertible Notes Matter

Convertible notes matter because debt can become equity.

This is where traders can get into trouble. They may review current shares outstanding but miss notes that could convert into many new shares later.

A useful review asks:

1. What is the principal amount?
2. What is the conversion price?
3. Is the conversion price fixed or variable?
4. Are there reset or discount terms?
5. Are converted shares registered for resale?
6. What is the fully diluted share count?

Convertible notes should be reviewed with dilution and filing context.

## Example

A company raises money through a convertible note that can convert into common stock at a discount to market price. If the note converts, shares outstanding can increase.

That potential increase can affect dilution risk and future supply.

## How To Review A Convertible Note

A good convertible note review looks at conversion terms and dilution impact.

Useful questions include:

1. Is the note already convertible?
2. What is the conversion formula?
3. Is there a floor price?
4. Does the note include warrants?
5. Are resale shares registered?
6. Could conversion create selling pressure?

The goal is to understand how the note may affect future share supply.

## FAQ

### What is a convertible note?

A convertible note is debt that can convert into shares under certain terms.

### Are convertible notes dilutive?

They can be dilutive if they convert into shares.

### What is a conversion price?

The conversion price is the price used to determine how many shares the note converts into.

### Why do traders review convertible notes?

They review them to understand future dilution, share supply, and financing risk.

### Are all convertible notes risky?

Not all convertible notes are the same. Fixed, capped, variable, discounted, and reset terms can create very different risks.
