# Stop Limit Order

A stop limit order is a stop order that becomes a limit order after the stop price is triggered. Traders may use stop limit orders when they want a stop trigger but also want price control after the trigger.

A stop limit order does not guarantee a fill. If price moves through the limit too quickly or liquidity is not available, the order may not execute.

## Simple Definition

A stop limit order has two key prices:

1. Stop price: the trigger level.
2. Limit price: the worst acceptable fill price.

For example, a trader may set a sell stop at $1.90 with a limit of $1.88. If price reaches $1.90, the order activates, but it will only sell at $1.88 or better.

## Why Stop Limit Orders Matter

Stop limit orders matter because they offer more price control than stop market orders, but they can leave the trader stuck if the order does not fill.

This is where traders can get into trouble. They may think they are protected by a stop, but the stop limit order may not execute during a fast drop.

A useful review asks:

1. What was the stop price?
2. What was the limit price?
3. Did the order fill?
4. Was there enough liquidity?
5. Did price move through the limit too fast?
6. Did the missed fill increase the loss?

Stop limit orders should be reviewed for both price control and fill risk.

## Example

A trader holds a stock at $2.00 and places a stop limit sell order with a stop at $1.90 and a limit at $1.88. Price quickly drops to $1.80.

The order may trigger but not fill if the market skips through the limit. The trader still has the position unless another exit happens.

## How To Review Stop Limit Orders

A good review looks at order design and market conditions.

Useful questions include:

1. Was the limit price realistic?
2. Was the stock liquid enough?
3. Did the order fill fully, partially, or not at all?
4. Did the trader understand the missed-fill risk?
5. Would a stop market order have matched the risk plan better?
6. Did the order type protect or hurt the trade?

The goal is to understand whether price control was worth the fill risk.

## FAQ

### What is a stop limit order?

A stop limit order triggers a limit order after the stop price is reached.

### Does a stop limit order guarantee a fill?

No. It controls price, but it may not fill if price moves beyond the limit or liquidity is not available.

### Why use a stop limit order?

A trader may use it to avoid filling far below or above a certain price after the stop trigger.

### What is the risk of a stop limit order?

The main risk is that the order may not fill, leaving the trader still in the position.

### How is a stop limit different from a stop market order?

A stop market order prioritizes execution after the stop trigger. A stop limit order adds price control but may miss the fill.
