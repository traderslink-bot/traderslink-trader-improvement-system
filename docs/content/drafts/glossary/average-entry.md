# Average Entry

Average entry is the weighted average price of a position after one or more buys or entries. It helps traders understand the actual cost basis of the position after scaling in, adding, or receiving multiple fills.

Average entry is important because it can change the risk profile of a trade. Adding at the wrong time can improve or worsen the average entry depending on the setup and plan.

## Simple Definition

Average entry is the average price paid for a position, weighted by share size.

For example:

1. A trader buys 100 shares at $2.00.
2. The trader buys another 100 shares at $2.20.
3. The average entry is $2.10.

If the share sizes are different, the average entry needs to be weighted by the number of shares in each fill.

## Why Average Entry Matters

Average entry matters because it affects risk, profit and loss, stop placement, and trade management.

This is where traders can get into trouble. They may add to a position without realizing how much the average entry changed or how much risk increased.

A useful review asks:

1. What was the first entry?
2. What was the final average entry?
3. Did adds improve the trade?
4. Did adds increase risk too much?
5. Did the average entry move closer to invalidation?
6. Did the trader add according to plan?

Average entry helps reveal whether scaling decisions were structured or emotional.

## Example

A trader enters at $2.00 and adds at $1.80 after the stock drops. The average entry may improve numerically, but the setup may be weaker if the stock broke support.

That means a lower average entry is not automatically good. The reason for the add matters.

## How To Review Average Entry

A good average entry review looks at both math and behavior.

Useful questions include:

1. Was the initial entry planned?
2. Were adds planned or emotional?
3. Did the average entry improve risk-reward?
4. Did the average entry hide a broken setup?
5. Was position size still controlled?
6. Did the trader exit based on the plan or the average price?

The goal is to understand whether the average entry supported the trade idea.

## FAQ

### What is average entry?

Average entry is the weighted average price of a position after one or more entries or adds.

### Is average entry the same as first entry?

Not always. If a trader adds or receives multiple fills, the average entry can differ from the first entry.

### Why does average entry matter?

It affects profit and loss, risk, stop planning, and trade management.

### Is lowering average entry always good?

No. Lowering average entry by adding to a weak or broken setup can increase risk.

### Should traders review average entry after adding?

Yes. Reviewing average entry can show whether adds were planned, helpful, or emotional.
