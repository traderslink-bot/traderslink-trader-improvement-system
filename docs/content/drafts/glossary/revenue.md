# Revenue

Revenue is the money a company earns from selling products or services before expenses are subtracted. Traders review revenue because it can help show business activity, growth, customer demand, and whether a company is generating real sales.

Revenue does not guarantee profitability. A company can have revenue and still lose money if costs and expenses are too high.

## Simple Definition

Revenue is the top-line sales number.

Revenue may come from:

1. Product sales.
2. Service fees.
3. Subscriptions.
4. Licensing.
5. Contracts.
6. Usage fees.
7. Royalties.
8. Other business activity.

The source and quality of revenue matter during review.

## Why Revenue Matters

Revenue matters because it helps traders understand whether a company is generating business activity.

This is where traders can get into trouble. They may focus on revenue growth without checking margins, expenses, cash burn, or whether the revenue is recurring.

A useful review asks:

1. How much revenue did the company report?
2. Is revenue growing or shrinking?
3. Is the revenue recurring or one-time?
4. What are gross margins?
5. Are expenses growing faster than revenue?
6. Is revenue enough to reduce cash burn?

Revenue should be reviewed with profitability and cash context.

## Example

A company reports revenue growth from $2 million to $5 million. That sounds positive, but traders still need to review whether operating expenses also increased and whether the company remains unprofitable.

Revenue growth alone does not tell the full story.

## How To Review Revenue

A good revenue review looks at trend, quality, and sustainability.

Useful questions include:

1. Was revenue higher than the prior period?
2. What caused the change?
3. Was revenue from core operations?
4. Is revenue recurring?
5. Did gross margin improve?
6. Did revenue reduce financing risk?

The goal is to understand whether revenue supports the company story.

## FAQ

### What is revenue?

Revenue is the money a company earns from selling products or services before expenses are subtracted.

### Is revenue the same as profit?

No. Revenue is the top line. Profit is what remains after costs and expenses.

### Why do traders review revenue?

They review revenue to understand business activity, growth, customer demand, and financial context.

### Can a company have revenue and still lose money?

Yes. A company can have revenue but still report net losses if costs and expenses are higher.

### What is revenue growth?

Revenue growth means revenue increased compared with a prior period.
