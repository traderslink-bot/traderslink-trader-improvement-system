# Alert

An alert is a notification that fires when a selected condition happens. Traders use alerts to monitor price levels, news, volume, scanner activity, watchlists, and trade conditions without staring at every ticker all day.

An alert is not a trade signal by itself. It tells the trader that something happened and that the stock may need review.

## Simple Definition

An alert is a notification based on a condition.

Alerts may be based on:

1. Price levels.
2. News releases.
3. Volume spikes.
4. Relative volume.
5. Scanner conditions.
6. Watchlist tickers.
7. Halt or resumption events.
8. Support or resistance levels.
9. Breakouts or breakdowns.
10. Filing activity.

The alert should connect to a clear review process.

## Why Alerts Matter

Alerts matter because they help traders focus attention at the right time.

This is where traders can get into trouble. They may treat an alert as a reason to immediately enter instead of reviewing the setup.

A useful review asks:

1. What condition triggered the alert?
2. Was the alert useful?
3. Was the level clear?
4. Did the trader review context first?
5. Did the trader chase after the alert?
6. Did the alert improve decision quality?

Alerts should support preparation, not create impulse trades.

## Example

A trader sets an alert at the premarket high of a news-driven stock. When the alert fires, the trader checks volume, spread, liquidity, and whether price is holding the level.

The alert starts the review. It does not complete the trade decision.

## How To Review Alerts

A good alert review looks at alert quality and trader response.

Useful questions include:

1. Was the alert placed at a meaningful level?
2. Did it fire too early or too late?
3. Did it reduce noise?
4. Did it cause emotional trading?
5. Did the trader have a plan after the alert?
6. Should the alert criteria be adjusted?

The goal is to make alerts more useful and less distracting.

## FAQ

### What is an alert in trading?

An alert is a notification that fires when a selected trading condition happens.

### Is an alert a trade signal?

No. An alert is a prompt to review a stock or condition, not an automatic buy or sell signal.

### What can traders set alerts for?

Traders can set alerts for price, news, volume, scanner conditions, watchlists, filings, and other market events.

### Can alerts help reduce screen watching?

Yes. Alerts can help traders monitor important conditions without watching every ticker constantly.

### Can alerts cause bad trades?

They can if a trader reacts emotionally instead of reviewing the setup and risk.

### How does Trader Intelligence relate to alerts?

Trader Intelligence is being built to help traders review alert context, execution, and trade management patterns.
