# Scale Out

Scale out means reducing or closing a position in stages instead of exiting all at once. Traders may scale out to protect profit, reduce risk, or manage a trade as price reaches different levels.

Scaling out is not automatically good or bad. It depends on whether the exits are planned, whether they match the setup, and whether the trader manages the remaining position well.

## Simple Definition

To scale out means to exit part of a position at a time.

For example:

1. A trader holds 1,000 shares.
2. The trader sells 300 shares at the first target.
3. The trader sells another 300 shares later.
4. The trader keeps 400 shares open.

Each exit affects the average exit price and remaining risk.

## Why Scaling Out Matters

Scaling out matters because it changes profit protection and upside exposure.

This is where traders can get into trouble. They may scale out too early from fear, sell too much before the planned move, or fail to manage the remaining shares.

A useful review asks:

1. Was the scale-out planned?
2. Did it happen at a key level?
3. Did it protect profit?
4. Did it reduce upside too much?
5. Did the trader manage the remaining position well?
6. Did the average exit improve or weaken the trade result?

Scaling out should be reviewed as part of the full trade sequence.

## Example

A trader enters at $2.00 and sells half at $2.30. The remaining shares are held for a larger target. If the trader planned that exit, the scale-out may support the trade plan.

If the trader sold half only because of fear and then had no plan for the rest, the scale-out may need review.

## How To Review Scale-Outs

A good scale-out review looks at timing, reason, and remaining position management.

Useful questions include:

1. Why was the first partial exit taken?
2. Was the exit based on a target or emotion?
3. How much size was reduced?
4. Did risk decrease after the scale-out?
5. Was the remaining position managed with a plan?
6. Did the scale-out improve the average exit?

The goal is to understand whether scaling out helped the trade plan.

## FAQ

### What does scale out mean?

Scale out means reducing or exiting a position in stages instead of closing the entire position at once.

### Is scaling out the same as taking partial profits?

Often, yes. Taking partial profits is a common form of scaling out.

### Why do traders scale out?

They may scale out to protect profit, reduce risk, or manage different target levels.

### Can scaling out hurt a trade?

Yes. Scaling out too early or without a plan can reduce upside or weaken trade management.

### How does scaling out affect average exit?

Each partial exit contributes to the weighted average exit price of the trade.
