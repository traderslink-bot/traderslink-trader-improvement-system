# Stop Market Order

A stop market order is a stop order that becomes a market order after the stop price is triggered. Traders often use stop market orders to exit quickly when price reaches a planned risk level.

A stop market order can help with speed, but it does not guarantee the stop price. Once triggered, the order fills at the best available market price, which can create slippage.

## Simple Definition

A stop market order has two parts:

1. A stop price that triggers the order.
2. A market order that executes after the trigger.

For example, a trader may place a sell stop market order at $1.90. If price reaches $1.90, the stop triggers and sends a market sell order.

## Why Stop Market Orders Matter

Stop market orders matter because they prioritize getting out after the stop is triggered.

This is where traders can get into trouble. They may expect to exit exactly at the stop price, but fast markets or low liquidity can create a worse fill.

A useful review asks:

1. What was the stop price?
2. What was the actual fill price?
3. Was there slippage?
4. Was the stock liquid enough?
5. Was the spread wide?
6. Did the trader size properly for the risk?

Stop market orders should be reviewed with execution quality in mind.

## Example

A trader buys a stock at $2.00 and places a stop market order at $1.90. Price drops quickly through $1.90 and the order fills at $1.86.

The stop triggered correctly, but the final fill had $0.04 of slippage.

## How To Review Stop Market Orders

A good review looks at both risk control and fill quality.

Useful questions include:

1. Was the stop price based on a real invalidation level?
2. Did the order trigger as expected?
3. How much slippage occurred?
4. Was the stock volatile or illiquid?
5. Would a different order type have fit the plan better?
6. Did the stop help prevent a larger loss?

The goal is to understand whether the stop order matched the trade situation.

## FAQ

### What is a stop market order?

A stop market order triggers a market order after the stop price is reached.

### Does a stop market order guarantee the stop price?

No. Once triggered, it becomes a market order and can fill with slippage.

### Why do traders use stop market orders?

They use them when fast execution after a stop trigger is more important than exact price control.

### What is the risk of a stop market order?

The main risk is slippage, especially in volatile or illiquid stocks.

### Is a stop market order the same as a stop limit order?

No. A stop market order prioritizes execution. A stop limit order adds a limit price but may not fill.
