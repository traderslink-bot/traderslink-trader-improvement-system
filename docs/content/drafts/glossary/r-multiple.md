# R Multiple

R multiple measures a trade result in units of planned risk. It helps traders compare trades of different sizes by measuring outcomes relative to the amount risked.

R multiple does not guarantee better trading. It is a review tool that helps traders understand whether wins and losses are large or small compared with the original risk plan.

## Simple Definition

R means the amount planned to risk on a trade.

For example:

1. Planned risk: $100.
2. Trade profit: $300.
3. Result: +3R.

If the trader loses $100, the result is -1R. If the trader loses $50, the result is -0.5R.

## Why R Multiple Matters

R multiple matters because dollar results can be misleading.

This is where traders can get into trouble. A $500 win may look good, but if the trader risked $1,000, the result is only +0.5R. A $200 win may be stronger if the trader only risked $50.

A useful review asks:

1. What was the planned risk?
2. What was the actual result in R?
3. Did the trader lose more than 1R?
4. Did the trader cut winners before the plan?
5. Which setups produce the best R multiples?
6. Did trade management improve or reduce R outcome?

R multiple helps normalize trade review.

## Example

A trader risks $100 on each trade.

One trade makes $250. That result is +2.5R. Another trade loses $100. That result is -1R.

Instead of only comparing dollars, the trader can compare the quality of risk-adjusted results.

## How To Review R Multiple

A good R multiple review looks at planned risk and actual behavior.

Useful questions include:

1. Was the original risk clearly defined?
2. Did the trader keep losses near -1R?
3. Did winners reach planned R targets?
4. Did early exits reduce R results?
5. Did oversized losses break the plan?
6. Which setups produce positive R over time?

The goal is to review consistency across different trade sizes and setups.

## FAQ

### What is R multiple in trading?

R multiple measures a trade result compared with the planned risk on that trade.

### What does +2R mean?

+2R means the trade made two times the planned risk amount.

### What does -1R mean?

-1R means the trade lost the planned risk amount.

### Why use R multiple instead of dollars?

R multiple normalizes trade results, making it easier to compare trades with different sizes or risk amounts.

### Can a trade lose more than -1R?

Yes. Slippage, moving stops, oversized positions, or ignoring risk can create losses larger than planned.
