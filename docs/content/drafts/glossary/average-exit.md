# Average Exit

Average exit is the weighted average price where a trader closes or reduces a position across one or more exit fills. It helps traders understand the actual exit result when they scale out, take partial profits, or exit in multiple orders.

Average exit is important because the final result of a trade may not be represented by one exit price. Multiple exits can help or hurt trade management depending on timing and plan.

## Simple Definition

Average exit is the average price received when closing or reducing a position, weighted by share size.

For example:

1. A trader sells 100 shares at $2.20.
2. The trader sells another 100 shares at $2.40.
3. The average exit is $2.30.

If the share sizes are different, the average exit should be weighted by the number of shares sold in each exit.

## Why Average Exit Matters

Average exit matters because it shows how well the trader handled the exit process as a whole.

This is where traders can get into trouble. They may take one strong partial exit but then give back the rest, or they may scale out too early and leave the main move behind.

A useful review asks:

1. What was the planned exit?
2. What was the actual average exit?
3. Did partial exits protect profit?
4. Did the trader exit too early?
5. Did the trader hold the rest too long?
6. Did the final average exit match the trade plan?

The average exit helps review the full exit sequence.

## Example

A trader enters at $2.00, sells half at $2.30, and sells the rest at $2.10. The average exit is lower than the first profit-taking price.

That does not automatically mean the exit was bad, but it does show why reviewing only the best exit fill can be misleading.

## How To Review Average Exit

A good average exit review looks at timing, structure, and decision quality.

Useful questions include:

1. Were exits planned before the trade?
2. Did the trader scale out with a reason?
3. Did the average exit protect profit?
4. Did the trader give back too much open profit?
5. Was the final exit emotional or planned?
6. How did the average exit compare with the target?

The goal is to understand whether the exit process supported the trade idea.

## FAQ

### What is average exit?

Average exit is the weighted average price where a trader closes or reduces a position across one or more exit fills.

### Is average exit the same as final exit price?

Not always. The final exit price is the last exit fill. Average exit includes all exit fills weighted by size.

### Why does average exit matter?

It helps traders review the full exit sequence, including partial exits and final exits.

### Can average exit show poor trade management?

Yes. It can reveal early exits, profit giveback, late exits, or uneven scaling decisions.

### Should traders compare average exit with average entry?

Yes. Comparing average exit with average entry helps review realized trade performance.
