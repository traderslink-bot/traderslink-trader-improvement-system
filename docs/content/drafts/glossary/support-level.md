# Support Level

A support level is a price area where buyers have previously stepped in or where price has stopped falling. Traders use support levels to plan entries, exits, stops, invalidation, and risk.

A support level does not guarantee a bounce. It can hold, break, fake out, or become resistance later.

## Simple Definition

A support level is a specific price area where price has found support before.

Support levels may come from:

1. Prior lows.
2. Previous consolidation.
3. High-volume price areas.
4. Prior breakout levels.
5. Moving averages.
6. VWAP.
7. Psychological prices.
8. Daily or intraday chart structure.

The stronger the level history, the easier it is to review after the trade.

## Why Support Levels Matter

Support levels matter because they can help traders define risk.

This is where traders can get into trouble. They may buy near support but fail to exit when the support level breaks.

A useful review asks:

1. What support level was used?
2. Was it based on real chart history?
3. Did price hold the level?
4. Did the level break?
5. Was the stop below support?
6. Did the trader exit after breakdown?

A support level is most useful when tied to a clear plan.

## Example

A stock bounces from $2.00 three times. Traders may mark $2.00 as a support level.

If price later breaks below $2.00 and fails to reclaim, the support level may no longer be valid for the trade thesis.

## How To Review A Support Level

A good support level review looks at level quality and the trader’s response.

Useful questions include:

1. Was the support level clear?
2. Was the entry close enough to support?
3. Did price respect the level?
4. Did the trader use the level for invalidation?
5. Did the trader add after the level broke?
6. Did the level later become resistance?

The goal is to understand whether the support level helped the trade plan.

## FAQ

### What is a support level?

A support level is a price area where buyers have previously stepped in or where price has stopped falling.

### Does a support level always hold?

No. Support levels can break, fake out, or become resistance later.

### How do traders find support levels?

They may use prior lows, consolidation areas, volume zones, moving averages, VWAP, or psychological price levels.

### Why do traders use support levels?

They use support levels to plan entries, exits, stops, invalidation, and risk.

### What happens when support breaks?

A support break can become a breakdown, but it does not guarantee continued downside.
