# Dilution

Dilution happens when a company issues new shares or creates potential new shares, reducing the ownership percentage of existing shareholders. Traders review dilution because it can affect share supply, market cap, price reaction, and risk.

Dilution does not always mean a stock must go down, but it can create pressure depending on the amount, timing, pricing, and market context.

## Simple Definition

Dilution means existing shareholders own a smaller percentage after new shares or potential shares are added.

Dilution can come from:

1. Stock offerings.
2. Warrants.
3. Convertible notes.
4. Preferred shares.
5. Stock-based compensation.
6. PIPE financings.
7. Resale registrations.
8. ATM offerings.

The details are usually found in filings.

## Why Dilution Matters

Dilution matters because it can increase share supply and change valuation.

This is where traders can get into trouble. They may trade a stock based on float or catalyst while ignoring recent filings that show new shares, warrants, or convertible securities.

A useful review asks:

1. What caused the dilution?
2. How many new shares are involved?
3. Are warrants attached?
4. Is there a resale registration?
5. What is the fully diluted share count?
6. Did the dilution risk affect price reaction?

Dilution review is part of share structure review.

## Example

A company sells 10 million new shares in an offering. If the company previously had 20 million shares outstanding, the new shares can significantly increase total supply.

The trader should review the offering price, warrant terms, and whether the market had already expected the raise.

## How To Review Dilution

A good dilution review looks at filings, share count, and timing.

Useful questions include:

1. What filing disclosed the dilution?
2. Did shares outstanding increase?
3. Are there warrants or convertibles?
4. Is the dilution immediate or potential?
5. How much cash did the company raise?
6. Did the stock react strongly or absorb the news?

The goal is to understand whether dilution risk changes the trade thesis.

## FAQ

### What is dilution in stocks?

Dilution happens when new shares or potential shares reduce existing shareholders’ ownership percentage.

### Is dilution always bad?

Not always. Dilution can fund growth or survival, but it can also pressure price and reduce ownership percentage.

### What causes dilution?

Offerings, warrants, convertibles, preferred shares, stock compensation, and resale registrations can create dilution.

### Why do traders review dilution risk?

They review it because new supply can affect price behavior, valuation, and trade risk.

### Where can traders find dilution information?

Dilution details are often found in SEC filings, prospectuses, 8-Ks, S-1s, 424 filings, and financial statements.
