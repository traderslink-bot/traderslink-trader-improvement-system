# Gross Loss

Gross loss is the total loss from losing trades before it is subtracted from gross profit. Traders use gross loss to understand how much losing trades cost over a selected period.

Gross loss does not show overall performance by itself. It should be reviewed with gross profit, net profit, profit factor, average loss, drawdown, and risk behavior.

## Simple Definition

Gross loss is the sum of all losing trades.

For example:

1. Losing trade 1: $100.
2. Losing trade 2: $250.
3. Losing trade 3: $150.
4. Gross loss: $500.

This number does not include winning trades. It only measures the total losing side of the trade history.

## Why Gross Loss Matters

Gross loss matters because it shows how much damage losing trades caused.

This is where traders can get into trouble. They may have strong gross profit, but gross loss may be large enough to erase most of it.

A useful review asks:

1. What was gross loss?
2. What was gross profit?
3. What was net profit?
4. What was profit factor?
5. Did a few large losses create most of the gross loss?
6. Were losses controlled according to the plan?

Gross loss helps traders review whether risk is being contained.

## Example

A trader has $5,000 in gross profit and $4,800 in gross loss. The winning trades produced a lot, but losing trades nearly erased the result.

That does not automatically mean the strategy is bad. It means the trader should review risk control, average loss, and loss containment.

## How To Review Gross Loss

A good review looks at where the losses came from.

Useful questions include:

1. Which setups created the most gross loss?
2. Were losses planned or avoidable?
3. Did the trader ignore stops?
4. Did one trade cause most of the loss?
5. Did losses increase after emotional decisions?
6. Did slippage, fees, or liquidity add to the loss?

The goal is to understand whether losses were normal strategy losses or process problems.

## FAQ

### What is gross loss in trading?

Gross loss is the total loss from all losing trades before subtracting it from gross profit.

### Is gross loss the same as net loss?

No. Gross loss only counts losing trades. Net profit or net loss compares gains and losses after combining results.

### Why should traders review gross loss?

It helps traders understand how much losing trades cost and whether risk is being controlled.

### Can gross loss be high even if the trader is profitable?

Yes. A trader can be profitable overall while still having high gross loss. The question is whether gross profit is meaningfully larger.

### How is gross loss used in profit factor?

Profit factor is calculated by dividing gross profit by gross loss.
