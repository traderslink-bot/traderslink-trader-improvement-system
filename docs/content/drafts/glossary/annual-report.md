# Annual Report

An annual report is a filing that provides company financial and business information for a full fiscal year. Traders review annual reports to understand business model, revenue, expenses, cash, debt, risk factors, dilution, and long-term context.

An annual report does not guarantee a stock will move. It is a research document that helps traders understand company risk and financial condition.

## Simple Definition

An annual report is a company’s full-year financial and business report.

It may include:

1. Business description.
2. Risk factors.
3. Financial statements.
4. Revenue.
5. Profit or loss.
6. Cash position.
7. Debt.
8. Share count.
9. Management discussion.
10. Auditor information.

For traders, risk factors and liquidity sections can be especially useful.

## Why Annual Reports Matter

Annual reports matter because they show deeper context than a press release or headline.

This is where traders can get into trouble. They may trade a stock based only on news while missing financial weakness, going concern language, dilution risk, or business risks disclosed in the annual report.

A useful review asks:

1. What does the company actually do?
2. Is revenue growing or shrinking?
3. How much cash does the company have?
4. Does the company have a going concern warning?
5. Did share count increase?
6. What risks are disclosed?

Annual reports help traders understand the background behind a stock.

## Example

A company announces a new product update, but its annual report shows limited cash, large losses, and a history of financing through share issuance. Traders may review whether the news reaction could be affected by future dilution risk.

The annual report can add important context to the catalyst.

## How To Review An Annual Report

A good annual report review focuses on financial condition, risks, and business reality.

Useful questions include:

1. What changed from the prior year?
2. Is the company generating revenue?
3. Is cash enough for operations?
4. Are losses increasing?
5. What are the major risk factors?
6. Did dilution occur during the year?

The goal is to understand whether the company context supports or weakens the trade thesis.

## FAQ

### What is an annual report?

An annual report is a company filing that provides financial and business information for a full fiscal year.

### Why do traders review annual reports?

They review them to understand business model, cash, revenue, debt, risk factors, dilution, and company context.

### Does an annual report always move a stock?

No. Market reaction depends on expectations, details, timing, and trading context.

### What should small-cap traders look for in annual reports?

Small-cap traders often review cash, cash burn, debt, share count, risk factors, going concern language, and financing history.

### Is an annual report the same as a quarterly report?

No. An annual report covers a full fiscal year, while a quarterly report covers one quarter.
