# Average Down

Average down means adding to a position after price moves against the trade, lowering the average entry price. Traders may do this intentionally as part of a plan, but it can also become a risky habit when used emotionally.

Averaging down is not automatically smart or safe. It can increase exposure while the setup is weakening. That is why it should be reviewed carefully.

## Simple Definition

To average down means to add to a position at a lower price than the original entry.

For example:

1. A trader buys at $2.00.
2. The stock drops to $1.80.
3. The trader buys more shares.
4. The average entry becomes lower than $2.00.

The average entry improves mathematically, but the trade risk may increase.

## Why Averaging Down Matters

Averaging down matters because it can turn a planned trade into a larger loss if the trader keeps adding while the setup fails.

This is where traders can get into trouble. They may focus on lowering average entry instead of asking whether the trade idea is still valid.

A useful review asks:

1. Was the add planned before entry?
2. Did the setup remain valid?
3. Did the stock break support?
4. Did risk increase beyond the plan?
5. Was the trader adding because of hope?
6. Did the trader exit if the idea failed?

A lower average entry is not the same as lower risk.

## Example

A trader buys a stock at $2.00 with support near $1.90. The stock breaks below $1.90, but the trader adds at $1.80 to lower the average.

The average entry may improve, but the setup may already be broken. That makes the add important to review.

## How To Review Averaging Down

A good averaging down review looks at plan, risk, and setup validity.

Useful questions include:

1. Was averaging down part of the original plan?
2. Was there a defined second entry level?
3. Did the trader add after invalidation?
4. Did position size become too large?
5. Did the trader ignore the stop?
6. Has this behavior repeated before?

The goal is to separate planned scaling from emotional loss management.

## FAQ

### What does average down mean?

Average down means adding to a position at a lower price after the trade has moved against the original entry.

### Is averaging down always bad?

Not always, but it can be risky if it is emotional or if the setup has failed.

### Why do traders average down?

They may average down to lower their average entry, but the reason should be reviewed carefully.

### Can averaging down increase risk?

Yes. It increases position size and can increase loss if the trade continues moving against the trader.

### How is averaging down different from scaling in?

Scaling in can be planned in stages. Averaging down specifically adds after price moves against the position.
