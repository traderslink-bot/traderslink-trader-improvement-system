# Short Squeeze

A short squeeze happens when rising price action pressures short sellers to buy back shares, which can add more buying pressure. Traders watch short-squeeze conditions because they can create fast moves and high volatility.

A short squeeze is never guaranteed. High short interest, high borrow fees, or low float can add context, but price action, volume, catalysts, and risk management still matter.

## Simple Definition

A short squeeze is a move where short sellers are pressured to cover as price rises.

Short-squeeze context may include:

1. High short interest.
2. Low float.
3. Rising borrow fees.
4. Strong catalyst.
5. High relative volume.
6. Breakout through key levels.
7. Limited liquidity.
8. Fast price movement.

The setup depends on multiple factors working together.

## Why Short Squeezes Matter

Short squeezes matter because they can move quickly and create emotional trading conditions.

This is where traders can get into trouble. They may call every high-short-interest stock a squeeze even when price action is weak or volume is missing.

A useful review asks:

1. Was short interest actually high?
2. Was volume increasing?
3. Was there a real catalyst?
4. Were key levels breaking?
5. Were borrow fees elevated?
6. Did the trader chase an extended move?

Short-squeeze review should separate real pressure from hype.

## Example

A low-float stock with high short interest releases strong news. Volume increases and price breaks above major resistance. Short sellers may begin to cover, adding buying pressure.

That can create short-squeeze behavior, but the move can still reverse quickly if momentum fades.

## How To Review A Short Squeeze

A good review looks at context and execution.

Useful questions include:

1. What created the squeeze thesis?
2. Was short interest current?
3. Did days to cover matter?
4. Did the stock have a catalyst?
5. Did the trader have a risk plan?
6. Did the trader exit when momentum failed?

The goal is to understand whether the trade was based on structure or excitement.

## FAQ

### What is a short squeeze?

A short squeeze happens when rising price action pressures short sellers to cover, which can add buying pressure.

### Does high short interest guarantee a short squeeze?

No. High short interest does not guarantee a squeeze.

### What can contribute to a short squeeze?

Short interest, low float, borrow fees, catalysts, volume, and strong price action can all add context.

### Why are short squeezes risky?

They can move quickly, reverse sharply, and create emotional chase entries.

### How should traders review short-squeeze trades?

They can review short interest, volume, catalyst, key levels, borrow fee, entry timing, and exit discipline.
