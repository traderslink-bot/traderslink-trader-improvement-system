# Fill Price

Fill price is the actual price where an order executes. It may be the same as the price a trader expected, or it may be different because of spread, liquidity, volatility, slippage, or order type.

Fill price matters because trade review should be based on what actually happened, not only what the trader intended to happen.

## Simple Definition

Fill price is the executed price of an order.

For example:

1. A trader wants to buy near $2.00.
2. The order executes at $2.03.
3. The fill price is $2.03.

If an order fills in multiple pieces, there may be multiple fill prices and an average fill price.

## Why Fill Price Matters

Fill price matters because it affects risk, profit and loss, average entry, average exit, and trade management.

This is where traders can get into trouble. They may plan a trade around one price but receive a worse fill that changes the risk-reward immediately.

A useful review asks:

1. What price was expected?
2. What was the actual fill price?
3. Was there slippage?
4. Was the order type appropriate?
5. Was liquidity strong enough?
6. Did the fill price change the trade plan?

Fill price is a key part of execution analysis.

## Example

A trader places a market order during a fast move. The expected entry is $2.00, but the fill price is $2.08.

That fill may still be acceptable, but the trader should review whether the entry became too extended or whether risk needed to be adjusted.

## How To Review Fill Price

A good fill price review looks at execution quality and trade context.

Useful questions include:

1. Was the fill close to the intended price?
2. Did the order partially fill?
3. Did the trader chase after a missed fill?
4. Was the spread wide?
5. Did the fill create extra risk?
6. Did fill quality repeat as a problem across trades?

The goal is to understand whether execution helped or hurt the trade.

## FAQ

### What is fill price?

Fill price is the actual price where an order executes.

### Is fill price always the same as the order price?

No. The fill price can differ because of slippage, spread, liquidity, or order type.

### What is average fill price?

Average fill price is the weighted average price when an order fills in multiple pieces.

### Why should traders review fill price?

It helps traders understand execution quality, slippage, and whether the actual trade matched the plan.

### Can fill price affect risk?

Yes. A worse fill can increase risk or weaken risk-reward.
