# Warrant

A warrant gives the holder the right to buy shares at a specific exercise price before a certain expiration date. Traders review warrants because they can create future share issuance and dilution risk.

Warrants are common in small-cap offerings, private placements, and unit financings. The terms matter because some warrants can affect how traders view future supply.

## Simple Definition

A warrant is a security that can allow the holder to buy shares later.

Warrant terms may include:

1. Exercise price.
2. Expiration date.
3. Number of warrant shares.
4. Cash exercise or cashless exercise terms.
5. Reset provisions.
6. Anti-dilution provisions.
7. Lockup or exercisability timing.
8. Registration rights.

The impact depends on the specific filing terms.

## Why Warrants Matter

Warrants matter because they can become future shares.

This is where traders can get into trouble. They may review current shares outstanding but ignore warrants that could add supply later.

A useful review asks:

1. How many warrants exist?
2. What is the exercise price?
3. When can they be exercised?
4. Are the warrant shares registered?
5. Are there reset or anti-dilution terms?
6. Could exercise create new selling pressure?

Warrant review is part of dilution review.

## Example

A company completes an offering with common shares and warrants. The warrants allow holders to buy more shares later at a set exercise price.

If the stock trades above the exercise price, warrant holders may have more incentive to exercise, depending on the terms.

## How To Review Warrants

A good warrant review looks at terms, timing, and dilution impact.

Useful questions include:

1. Were warrants included in a recent offering?
2. What is the exercise price?
3. Are the warrants in the money?
4. When do they expire?
5. Are the underlying shares registered?
6. What is the fully diluted share count?

The goal is to understand how warrants may affect future supply.

## FAQ

### What is a warrant in stocks?

A warrant gives the holder the right to buy shares at a specific exercise price before expiration.

### Are warrants dilutive?

They can be dilutive if exercised because exercise can create new shares.

### What is an exercise price?

The exercise price is the price at which the warrant holder can buy shares.

### Why do traders review warrants?

They review warrants to understand future share supply, dilution risk, and potential selling pressure.

### Are warrants the same as options?

They are similar in concept, but warrants are usually issued by the company and can create new shares when exercised.
