# Borrow Fee

Borrow fee is the cost paid to borrow shares for short selling. Traders review borrow fees because rising borrow costs can show tighter share availability and can add pressure to short positions.

A high borrow fee does not guarantee a short squeeze. Borrow costs are only one part of the broader short-interest and market-structure picture.

## Simple Definition

Borrow fee is the cost of borrowing shares to short a stock.

Borrow fee may be affected by:

1. Share availability.
2. Demand to short the stock.
3. Float.
4. Liquidity.
5. Broker inventory.
6. Volatility.
7. Corporate events.
8. Market conditions.

Borrow fee can vary by broker and can change quickly.

## Why Borrow Fee Matters

Borrow fee matters because it can affect the cost of staying short.

This is where traders can get into trouble. They may see a high borrow fee and assume a squeeze is guaranteed, even when price action is weak or volume is low.

A useful review asks:

1. What is the borrow fee?
2. Is the stock hard to borrow?
3. Are fees rising or falling?
4. Is short interest high?
5. Is price action pressuring shorts?
6. Is there a catalyst?

Borrow fee should be reviewed with price action and liquidity.

## Example

A stock has a borrow fee that rises sharply while the stock is also moving higher on news. Traders may view that as part of a short-squeeze context, but the squeeze still depends on volume, price action, and covering pressure.

The fee alone is not enough.

## How To Review Borrow Fee

A good borrow fee review looks at availability, trend, and trade context.

Useful questions include:

1. Is the borrow fee unusually high?
2. Did the fee increase recently?
3. Is share availability low?
4. Does the stock have high short interest?
5. Is volume strong enough to matter?
6. Is the trader overreacting to borrow data?

The goal is to understand whether borrow cost adds meaningful context.

## FAQ

### What is borrow fee?

Borrow fee is the cost to borrow shares for short selling.

### Why does borrow fee increase?

Borrow fee can rise when shares are harder to borrow or demand to short the stock increases.

### Does high borrow fee guarantee a short squeeze?

No. High borrow fee does not guarantee a short squeeze.

### Can borrow fee vary by broker?

Yes. Borrow availability and fees can differ by broker and can change quickly.
