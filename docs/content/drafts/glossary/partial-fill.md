# Partial Fill

A partial fill happens when only part of an order executes. The rest of the order may remain open, fill later, be modified, or be cancelled depending on the order settings and market conditions.

Partial fills matter because they can change position size, average entry, average exit, risk, and trade management.

## Simple Definition

A partial fill means the order was only partly executed.

For example:

1. A trader places an order for 1,000 shares.
2. Only 400 shares fill.
3. The remaining 600 shares do not fill yet.
4. The order has a partial fill.

Partial fills can happen on entries and exits.

## Why Partial Fills Matter

Partial fills matter because the actual position may be different from the planned position.

This is where traders can get into trouble. They may think they entered or exited full size, but only part of the order filled.

A useful review asks:

1. How much of the order filled?
2. Why did the rest not fill?
3. Was liquidity too low?
4. Was the limit price too strict?
5. Did the partial fill change position risk?
6. Did the trader manage the remaining order properly?

Partial fills are part of execution quality.

## Example

A trader places a buy limit order for 1,000 shares at $2.00. Only 300 shares are available at that price, so only 300 shares fill.

The trader now has a smaller position than planned and needs to decide whether to wait, adjust, or cancel the rest.

## How To Review Partial Fills

A good partial fill review looks at order type, liquidity, and trade response.

Useful questions include:

1. Was the order size too large for liquidity?
2. Did the trader use a limit order?
3. Did the partial fill affect the trade plan?
4. Did the trader chase after the missed shares?
5. Did the partial exit leave too much risk open?
6. Did partial fills repeat across similar trades?

The goal is to understand whether partial fills helped, hurt, or changed the trade.

## FAQ

### What is a partial fill?

A partial fill happens when only part of an order executes.

### Why do partial fills happen?

They can happen because of limited liquidity, strict limit prices, order size, spread, or fast market movement.

### Is a partial fill bad?

Not always. It depends on whether the partial fill still fits the trade plan and risk management.

### Can partial fills happen with limit orders?

Yes. A limit order may partially fill if only some shares are available at the limit price.

### Can partial fills affect risk?

Yes. They can change position size, average entry, average exit, and how the remaining position should be managed.
