# Invalidation Level

An invalidation level is the price level, condition, or event that shows a trade idea is no longer valid. Traders use invalidation levels to define risk and avoid holding trades after the original thesis has failed.

An invalidation level is not a guarantee that price will move a certain way. It is a planning tool that helps a trader know when the reason for the trade no longer applies.

## Simple Definition

An invalidation level is the point where the trade thesis is considered wrong.

It may be based on:

1. A support level breaking.
2. A resistance reclaim failing.
3. Volume fading.
4. A news reaction failing.
5. A moving average losing significance.
6. A key candle level breaking.
7. A time-based condition.
8. A filing or catalyst changing the trade context.

The level should be defined before or during the plan, not invented after the trade is already going badly.

## Why Invalidation Levels Matter

Invalidation levels matter because they give traders a reason to exit beyond emotion.

This is where traders can get into trouble. They may enter with a thesis, but when the setup fails, they keep holding and create a new story to justify the trade.

A useful review asks:

1. What was the invalidation level?
2. Was it defined before entry?
3. Did the trader exit when invalidation happened?
4. Did the trader move the level emotionally?
5. Was the level realistic for volatility?
6. Did the trade remain valid after the level broke?

The invalidation level connects the trade idea to risk management.

## Example

A trader buys a breakout because the stock is holding above $2.00. The thesis is valid only while price holds above that level.

If price breaks below $2.00 and cannot reclaim it, the breakout thesis may be invalidated.

The trader can then review whether they exited according to the plan.

## How To Review Invalidation

A good invalidation review looks at both the level and the trader’s response.

Useful questions include:

1. Was the invalidation level clear?
2. Was the stop based on invalidation?
3. Did the trader respect the level?
4. Did the trader add after invalidation?
5. Did the trader change the thesis after the level failed?
6. Was the level too tight or too loose?

The goal is to make trade failure clear and reviewable.

## FAQ

### What is an invalidation level?

An invalidation level is the price or condition that shows a trade idea is no longer valid.

### Is invalidation the same as a stop loss?

They are related but not always identical. A stop may be placed near an invalidation level, but invalidation is the reason the trade thesis fails.

### Why should traders define invalidation?

It helps traders avoid holding after the original trade idea is no longer valid.

### Can invalidation be based on more than price?

Yes. It can be based on price, volume, time, news, filings, or other trade-specific conditions.

### What happens if a trader ignores invalidation?

Ignoring invalidation can lead to larger losses, emotional decisions, and poor trade management.
