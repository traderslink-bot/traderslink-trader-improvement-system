# Liquidity

Liquidity means how easily a stock can be bought or sold without causing a large price move. Traders review liquidity because it affects entries, exits, fills, slippage, spreads, and position sizing.

Liquidity does not guarantee a good trade. It only helps traders understand whether a stock has enough activity to enter and exit according to the plan.

## Simple Definition

Liquidity describes how easily traders can buy or sell a stock.

Liquidity can be affected by:

1. Trading volume.
2. Bid and ask size.
3. Spread.
4. Float.
5. Market maker activity.
6. Time of day.
7. News or catalyst activity.
8. Overall market conditions.

A liquid stock usually has tighter spreads and more active trading. An illiquid stock may have wider spreads and harder fills.

## Why Liquidity Matters

Liquidity matters because poor liquidity can make a trade harder to execute and manage.

This is where traders can get into trouble. They may enter a thin stock and then realize the spread is too wide or there are not enough buyers when they need to exit.

A useful review asks:

1. Was the stock liquid enough?
2. Was the spread manageable?
3. Did volume support the trade?
4. Was position size too large for liquidity?
5. Did slippage affect entry or exit?
6. Did liquidity change during the trade?

Liquidity is part of execution quality.

## Example

A stock trading millions of shares with a one-cent spread may be easier to enter and exit than a stock trading very little volume with a twenty-cent spread.

The second stock may still move, but the execution risk can be much higher.

## How To Review Liquidity

A good liquidity review looks at both the trade and the market conditions around the trade.

Useful questions include:

1. What was the volume at the time of entry?
2. What was the spread?
3. Was there enough bid and ask size?
4. Did the trader get filled near the expected price?
5. Did the trader have trouble exiting?
6. Did position size match liquidity?

The goal is to understand whether liquidity supported or hurt the trade plan.

## FAQ

### What is liquidity in trading?

Liquidity is how easily a stock can be bought or sold without a major price impact.

### Why does liquidity matter?

It affects fills, spreads, slippage, position sizing, and the ability to exit a trade.

### What is an illiquid stock?

An illiquid stock is a stock with limited trading activity, often wider spreads and harder fills.

### Is high volume the same as good liquidity?

High volume can help liquidity, but traders should also review spread, bid and ask size, and execution quality.

### Can low liquidity increase risk?

Yes. Low liquidity can increase slippage, widen spreads, and make exits harder.
