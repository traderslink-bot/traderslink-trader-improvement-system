# Position Sizing

Position sizing means deciding how large a trade should be. It connects account size, risk per trade, stop distance, liquidity, volatility, and confidence in the setup.

Position sizing does not guarantee a trade will work. It helps traders control how much they can lose if the trade fails.

## Simple Definition

Position sizing is the process of choosing trade size based on risk.

A trader may consider:

1. Account size.
2. Maximum risk per trade.
3. Entry price.
4. Stop price.
5. Share size.
6. Volatility.
7. Liquidity.
8. Daily loss limit.

The goal is to size the trade so one mistake does not do too much damage.

## Why Position Sizing Matters

Position sizing matters because even a good setup can become dangerous if the size is too large.

This is where traders can get into trouble. They may use the same share size on every stock without considering stop distance or volatility.

A useful review asks:

1. Was the position size planned?
2. Was risk defined before entry?
3. Was size adjusted for volatility?
4. Was the trade too large for the setup?
5. Did the trader increase size emotionally?
6. Did the loss stay within plan?

Position sizing is one of the most important parts of risk control.

## Example

A trader plans to risk $100 on a trade. They enter at $2.00 and set a stop at $1.90. The risk is $0.10 per share. That means a 1,000-share position would risk about $100 before fees and slippage.

If the stop distance is wider, the share size should usually be smaller to keep risk controlled.

## How To Review Position Sizing

A good post-trade review looks at whether the size matched the risk plan.

Useful questions include:

1. What was the planned risk?
2. What was the actual risk?
3. Did slippage affect the loss?
4. Was the position too large for liquidity?
5. Did the trader add with a plan?
6. Did the position size change after emotion or frustration?

The goal is to learn whether size helped the plan or made the trade harder to manage.

## FAQ

### What is position sizing?

Position sizing is choosing how large a trade should be based on risk, stop distance, account context, and trade plan.

### Why is position sizing important?

It helps traders control risk so one trade does not damage the account too much.

### Is position sizing the same as share size?

Not exactly. Share size is the number of shares. Position sizing is the process of choosing that number based on risk.

### How do traders calculate position size?

Many traders start with planned dollar risk and divide it by risk per share, while also considering liquidity, slippage, and volatility.

### Can position size be too large even if the setup is good?

Yes. A good setup can still be difficult to manage if the size is too large.
