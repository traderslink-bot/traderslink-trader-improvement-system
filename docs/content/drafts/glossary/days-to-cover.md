# Days To Cover

Days to cover estimates how many trading days it could take for short sellers to cover based on reported short interest and average trading volume. It is also called the short ratio.

Days to cover does not guarantee a short squeeze. It is a context metric that should be reviewed with price action, volume, borrow fee, catalysts, and liquidity.

## Simple Definition

Days to cover compares short interest with average daily volume.

A simple version is:

1. Reported short interest.
2. Divided by average daily volume.
3. Equals days to cover.

For example, if a stock has 5 million shares short and average daily volume of 1 million shares, days to cover is about 5.

## Why Days To Cover Matters

Days to cover matters because it can show how crowded short positioning may be relative to normal volume.

This is where traders can get into trouble. They may see a high days-to-cover number and assume a squeeze is guaranteed, even if the stock lacks volume or a catalyst.

A useful review asks:

1. What is reported short interest?
2. What average volume is being used?
3. Is current volume much higher than average?
4. Is there a catalyst?
5. Is price action pressuring shorts?
6. Are borrow fees high?

Days to cover should be reviewed as context, not as a standalone signal.

## Example

A stock has 2 million shares short and usually trades 500,000 shares per day. Days to cover is about 4.

If volume suddenly rises to 10 million shares after news, the historical days-to-cover number may not describe the current trading environment well.

## How To Review Days To Cover

A good review looks at data timing and current market conditions.

Useful questions include:

1. Is short interest data current?
2. What volume period was used?
3. Has current volume changed dramatically?
4. Is float low or high?
5. Are borrow conditions tight?
6. Is price breaking key levels?

The goal is to understand whether the metric adds useful squeeze context.

## FAQ

### What is days to cover?

Days to cover estimates how many trading days it could take for short sellers to cover based on short interest and average volume.

### Is days to cover the same as short ratio?

Yes. Days to cover is often called the short ratio.

### Does high days to cover guarantee a squeeze?

No. High days to cover does not guarantee a short squeeze.

### Why does average volume matter?

Average volume affects how quickly short interest could theoretically be covered.

### Can days to cover become less useful during high-volume news events?

Yes. If current volume is much higher than the average used in the calculation, the metric may be less representative of current conditions.
