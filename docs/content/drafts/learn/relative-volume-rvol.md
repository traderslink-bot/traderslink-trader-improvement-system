# Relative Volume RVOL: What It Means for Active Traders

Relative volume RVOL compares current trading volume to the stock's normal volume. Traders watch RVOL because it can show when a stock is getting unusual attention compared with its usual activity.

High RVOL can be useful, especially for day traders and small-cap traders, but it does not automatically mean a stock is a good trade. It only tells you that activity is unusually high.

## Quick Definition

Relative volume, often called RVOL, measures how current volume compares to average volume over a similar period.

For example, if a stock usually trades 500,000 shares by a certain time of day but today has already traded 5 million shares, its relative volume is much higher than normal.

Traders use RVOL to help spot:

- Unusual activity.
- News-driven moves.
- Momentum candidates.
- Liquidity changes.
- Volume spikes.
- Stocks attracting attention from scanners.

RVOL is a context tool, not a buy or sell signal.

## Why It Matters To Traders

Relative volume matters because raw volume can be misleading. One million shares may be huge for a thin small-cap stock but normal for a heavily traded large-cap stock.

RVOL helps answer a better question: is this stock trading more actively than it usually does?

That matters for traders because unusual volume often appears around news, filings, earnings, sector moves, or major technical levels. But high RVOL can also appear during panic selling, failed breakouts, or messy choppy moves.

## How It Works

RVOL is usually calculated by comparing current volume to average volume.

Different platforms may calculate it differently. Some compare current daily volume to average daily volume. Others compare volume at the current time of day to the typical volume by that same time.

Traders may review RVOL alongside:

- Price movement.
- News or catalyst quality.
- Float and liquidity.
- Spread.
- Support and resistance.
- Premarket high and low.
- Volume continuation or fade.

A stock with high RVOL but a wide spread and poor liquidity can still be difficult to trade.

## Example Scenario

A small-cap stock usually trades 300,000 shares per day. Today, after a press release, it trades 8 million shares before lunch.

A trader may see high RVOL and ask:

- What caused the unusual volume?
- Is the news meaningful or just promotional?
- Is price holding gains or fading?
- Is liquidity clean enough to enter and exit?
- Did I chase only because the RVOL number was high?

The RVOL number gets the stock on the radar. It does not complete the trade plan.

## Common Mistakes

Common RVOL mistakes include:

- Treating high RVOL as automatically bullish.
- Ignoring whether price is holding the move.
- Chasing scanner alerts without reading the catalyst.
- Ignoring spread and liquidity.
- Comparing raw volume without considering what is normal for the stock.
- Forgetting that RVOL can be high during selloffs too.
- Using different platforms without understanding how each calculates RVOL.

High RVOL tells you something is happening. It does not tell you whether the trade is clean.

## How To Review This In Your Trading Journal

RVOL review can help traders understand whether they trade unusual activity well or chase it poorly.

Ask:

- What was RVOL when I entered?
- Was unusual volume tied to news, a filing, a sector move, or a technical level?
- Did volume continue after my entry or fade quickly?
- Was the spread manageable despite high RVOL?
- Did I enter because the setup was good or because the scanner looked exciting?
- Did high RVOL make me size too aggressively?
- Did the trade behave differently from normal-volume trades?

A journal can help show whether high-RVOL trades are actually part of your best setups or just your most emotional trades.

## FAQ

### What does relative volume RVOL mean?

Relative volume RVOL compares current volume to the stock's normal or average volume.

### Is high RVOL good?

High RVOL means unusual activity, but it is not automatically good or bullish. It needs price, catalyst, liquidity, and risk context.

### Why do day traders watch RVOL?

Day traders watch RVOL because it can help identify stocks with unusual activity, stronger attention, and possible intraday opportunity.

### Can RVOL be high during a selloff?

Yes. RVOL can be high during buying, selling, panic, profit-taking, or failed moves.

### Is RVOL the same on every platform?

No. Platforms may calculate RVOL differently, especially around time-of-day comparisons and average-volume periods.

### Should traders buy only because RVOL is high?

No. High RVOL is context, not a buy signal. Traders still need a plan, risk control, and chart confirmation.
