# Volume By Price

Volume by price shows how much trading activity happened at different price levels. Instead of only asking how much volume traded during a time period, it asks where that volume traded on the price chart.

That difference matters. A stock may have high total volume, but traders still need to know whether the most active trading happened near support, near resistance, during a breakout, or inside a choppy range.

Volume by price can help traders see where buyers and sellers spent the most time doing business. It does not predict the future, but it can add useful context to levels, breakouts, rejections, and trade review.

## Quick Definition

Volume by price is a way to organize volume based on price levels instead of time.

A regular volume bar shows how many shares traded during a specific candle or time period.

Volume by price shows how much volume traded around each price area.

For example, if a stock traded heavy volume between $2.40 and $2.50, that area may stand out as a high-activity zone. Traders may later watch that zone to see whether price accepts it, rejects it, holds above it, or fails below it.

## Why It Matters To Traders

Volume by price matters because markets often react around areas where a lot of trading previously happened.

Heavy volume at a price area can suggest that many traders made decisions there. Some may be holding from that zone. Some may be trapped. Some may want to exit if price returns. Others may watch it as a support or resistance area.

This is not magic. It is just context.

Traders may use volume by price to think about:

- Where the stock spent the most active trading time.
- Where price may face resistance after a move back up.
- Where support may form after a breakout holds.
- Whether a breakout cleared a high-volume area or failed inside it.
- Whether a trade entry happened near a crowded price zone.

This is especially useful when reviewing small-cap moves that spike, chop, reject, reclaim, or fade around obvious levels.

## How It Works

Volume by price groups traded volume into price ranges.

A charting platform may show this as horizontal volume bars on the side of a chart. Longer bars usually mean more volume traded at that price area. Shorter bars usually mean less volume traded there.

Traders often watch two types of areas:

1. High-volume price areas where a lot of trading happened.
2. Low-volume areas where price moved through quickly.

A high-volume area can act like a decision zone. Price may chop there because many traders are active around that level.

A low-volume area may sometimes move faster because there was less previous trading activity there. But that does not mean price has to move quickly. It only gives context.

## Example Scenario

Imagine a stock runs from $1.20 to $2.00, then spends most of the afternoon trading between $1.65 and $1.75.

The next morning, price gaps up and pulls back toward $1.70.

A trader looking at volume by price might notice that $1.65 to $1.75 was the prior high-volume area. That zone may matter because many traders made decisions there the day before.

The trader may then review:

- Does price hold that zone or slice through it?
- Does volume increase when price returns to the area?
- Does the bid absorb selling, or does the spread widen?
- Does the stock reclaim the zone after dipping below it?

The volume by price area does not tell the trader what to do. It helps frame the level.

## Volume By Price And Support Resistance

Volume by price can support a support and resistance review because it shows whether a level had meaningful participation.

A resistance level with heavy previous volume may be more important than a random price line. A support level that formed during heavy volume may matter more than a level where price barely traded.

Still, volume by price should not replace price action.

A high-volume zone can fail. A low-volume area can become important later. A level can look obvious and still break down. This is not about predicting the move. It is about understanding the risk and context.

## Common Mistakes

One common mistake is treating the biggest volume by price area as a guaranteed support or resistance level.

Another mistake is ignoring the time frame. A high-volume price area on a daily chart may not matter the same way as a high-volume area from the current intraday session.

Traders also sometimes add too many levels. When every volume area becomes a level, the chart becomes noisy and harder to trade.

Another mistake is ignoring what happened after price reached the level. The reaction matters. Did price reject? Did it hold? Did it chop? Did volume expand or fade?

The level is only part of the story.

## How To Review This In Your Trading Journal

Volume by price can make trade review more specific.

Instead of only writing, "I bought near support," a trader can review whether the entry was near a meaningful high-volume price area and how price behaved there.

Useful review questions include:

- Was my entry near a high-volume price area?
- Was I buying into a crowded resistance zone?
- Did price accept or reject the level after I entered?
- Did I chase after price had already moved through a low-volume area?
- Did the stock fail to reclaim a major volume area before I entered?
- Did I exit before or after a known high-volume decision zone?

Over time, this can help traders understand whether they are entering clean areas, crowded areas, or levels where the chart often becomes messy.

## FAQ

### What does volume by price mean?

Volume by price shows how much trading volume happened at different price levels. It focuses on where volume traded, not just when it traded.

### Is volume by price the same as volume profile?

They are closely related. Many traders use volume profile tools to view volume by price areas on a chart.

### Why do traders watch high-volume price areas?

High-volume areas can show where a lot of trading decisions happened. Traders may watch those areas later for support, resistance, rejection, or reclaim behavior.

### Does volume by price predict support and resistance?

No. It can help identify areas worth watching, but it does not guarantee that price will hold, reject, or break out.

### What is a low-volume area?

A low-volume area is a price range where relatively little trading occurred. Price may sometimes move quickly through these areas, but context still matters.

### How can I review volume by price in my trading journal?

You can note whether your entry and exit happened near high-volume zones, low-volume areas, support, resistance, or previous rejection areas. Then compare those notes to execution quality and trade outcome.
