# Swing Trade Journal

A swing trade journal is a structured record of the trades a trader holds across multiple sessions. It helps the trader review the original idea, the risk plan, the key levels, the catalyst, and the decisions made while the trade was open.

Swing trades need more than a quick entry and exit note. A trade can change over several days. News can appear. Volume can fade. Support can hold or break. The trader may scale, adjust, panic, or get impatient.

A good swing trade journal helps separate the trade plan from the emotion of managing the position.

## Quick Definition

A swing trade journal is a trading journal focused on multi-session trades.

It may track:

- Trade thesis.
- Entry and exit prices.
- Support and resistance levels.
- Invalidation level.
- Position size.
- Overnight risk.
- Catalyst or news context.
- Volume behavior.
- Daily management notes.
- Mistakes and lessons.

The goal is not to write a perfect essay after every trade. The goal is to create useful review data.

## Why It Matters To Traders

A swing trade journal matters because swing trades can drift away from the original plan.

A trader may enter for a breakout and later hold through a failed level. They may enter for a catalyst and then ignore that the news reaction faded. They may size the trade like a day trade even though it is being held overnight.

This is where traders can get into trouble. Without a journal, it is easy to rewrite the story after the trade is over.

A swing trade journal can help traders review:

- Whether the setup was clear before entry.
- Whether risk was defined.
- Whether the trade was sized properly.
- Whether overnight risk was planned.
- Whether the trader followed the invalidation level.
- Whether management decisions improved or hurt the trade.

## How It Works

A swing trade journal should start before the trade is opened.

Before entry, the trader can record:

- Why the stock is being watched.
- What setup is being traded.
- What level matters most.
- Where the trade idea is wrong.
- What the planned hold time is.
- What risk exists overnight.

During the trade, the trader can add daily notes:

- Did the stock hold key levels?
- Did volume increase or fade?
- Did news appear?
- Did the original thesis change?
- Did the trader follow the plan?

After the trade, the trader can review the result, but also the process.

## Example Scenario

Imagine a trader enters a swing trade because a stock breaks above $10.00 resistance on strong volume.

The journal entry before the trade might say:

- Thesis: breakout above multi-week resistance.
- Key level: $10.00 must hold.
- Risk: failed breakout below $9.70.
- Catalyst: earnings reaction with high volume.
- Concern: stock is extended from prior support.

Two days later, the stock pulls back and closes at $9.85. The trader updates the journal:

- Breakout level is under pressure.
- Volume increased on the pullback.
- Need to watch reclaim or failed breakout.

This kind of tracking helps the trader make decisions based on structure, not only emotion.

## Common Mistakes

One common mistake is journaling only after the trade is closed. That can lead to hindsight bias.

Another mistake is only tracking profit and loss. P&L matters, but it does not explain whether the trader followed the plan.

Traders also make mistakes when they do not record why the trade was taken. Without the thesis, it is hard to know whether the trade changed.

Another mistake is ignoring management decisions. Swing trades often succeed or fail because of what happens after entry.

A final mistake is writing too much but reviewing too little. A journal should be useful, not just long.

## How To Review This In Your Trading Journal

A swing trade journal should help the trader answer direct review questions.

Useful questions include:

- What was the original thesis?
- What was the entry reason?
- What was the invalidation level?
- Did I size correctly for overnight risk?
- Did I check news, earnings, and filings?
- Did price hold the key level after entry?
- Did I change the plan for a valid reason?
- What mistake, if any, repeated from past trades?

Over time, these notes can help traders find patterns in their own behavior.

## FAQ

### What is a swing trade journal?

A swing trade journal is a structured record of multi-session trades, including thesis, risk, levels, catalysts, management decisions, and lessons.

### What should I track in a swing trade journal?

Track the setup, entry, exit, support and resistance, invalidation level, position size, overnight risk, news, volume, and trade management decisions.

### Should I journal before or after the trade?

Both. Journaling before entry captures the plan. Journaling after exit helps review whether the plan was followed.

### Why is P&L not enough for swing trade review?

P&L shows the result, but it does not explain whether the trade was well planned, correctly sized, or managed with discipline.

### How often should swing traders update their journal?

Many swing traders update notes after each session or whenever new information changes the trade context.

### Can a swing trade journal help identify mistakes?

Yes. A journal can help traders spot repeated issues like chasing, ignoring invalidation, oversizing, holding through news without a plan, or exiting too early.
