# Form 25 Delisting

Form 25 delisting is an SEC filing used to remove a security from listing and registration on a national securities exchange. Traders may see Form 25 when a company is delisting from an exchange, moving to another market, completing a corporate action, or ending exchange registration.

A Form 25 filing is not automatically a buy or sell signal. It does not guarantee a specific price move. Traders need to review why the filing was made, whether the delisting is voluntary or involuntary, what market the stock may trade on afterward, and whether there were warning signs before the filing.

## What This Filing Means

Form 25 is used for notification of removal from listing and registration. In simple terms, it is connected to a security being delisted from an exchange.

A Form 25 may relate to:

1. Exchange delisting.
2. Voluntary delisting.
3. Involuntary delisting.
4. Merger completion.
5. Going-private transaction.
6. Bankruptcy or restructuring context.
7. Failure to meet listing standards.
8. Transfer to another trading venue.

For small-cap traders, Form 25 can matter because exchange listing status can affect liquidity, access, spreads, and risk.

## Why Companies File It

Companies or exchanges may file Form 25 when a security is being removed from listing. The reason depends on the situation.

Common reasons include:

1. The company failed to meet exchange requirements.
2. The company voluntarily delisted.
3. A merger or acquisition closed.
4. The company went private.
5. The security was no longer eligible for listing.
6. A corporate restructuring changed the security.

The filing should be reviewed with prior exchange notices, company press releases, and related SEC filings.

## What Traders Usually Look For

Traders often review Form 25 for listing and liquidity risk.

Important areas include:

1. Which exchange is involved.
2. Whether delisting is voluntary or involuntary.
3. Effective date.
4. Prior delisting notices.
5. Whether the stock may move to OTC trading.
6. Reason for delisting.
7. Related merger or going-private context.
8. Liquidity changes.
9. Broker access after delisting.
10. Spread and volatility risk.

This is where traders can get into trouble. A delisting-related filing can dramatically change how a stock trades, especially if liquidity drops.

## What It Does Not Automatically Mean

Form 25 does not automatically mean the company is bankrupt. It also does not automatically mean the stock becomes worthless.

The reason matters. A Form 25 after a completed acquisition is different from a Form 25 tied to exchange noncompliance.

A stock may react strongly, but the filing itself needs context.

## Dilution Or Risk Considerations

Form 25 is not usually a dilution filing, but it can be important for risk review.

Traders may want to check:

1. Whether the company had prior noncompliance notices.
2. Whether a reverse split was attempted or proposed.
3. Whether liquidity may decline.
4. Whether the stock may trade OTC.
5. Whether corporate action terms affect holders.
6. Whether the delisting follows bankruptcy, merger, or going-private news.

Delisting can affect trade execution, spreads, and access more than share count itself.

## How To Review Trades Around This Filing

A trading journal can help connect Form 25 details to trade decisions.

Useful review notes include:

1. Filing date.
2. Exchange involved.
3. Delisting reason.
4. Voluntary or involuntary status.
5. Effective date.
6. Prior warnings or notices.
7. Liquidity before and after filing.
8. Stock reaction after the filing.
9. Whether you understood the listing risk before trading.

This kind of review is especially useful for small-cap and low-priced stocks where listing risk can be significant.

## FAQ

### What is Form 25?

Form 25 is an SEC filing used to notify the removal of a security from listing and registration on a national securities exchange.

### Does Form 25 mean a stock is delisted?

Form 25 is tied to delisting or removal from exchange registration, but traders should review the effective date and reason for the filing.

### Is Form 25 always bad?

No. The reason matters. A Form 25 after a merger closes is different from a Form 25 caused by exchange noncompliance.

### Can a stock still trade after Form 25?

Sometimes it may trade on another venue, such as OTC, depending on the situation. Liquidity and broker access can change.

### What should traders check in Form 25 filings?

Traders should check the exchange involved, effective date, reason for delisting, prior notices, related corporate actions, and liquidity impact.

### Does Form 25 guarantee a price move?

No. Form 25 does not guarantee a price move. The reaction depends on context, expectations, liquidity, and why the filing was made.
