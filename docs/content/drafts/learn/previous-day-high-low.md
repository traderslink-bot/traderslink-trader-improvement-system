# Previous Day High Low: Why Traders Watch These Levels

The previous day high low refers to the prior trading session's high and low. Traders often watch these two levels because they can act as important reference points during the next session.

A stock moving above the previous day high may attract breakout attention. A stock losing the previous day low may show weakness. But neither level guarantees anything by itself.

## Quick Definition

The previous day high is the highest price a stock reached during the prior regular trading session.

The previous day low is the lowest price a stock reached during the prior regular trading session.

Traders often shorten these terms to:

- PDH for previous day high.
- PDL for previous day low.

These levels are simple, visible, and widely watched, which is why they can matter.

## Why It Matters To Traders

Previous day levels help traders compare today's price action to yesterday's range.

For example:

- Trading above the previous day high may show strength if price can hold there.
- Rejecting near the previous day high may show that buyers are not strong enough yet.
- Holding above the previous day low may show stabilization.
- Losing the previous day low may show weakness or a shift in structure.

For day traders, these levels can be useful during premarket planning and market open trading. They give a clean starting map before the intraday chart develops.

## How It Works

Before the session, a trader may mark the previous day high and previous day low on the chart. Then they watch how price behaves around those levels.

The previous day high may become:

- A breakout level.
- A resistance area.
- A reclaim area.
- A failed breakout level.
- A retest area after a breakout.

The previous day low may become:

- A support area.
- A breakdown level.
- A reclaim level after weakness.
- A failed breakdown area.
- A risk line for traders watching downside structure.

The level matters most when the reaction is clear and supported by volume, liquidity, and broader context.

## Example Scenario

A stock closed yesterday after trading between $2.10 and $2.85. Today in premarket, it opens near $2.70 and pushes toward $2.85.

A trader might watch the previous day high at $2.85 and ask:

- Does price reject immediately at $2.85?
- Does volume increase into the level?
- Does price break above and hold?
- If it breaks and fails, does it trap breakout buyers?
- Where is support if the breakout does not hold?

The level gives the trader a decision area. It does not replace a plan.

## Common Mistakes

Common mistakes around previous day high low levels include:

- Assuming a break of the previous day high must continue.
- Assuming a break of the previous day low must keep fading.
- Ignoring premarket levels that form before the open.
- Buying into the previous day high after a stock is already extended.
- Holding a failed breakout with no invalidation plan.
- Forgetting that volatile stocks can whip around obvious levels.
- Treating the level as exact instead of using a small zone.

Obvious levels are useful, but obvious levels can also attract fakeouts.

## How To Review This In Your Trading Journal

Previous day levels are easy to review because they are objective. You can mark them before the session and compare your trades against them later.

Ask:

- Did I know the previous day high and low before trading?
- Did I enter near one of these levels or far away from structure?
- Did I chase a previous day high breakout after the move was extended?
- Did I respect a failed breakout or failed breakdown?
- Did price hold above the level or only spike through it?
- Did I ignore a cleaner level nearby, such as premarket high or support?
- Did the level actually matter during the session?

This helps turn level trading from a vague chart idea into a reviewable process.

## FAQ

### What does previous day high low mean?

Previous day high low refers to the highest and lowest prices from the prior regular trading session.

### Why do traders watch the previous day high?

Traders watch the previous day high because it can act as resistance, a breakout level, or a reclaim area depending on how price reacts.

### Why do traders watch the previous day low?

Traders watch the previous day low because it can act as support, a breakdown level, or a reclaim area after weakness.

### Does breaking the previous day high mean a stock is bullish?

Not automatically. A break above the previous day high can fail if price does not hold, volume fades, or sellers step in.

### Is previous day high low useful for day trading?

Yes, many day traders use these levels as part of premarket planning and intraday review, especially around the market open.

### Should previous day levels be exact prices?

They are objective prices, but traders often watch a small zone around them because price can react slightly above or below the exact level.
