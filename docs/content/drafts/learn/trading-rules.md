# Trading Rules

Trading rules are the specific guidelines a trader uses to decide when to enter, when to exit, how much to risk, and when to stop trading. They turn a trading plan from a loose idea into something that can actually be followed and reviewed.

A rule does not have to be complicated to be useful. In many cases, the best rule is simple, specific, and easy to check after the trade.

This is where traders can get into trouble. They may say they have rules, but the rules are too vague to follow when the market starts moving fast.

## Quick Definition

Trading rules are written instructions that define what a trader should or should not do.

They can cover:

- Entry criteria.
- Exit criteria.
- Position sizing.
- Stop or invalidation levels.
- Daily loss limits.
- Time-of-day restrictions.
- News and catalyst requirements.
- Re-entry rules.
- Averaging down rules.
- Rules for when to stop trading.

A trading rule should be clear enough that the trader can review whether it was followed.

## Why It Matters To Traders

Trading rules matter because real-time trading can be emotional.

Without rules, every decision can feel flexible. The trader may chase because the candle is moving. They may hold a loser because it might bounce. They may take one more trade after hitting a loss limit.

Rules create structure before emotions show up.

Good trading rules can help traders:

- Avoid random entries.
- Control position size.
- Respect risk.
- Avoid overtrading.
- Reduce revenge trading.
- Review mistakes more clearly.
- Separate strategy issues from discipline issues.

The rule is useful because it makes behavior measurable.

## How It Works

A strong trading rule is specific.

A weak rule might say:

```text
Only take good setups.
```

A stronger rule might say:

```text
Only enter if price is near a planned level, volume supports the move, and risk is defined before entry.
```

A weak rule might say:

```text
Do not overtrade.
```

A stronger rule might say:

```text
After two full-risk losses, stop trading or reduce size for the rest of the session.
```

The clearer the rule, the easier it is to review.

## Example Scenario

Imagine a trader creates a rule: no new trades after hitting the daily loss limit.

During a red session, the trader hits the limit but takes one more trade to try to recover. That trade loses more money.

The journal review is clear. The issue was not only the final trade. The issue was a broken rule.

That clarity matters because it gives the trader something specific to fix.

## Common Mistakes

One common mistake is making too many rules. If the rule set is too complicated, the trader may not follow it in real time.

Another mistake is making rules too vague. A vague rule is hard to review.

Traders also make mistakes when they change rules during the session because of emotion.

Another mistake is only tracking rules on losing trades. Winning trades can break rules too.

A final mistake is not updating rules after repeated evidence. If the same mistake keeps happening, the rule may need to be clearer or easier to enforce.

## How To Review This In Your Trading Journal

Trading rules should be part of the journal review.

Useful journal questions include:

- What rule applied to this trade?
- Did I follow the rule?
- If I broke it, why?
- Was the rule clear enough?
- Did the rule break cost money or increase risk?
- Did I break the rule on a winning trade too?
- Does this rule need to be changed or reinforced?
- Is this a repeated rule-break pattern?

A journal makes rules more useful because it shows whether the trader is actually following them.

## FAQ

### What are trading rules?

Trading rules are written guidelines that define how a trader enters, exits, sizes positions, manages risk, and reviews trades.

### Why do traders need rules?

Rules help traders avoid emotional decisions and make their process easier to follow and review.

### What are examples of trading rules?

Examples include defining risk before entry, stopping after a daily loss limit, avoiding trades outside planned time windows, and not averaging down unless planned.

### Should trading rules be simple?

Often, yes. Rules should be clear enough to follow during live trading and easy to review after the session.

### Can a winning trade still break a rule?

Yes. A trade can make money but still violate the plan. Reviewing only losses can hide discipline problems.

### How should I journal trading rules?

Track which rule applied, whether it was followed, why it was broken, and whether the rule needs to be clearer or better enforced.
