# Trade Risk Review

Trade risk review is the process of studying how risk was planned, taken, managed, and adjusted during a trade. It helps traders understand whether the loss or gain came from a clean process, a risk mistake, or an emotional decision.

A trade can make money and still have poor risk. A trade can lose money and still be managed correctly. That is why risk review needs to look deeper than profit and loss.

This is not about predicting the next trade. It is about understanding whether the trader respected the risk plan.

## Quick Definition

Trade risk review means reviewing the risk side of a completed trade.

It can include:

- Planned risk before entry.
- Actual loss or gain.
- Position size.
- Stop or invalidation level.
- Slippage and spread.
- Adds and reductions.
- Daily loss limit impact.
- Whether the trader followed the plan.
- Whether emotions changed the risk.

The goal is to make risk behavior visible.

## Why It Matters To Traders

Trade risk review matters because traders often focus too much on the result.

A green trade can hide bad risk habits. A red trade can hide good discipline. Without review, the trader may repeat poor behavior because the final P&L did not expose the issue clearly.

For example, a trader may average down, ignore a stop, and still get bailed out by a bounce. The trade made money, but the risk behavior may still be dangerous.

This is where traders can get into trouble. The market rewards a bad habit once, and the trader starts trusting the habit.

## How It Works

A useful trade risk review compares the planned risk with the actual risk.

Before the trade, the trader should know:

- Where the trade idea is wrong.
- How much is being risked.
- How position size was chosen.
- What happens if price reaches the invalidation area.

After the trade, the trader reviews what actually happened:

- Did the trader follow the stop?
- Did the trader add risk?
- Did the trader reduce risk?
- Did the trader hold after the setup failed?
- Did the trade exceed planned loss?
- Did slippage change the result?

This review turns risk from a vague feeling into something specific.

## Example Scenario

Imagine a trader enters a stock at $8.00 with a planned invalidation under $7.80. The planned loss is $200 based on position size.

The stock breaks $7.80. The trader does not exit. They average down at $7.65 and finally exit at $7.40. The final loss is $700.

A normal P&L review says the trade lost $700.

A trade risk review says more:

- The planned invalidation was ignored.
- Risk increased after failure.
- The original loss limit was exceeded.
- The real issue was trade management, not only the entry.

That is the kind of detail a trader can actually learn from.

## Common Mistakes

One common mistake is reviewing only losing trades. Winning trades can contain serious risk mistakes too.

Another mistake is ignoring unrealized risk. A trade may close green, but it may have been down far more than planned during the hold.

Traders also make mistakes when they do not record adds, reductions, or stop changes.

Another mistake is treating slippage as random noise instead of reviewing whether liquidity and spread were part of the risk.

A final mistake is not looking for repeated behavior. One risk mistake matters. A repeated risk mistake matters more.

## How To Review This In Your Trading Journal

A trade risk review should be a standard part of journaling.

Useful questions include:

- What was my planned risk before entry?
- What was my actual risk?
- Did I define invalidation before entry?
- Did I follow the stop or risk level?
- Did I add to the trade?
- Did I reduce risk when the setup changed?
- Did slippage or spread affect the outcome?
- Did the trade violate a daily loss or max loss rule?
- Was the risk mistake repeated from earlier trades?

The more specific the review, the easier it becomes to improve rules.

## FAQ

### What is trade risk review?

Trade risk review is the process of studying how risk was planned, managed, changed, and followed during a trade.

### Why is trade risk review important?

It helps traders see whether the trade followed the plan, whether losses were controlled, and whether risk mistakes are repeating.

### Should I review risk on winning trades?

Yes. Winning trades can still include poor risk behavior, such as averaging down, ignoring stops, or oversizing.

### What should I track in a risk review?

Track planned risk, actual risk, position size, stop behavior, adds, reductions, slippage, invalidation, and rule breaks.

### How is risk review different from P&L review?

P&L review shows the result. Risk review explains whether the trade was managed according to the plan.

### Can a trading journal help with risk review?

Yes. A journal can help traders compare planned risk against actual risk and identify repeated risk-management mistakes.
