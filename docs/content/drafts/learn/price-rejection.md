# Price Rejection: How Traders Read Failed Pushes at Key Levels

Price rejection happens when a stock pushes into a level, fails to hold there, and moves back away from it. Traders often watch rejection around support, resistance, high of day, premarket levels, and other key chart areas.

A rejection can be useful information, but it does not guarantee a full reversal. It simply shows that price failed to hold a certain area at that moment.

## Quick Definition

Price rejection is when price tests a level and gets pushed away from it.

A rejection may happen at:

- Resistance.
- Support.
- High of day.
- Low of day.
- Premarket high or low.
- Previous day high or low.
- A breakout level.
- A breakdown level.

A rejection near resistance may show sellers stepping in. A rejection below support that quickly reclaims may show sellers failing to control the move.

## Why It Matters To Traders

Price rejection matters because it can show whether a level is being accepted or rejected by the market.

For example, a stock may break above high of day, trade there briefly, then fall back under the level. Traders may view that as rejection at the breakout area.

This is where traders can get into trouble. They may see the breakout first and ignore the rejection that follows. The chart can look exciting for a few seconds, but the failed hold still matters.

## How It Works

Rejection is usually easier to understand when tied to a specific level.

Traders may ask:

- What level was price testing?
- Did price hold above or below the level?
- Was the move rejected quickly?
- Did volume expand on the rejection?
- Was the rejection candle large or small?
- Did price reclaim the level afterward?
- Did the rejection change the trade idea?

A single candle wick can be a clue, but it is not always enough. Traders often review what happened after the rejection to see whether price followed through or recovered.

## Example Scenario

A stock is trading under resistance at $5.00. It breaks to $5.08, then quickly drops back to $4.92 with a long upper wick.

A trader may review that as price rejection near $5.00.

Useful questions include:

- Was $5.00 a known resistance level before the trade?
- Did price break above it with enough volume?
- Did it hold above the level or fail immediately?
- Did I enter into the rejection?
- Did I respect the failed breakout?

The rejection itself does not tell the whole story, but it gives the trader a clear event to review.

## Common Mistakes

Common mistakes around price rejection include:

- Ignoring rejection after entering a breakout late.
- Treating every wick as a major rejection.
- Forgetting to check the level where rejection happened.
- Holding after a planned breakout level fails.
- Shorting every rejection without considering trend and support.
- Confusing normal pullback with meaningful rejection.
- Ignoring volume and liquidity around the move.

A rejection is more useful when it happens at a level that already mattered before the trade.

## How To Review This In Your Trading Journal

Price rejection review can help traders study entries, exits, and failed breakout behavior.

Ask:

- What level rejected price?
- Did I know that level before the trade?
- Did I enter before or after the rejection candle?
- Did the rejection invalidate my trade idea?
- Did I exit according to plan or freeze?
- Did price later reclaim the rejected level?
- Do I often buy into rejection near resistance?

A trading journal can help reveal whether rejection is a real part of your review process or something you only notice after the loss.

## FAQ

### What is price rejection in trading?

Price rejection is when price tests a level, fails to hold there, and moves back away from that area.

### Is price rejection bearish?

It depends on where it happens. Rejection at resistance can show weakness, while rejection below support followed by a reclaim can show sellers failed.

### Does price rejection guarantee a reversal?

No. Price rejection does not guarantee a reversal. Price can reject briefly and later reclaim the level.

### What does a long wick show?

A long wick can show that price moved into an area and failed to hold there, but traders should review the level, volume, and follow-through.

### Why do traders watch rejection at resistance?

Traders watch rejection at resistance because it may show sellers defending the level or buyers failing to hold the breakout.

### How should traders review rejection trades?

Review the level involved, whether rejection changed the trade idea, whether volume supported the move, and whether the trader respected the failed level.
