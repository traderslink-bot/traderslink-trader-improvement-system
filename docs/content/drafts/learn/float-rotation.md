# Float Rotation

Float rotation happens when a stock trades volume equal to or greater than its public float over a period of time. Traders often watch float rotation in low-float and small-cap stocks because it can show how aggressively shares are changing hands.

A simple example: if a stock has a float of 5 million shares and trades 5 million shares in a day, traders may say it rotated its float once. If it trades 10 million shares, they may say it rotated the float twice.

This does not mean the exact same shares traded once or twice. It is an estimate. Still, it can be useful when reviewing extreme volume, momentum, liquidity, and exhaustion risk.

## Quick Definition

Float rotation compares trading volume to a stock's public float.

The simple estimate is:

```text
Float rotation = total volume divided by public float
```

If a stock has a 2 million share float and trades 6 million shares, the estimated float rotation is 3x.

That means total reported trading volume was three times the size of the float during the measured period.

## Why It Matters To Traders

Float rotation matters because it can help traders understand how intense the trading activity is relative to the number of shares available to trade.

This is especially relevant in low-float stocks. When a stock has a small float and volume becomes unusually high, the price can become volatile very quickly. Momentum can build fast, but fades can also be sharp.

Traders often watch float rotation to think about:

- Whether a move has heavy participation.
- Whether the stock is attracting unusual attention.
- Whether a low-float move may be getting crowded.
- Whether volume is early, mature, or possibly exhausted.
- Whether liquidity is expanding or becoming unstable.

The chart can look exciting, but the volume context still matters.

## How It Works

Float rotation is usually calculated from volume and float data.

For example:

- Public float: 4 million shares.
- Intraday volume: 12 million shares.
- Estimated float rotation: 3x.

Traders may also measure float rotation by session:

- Premarket float rotation.
- First-hour float rotation.
- Full-day float rotation.
- Volume during a breakout window.
- Volume after a halt or major news release.

The time window changes the interpretation. A full-day float rotation may not mean the same thing as a float rotation that happens before the market opens or within the first 30 minutes of regular trading.

## Why It Is Only An Estimate

Float rotation is not a perfect measurement.

The public float may be outdated or inconsistent across sources. Some shares may be restricted, held by insiders, or not actively available in the market. Some volume can come from high-frequency trading, repeated buying and selling, short selling, covering, or the same participants trading multiple times.

That means float rotation does not prove that every available share changed hands. It simply compares reported volume to an estimated float.

This is where traders can get into trouble. A 5x float rotation sounds dramatic, but it should not be treated as a guaranteed bullish or bearish signal.

## Example Scenario

Imagine a low-float stock has a reported float of 3 million shares.

By 10:30 a.m., it has traded 9 million shares.

A trader might estimate that the stock has rotated its float three times.

That information can raise useful review questions:

- Is the stock still making clean higher lows, or is it starting to churn?
- Is volume supporting breakouts, or is price failing despite heavy volume?
- Are spreads staying manageable, or are fills getting worse?
- Is the move still early, or has the stock already had a major volume event?

Float rotation does not answer those questions by itself. It gives the trader a better starting point.

## Common Mistakes

One common mistake is assuming float rotation means a stock has to keep running. It does not.

Another mistake is assuming high float rotation means the move is over. That is not automatic either. Some low-float stocks can trade multiple rotations during intense momentum periods.

Traders also make mistakes when they rely on one float source without checking whether it may be stale. Float data can vary across platforms, and share structure can change after offerings, conversions, reverse splits, or other corporate actions.

Another mistake is ignoring price action. Heavy float rotation with strong structure may mean something different from heavy float rotation with repeated failed breakouts and lower highs.

Context matters.

## How To Review This In Your Trading Journal

Float rotation can be useful in a trading journal because it gives volume context to the trade.

After a trade, you can review:

- What was the estimated float rotation at entry?
- Was the float already rotated before the trade?
- Did volume continue expanding after entry?
- Did the stock hold key levels after heavy rotation?
- Did the trade happen during clean momentum or heavy churn?
- Did slippage increase as volume became crowded?
- Did you chase after most of the volume event had already happened?

For low-float traders, this can be especially helpful. Two trades may look similar on a chart, but one may happen early in the volume cycle while another happens after the stock has already traded many times its float.

## FAQ

### What is float rotation?

Float rotation compares a stock's trading volume to its public float. If volume equals the float, traders may say the stock rotated its float once.

### How do you calculate float rotation?

Divide total volume by the stock's public float. For example, 20 million shares of volume on a 5 million share float equals an estimated 4x float rotation.

### Does float rotation mean every share traded?

No. Float rotation is an estimate. It compares volume to float size, but it does not prove that every public share changed hands.

### Why do small-cap traders watch float rotation?

Small-cap traders watch it because low-float stocks can become very volatile when volume is high relative to the available float.

### Is high float rotation bullish?

Not always. High float rotation can appear during strong momentum, heavy churn, failed breakouts, or selling pressure. Price action and context matter.

### Can float data be wrong?

Float data can be outdated or different across sources. Traders should treat float rotation as an estimate and review it with share structure, filings, volume, and chart context.
