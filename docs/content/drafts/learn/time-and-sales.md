# Time and Sales: How Traders Read Actual Prints

Time and sales shows the actual trades that have printed in a stock. Traders often call it the tape. While Level 2 shows visible bids and asks, time and sales shows what actually traded.

That difference matters. Quotes can appear and disappear, but prints show completed transactions.

## Quick Definition

Time and sales is a real-time list of executed trades.

It usually shows:

- Time of the trade.
- Price of the trade.
- Number of shares traded.
- Whether trades are printing near the bid, ask, or between them.
- Trade sequence and pace.

Traders use it to understand whether actual buying or selling is happening, not just what the order book appears to show.

## Why It Matters To Traders

Time and sales matters because it can help confirm or challenge what a trader thinks they see.

For example, Level 2 may show a large ask at resistance. If time and sales shows repeated prints going through that ask, buyers may be absorbing supply. If price keeps tapping the ask but cannot trade through it, the level may be rejecting.

This is where traders can get into trouble. They rely only on flashing quotes without checking whether trades are actually printing.

## How It Works

Time and sales updates as trades execute.

Traders may watch:

- Print speed.
- Print size.
- Whether prints happen at or near the ask.
- Whether prints happen at or near the bid.
- Whether large prints move price.
- Whether buying pressure continues or fades.
- Whether selling pressure accelerates near support.

Time and sales is fast and can be overwhelming. It is best used with chart levels, spread, liquidity, and Level 2 context.

## Example Scenario

A stock is testing resistance at $3.00. Level 2 shows sellers at $3.00, but time and sales starts printing repeated trades at $3.00 and $3.01.

A trader may ask:

- Are buyers absorbing the ask?
- Is print speed increasing?
- Is price holding above $3.00 after trades go through?
- Is the move supported by volume?
- Did I enter because tape confirmed the level, or because I guessed?

The tape can help add context, but it still does not guarantee the breakout will hold.

## Common Mistakes

Common time and sales mistakes include:

- Reacting to every print without a plan.
- Ignoring the chart level where prints are happening.
- Confusing one large print with sustained buying or selling.
- Reading tape without considering spread and liquidity.
- Assuming green prints always mean price will continue up.
- Assuming red prints always mean price will continue down.
- Getting overwhelmed by speed and abandoning the trade plan.

Time and sales is useful when it supports better execution awareness, not when it creates panic.

## How To Review This In Your Trading Journal

Tape review can help traders understand whether their entries and exits matched actual order flow.

Ask:

- Were trades printing near the ask or bid when I entered?
- Did print speed support the move?
- Did large prints lead to continuation or rejection?
- Did I react to one print instead of waiting for context?
- Did tape slow down after my entry?
- Did selling pressure appear before I exited?
- Did I use tape as confirmation or as an emotional trigger?

Trader Intelligence is being built to help traders review executions and decision patterns. Time and sales context can help explain whether a trade had real participation when the execution happened.

## FAQ

### What is time and sales in trading?

Time and sales is a real-time list of executed trades, showing when trades happened, at what price, and with how many shares.

### Is time and sales the same as Level 2?

No. Level 2 shows visible bids and asks. Time and sales shows actual completed trades.

### What is tape reading?

Tape reading is watching time and sales to understand executed buying and selling activity.

### Does time and sales predict price direction?

No. It provides execution context, but it does not guarantee what price will do next.

### Why do traders watch prints near the ask?

Repeated prints near the ask can suggest buyers are actively paying up, but traders still need chart, volume, and liquidity context.

### How should traders review tape-reading mistakes?

Review whether the tape confirmed the trade idea, whether print speed changed, and whether the trader reacted emotionally to individual prints.
