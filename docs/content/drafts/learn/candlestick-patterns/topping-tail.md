# Topping Tail Candle

A topping tail candle is a candlestick with a long upper wick that shows price pushed higher during the candle but could not hold that higher area into the close. Traders often watch it as a possible sign of rejection near resistance, a previous high, or an extended move.

That does not mean the candle is automatically bearish. A topping tail candle is a piece of context. The location of the candle, the volume behind it, the broader trend, and what happens after it are what make the pattern useful or misleading.

## Quick Definition

A topping tail candle forms when price trades noticeably above the candle body and then pulls back before the candle closes.

The main feature is the upper wick. The longer the upper wick is compared with the candle body, the more visible the rejection becomes on the chart.

Traders usually pay attention to topping tails when they appear:

1. Near a resistance level.
2. After a fast move higher.
3. Around the high of day or previous day high.
4. After a failed breakout attempt.
5. On high volume compared with nearby candles.

The candle can show that buyers pushed price up, but sellers or profit takers stepped in before the close. Context matters because a single candle does not prove that a trend is finished.

## Why It Matters To Traders

A topping tail candle matters because it can show where a stock had trouble holding higher prices.

For active traders, that information can be useful during trade review. It can help answer questions like:

1. Did price reject a known resistance area?
2. Did the candle appear after an extended move?
3. Was volume increasing as price failed to hold the high?
4. Did the next candles confirm weakness, or did price reclaim the area?
5. Did I react to the candle alone instead of waiting for more structure?

This is where traders can get into trouble. A topping tail can look dramatic, especially on a volatile small-cap stock, but the next candle can still reclaim the wick area and continue higher. The pattern is not a guarantee. It is a clue that needs confirmation.

## How It Works

A topping tail candle usually starts with upward pressure. Price moves higher, sometimes through a visible level, but the move does not hold. By the close of the candle, price has moved back down and leaves a long upper wick behind.

That wick can reflect several possible situations:

1. Buyers chased into the high and lost control before the close.
2. Sellers used the move into resistance to exit or short.
3. Early breakout traders got trapped above the level.
4. Liquidity appeared above a key price area.
5. The move needed more volume to continue.

None of these explanations are certain from the candle alone. The chart only shows the result. The job of trade review is to compare the candle with surrounding evidence.

## Example Scenario

Imagine a stock is moving up in the morning and approaches a resistance level from premarket. It pushes slightly above that level, prints a new high, and then closes back below the breakout area with a long upper wick.

That candle may be a topping tail candle because price tested higher and failed to hold.

A trader reviewing the setup would not simply write, "topping tail means short." A better review would ask:

1. Was the candle at a meaningful resistance level?
2. Was the move already extended from the last consolidation?
3. Did volume expand on the rejection?
4. Did the next candles continue lower or reclaim the wick?
5. Was the trade plan based on confirmation or emotion?

That sounds simple, but it matters. The same candle can mean different things in different market conditions.

## Common Mistakes

One common mistake is treating every upper wick as a major reversal sign. A topping tail candle near an important level can matter, but a random upper wick in the middle of a range may not mean much.

Another mistake is ignoring the next few candles. A topping tail that immediately gets reclaimed can show that sellers did not maintain control. In that case, the candle may have been temporary profit taking instead of meaningful rejection.

A third mistake is using the pattern without considering risk. A candle can look clean, but if the stock is highly volatile, thinly traded, or moving on fresh news, price can move through wick areas quickly.

Traders also get in trouble when they use the pattern as a prediction instead of a review point. The cleaner question is not "what will this candle do?" The better question is "what did this candle show about pressure at this price area?"

## How To Review This In Your Trading Journal

A trading journal can help you study whether topping tail candles affected your decisions and whether you handled them consistently.

Useful review notes include:

1. The level where the topping tail formed.
2. The time of day.
3. The candle timeframe.
4. Relative volume or volume change.
5. Whether the wick area was reclaimed.
6. Whether the candle appeared after extension or inside a range.
7. How your entry, exit, or trade management changed after seeing it.

Over time, the goal is not to prove that topping tail candles always work. They do not. The goal is to see whether you are using them with structure or reacting to them emotionally.

## FAQ

### Is a topping tail candle bearish?

A topping tail candle can be a bearish warning in the right context, especially near resistance or after a fast move higher. It is not automatically bearish by itself.

### What does the upper wick mean?

The upper wick shows that price traded higher during the candle but closed below that high. Traders often interpret this as possible rejection, but the surrounding chart still matters.

### Does a topping tail candle guarantee a reversal?

No. A topping tail candle does not guarantee a reversal. Price can reclaim the wick area and continue higher, especially in strong momentum or high-volume conditions.

### What should traders look for after a topping tail candle?

Traders often watch whether price holds below the wick area, breaks lower, consolidates, or reclaims the rejected zone. The follow-up action is often more useful than the candle alone.

### Is volume important on a topping tail candle?

Volume can add context. Higher volume may show more activity around the rejection area, but volume does not turn the candle into a guaranteed signal.

### Can topping tail candles appear on any timeframe?

Yes. They can appear on intraday, daily, or weekly charts. The meaning changes based on the timeframe and the trading plan being reviewed.
