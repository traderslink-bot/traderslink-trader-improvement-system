# Outside Bar Trading

Outside bar trading is based on a candle that takes out both the high and low of the previous candle. Traders often watch outside bars because they can show expansion, volatility, and a possible shift in control.

An outside bar is not automatically bullish or bearish. The candle direction, close location, volume, trend, and key levels all matter.

This is where traders can get into trouble. A large outside bar can look powerful, but it can also be a sign of emotional volatility or a trap if there is no follow-through.

## What The Pattern Looks Like

An outside bar has a high above the previous candle's high and a low below the previous candle's low.

It expands beyond the prior candle's full range.

An outside bar can close green or red. A strong close near the high may show buyers took control by the end of the candle. A strong close near the low may show sellers took control.

The close location often matters more than the candle being outside by itself.

## Why Traders Watch It

Traders watch outside bars because they show range expansion.

After a quiet period, an outside bar may show that volatility is increasing. Near support or resistance, it may show a battle where one side eventually takes control.

Traders may review:

- Where the outside bar formed.
- Whether it closed near the high or low.
- Whether volume expanded.
- Whether the candle broke a key level.
- Whether the next candle confirmed direction.
- Whether the candle was too extended to chase.

The outside bar can give information, but it still needs context.

## What Can Go Wrong

The biggest risk is chasing a large outside bar after the move already happened.

A big candle can attract attention, but if the entry is late, the risk may be wide. The trader may enter near the candle high and then panic during a normal pullback.

Other problems include:

- Ignoring the candle close location.
- Treating every outside bar as a reversal.
- Entering without a clear risk level.
- Ignoring nearby resistance or support.
- Misreading volatility as clean direction.
- Trading a low-volume outside bar in a thin stock.

The candle can be useful, but it should not replace a trade plan.

## Volume And Context

Volume can help traders review whether the outside bar had meaningful participation.

An outside bar on high volume may show a real battle between buyers and sellers. But high volume can also mean churn or distribution if price does not follow through.

Context should include:

- Trend direction.
- Support and resistance.
- Candle close location.
- Volume.
- News or catalyst.
- Time of day.
- Spread and liquidity.

A bullish-looking outside bar into major resistance may not mean the same thing as an outside bar reclaiming a key level with strong volume.

## Small-Cap Considerations

Small-cap outside bars can be especially volatile.

A low-float stock can print an outside bar because of sudden volume, a halt, a spread spike, or a failed breakout. The candle may look meaningful, but execution can be difficult.

Small-cap traders should review:

- Whether the candle formed on real liquidity.
- Whether the spread distorted the candle.
- Whether the move was news-driven.
- Whether the stock is extended.
- Whether the next candle confirmed or rejected the outside bar.

Outside bars in small caps should be reviewed with extra attention to risk.

## How To Review Trades Involving This Pattern

Outside bar trade review should focus on candle location, close, and follow-through.

Useful journal questions include:

- Where did the outside bar form?
- Did it break a key level?
- Did it close near the high, low, or middle?
- Was volume meaningful?
- Did I enter late after the candle expanded?
- Where was my invalidation level?
- Did the next candle confirm the direction?
- Did I chase volatility instead of a setup?

A good review helps traders distinguish useful expansion from emotional chasing.

## FAQ

### What is outside bar trading?

Outside bar trading is based on a candle that breaks both the high and low of the previous candle.

### Is an outside bar bullish or bearish?

It depends on the close, trend, volume, and chart context. An outside bar is not automatically bullish or bearish.

### What does an outside bar show?

It shows range expansion and increased volatility compared with the previous candle.

### Can outside bars fail?

Yes. A large outside bar can fail if price does not follow through or quickly reverses.

### Why does close location matter?

The close location can show which side had control by the end of the candle, but it still needs confirmation.

### How should I journal outside bar trades?

Track the outside bar location, volume, close position, entry timing, invalidation level, and whether the next candle confirmed the idea.
