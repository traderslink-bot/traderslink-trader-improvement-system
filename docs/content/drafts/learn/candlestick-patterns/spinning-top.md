# Spinning Top Candle

A spinning top candle is a candlestick with a small real body and wicks on both sides. Traders often view it as a sign of indecision because price moved both up and down during the candle but closed near where it opened.

A spinning top candle does not predict the next move by itself. It only shows that neither buyers nor sellers clearly controlled the full candle.

This is where traders can get into trouble. They see an indecision candle and assume reversal, but the next candle and the surrounding context still matter.

## What The Pattern Looks Like

A spinning top candle usually has:

- A small body.
- An upper wick.
- A lower wick.
- A close near the open.

The candle can be green or red. The color is usually less important than the structure.

The small body shows that price opened and closed near the same area. The wicks show that price moved in both directions before settling.

Spinning tops often appear during pauses, pullbacks, failed moves, or uncertain areas on the chart.

## Why Traders Watch It

Traders watch spinning top candles because they may show hesitation.

After a strong move, a spinning top may show that momentum is slowing. Near support or resistance, it may show a fight between buyers and sellers. During consolidation, it may simply show normal chop.

Traders may review:

- Where the candle formed.
- Whether it appeared after a strong move.
- Whether it formed near support or resistance.
- Whether volume increased or faded.
- Whether the next candle confirmed direction.

The candle matters more when it forms at an important level.

## What Can Go Wrong

The biggest mistake is treating every spinning top as a reversal signal.

A spinning top can appear before a reversal, continuation, or more sideways action. Without context, it is only a small indecision candle.

Other problems include:

- Ignoring the trend.
- Ignoring support and resistance.
- Entering before confirmation.
- Overreacting to one candle.
- Ignoring volume.
- Treating candle color as the full story.

That sounds simple, but it matters. The candle is a clue, not a trade plan.

## Volume And Context

Volume can help traders understand whether the spinning top happened during active disagreement or quiet drift.

A spinning top on high volume near resistance may show heavy two-way trading. A spinning top on low volume during midday may mean very little.

Context should include:

- Trend direction.
- Key levels.
- Prior candle strength.
- Volume.
- News or catalyst.
- Time of day.

The next candle often matters because it shows whether buyers or sellers take control after the indecision.

## Small-Cap Considerations

Small-cap spinning top candles can be noisy.

Wide spreads, low liquidity, halts, and sudden volume changes can create candles that look meaningful but are really just unstable trading conditions.

Small-cap traders should review:

- Whether the candle formed on real volume.
- Whether the spread affected the candle shape.
- Whether the stock had news.
- Whether the candle formed near a key level.
- Whether the next candle confirmed anything.

A spinning top in a low-volume small cap should be treated carefully.

## How To Review Trades Involving This Pattern

A spinning top trade review should focus on context and confirmation.

Useful journal questions include:

- Where did the spinning top candle form?
- Was it near support or resistance?
- Did it appear after a strong move?
- Was volume meaningful?
- Did I wait for confirmation?
- Did I enter based on one candle only?
- Did the next candle support or reject the idea?
- Was my risk level clear?

The goal is to understand whether the candle improved the trade plan or distracted from the larger chart.

## FAQ

### What is a spinning top candle?

A spinning top candle is a candlestick with a small body and wicks on both sides, often showing indecision between buyers and sellers.

### Is a spinning top candle bullish or bearish?

It can appear in bullish or bearish contexts. The candle itself is neutral without confirmation and chart context.

### Does a spinning top mean reversal?

No. It may appear before a reversal, continuation, or consolidation. The next candles and key levels matter.

### Why do traders watch spinning tops?

Traders watch them because they can show hesitation, especially after a strong move or near support and resistance.

### Is volume important with spinning top candles?

Yes. Volume can help show whether the candle formed during active two-way trading or low-volume drift.

### How should I journal spinning top trades?

Track where the candle formed, volume, key levels, confirmation, entry timing, and whether the trade was based on context or one candle alone.
